Parliament Committee Seeks Reiterated Action on Facilities for Differently Abled Persons (Divyangjan) on Indian Railways

Report date: 2018-12-31

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

This Twenty Ninth Report by the Railway Convention Committee (2014) focuses on the action taken by the Government on the observations and recommendations made in the Committee's Seventeenth Report (Sixteenth Lok Sabha). The Seventeenth Report, which was presented to Lok Sabha and laid in Rajya Sabha on 20 December, 2017, concerned facilities provided by Indian Railways to Differently Abled Persons, also known as Divyangjan.

The Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) provided their replies regarding the action taken on these observations and recommendations on 31 July, 2018. This current report was considered and adopted by the Committee at its sitting held on 28 December, 2018, and was presented to Lok Sabha and laid in Rajya Sabha on 31 December, 2018.

What the report found

The report categorizes the Government's replies to the 19 observations/recommendations from the Seventeenth Report. Ten recommendations (52.6%) were accepted by the Government, while four (21.2%) were not desired to be pursued by the Committee. However, the Committee found that replies for three recommendations (15.7%) were not accepted and required reiteration, and replies for two recommendations (10.5%) were interim in nature.

Specifically, the Committee expressed dissatisfaction with the Railway Board's firm stance on not using the term ‘Differently Abled Persons (Divyangjan)’ instead of ‘Persons with Disabilities’ in all official documents, despite other Ministries/Departments, including the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment, using the former. The Committee also noted that the Railway Board has requested the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment to bear the subsidy cost for concessions given to Differently Abled Passengers, and a response is awaited. While the Committee acknowledged progress in providing lifts and escalators at stations, it observed that the Ministry had not responded to the concerns regarding the lack of ramp facilities on many Foot Over Bridges (FOBs) and the non-availability of sufficient expert contractors to build Divyangjan-friendly FOBs. The Committee appreciated the efforts by IRCTC to extend the e-wheelchair booking facility to all major stations and noted that dedicated helpline numbers for Divyangjan have started functioning at New Delhi, Mumbai (Central and Western Railways), Kolkata (Eastern Railway), and Chennai (Southern Railway) Railway Stations.

What was recommended

  • The Committee desires that Action Taken Notes for accepted observations/recommendations and final replies for observations/recommendations with interim responses be provided at the earliest.
  • The Committee is of the firm opinion that Indian Railways should use the term ‘Differently Abled Persons (Divyangjan)’ in place of ‘Persons with Disabilities’ in all their official documents, as other Departments/Organizations do.
  • The Committee desires that the matter of subsidy for concessions to Differently Abled Passengers be expeditiously and amicably settled between the Ministry of Railways and the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment to ensure concessions are provided to more categories of Differently Abled Passengers.
  • The Committee impresses upon the Railway Board to ensure the provision of ramp facilities in all existing and newly constructed Foot Over Bridges (FOBs), and to engage more expert contractors to build safe and Divyangjan-friendly FOBs.
  • The Committee desires that the provision for booking e-wheelchairs through IRCTC be extended to all major Railway Stations within a definite time frame.
  • The Committee desires that dedicated Helpline numbers for the benefit of Divyangjan, currently available in the four metros, be expanded to other Stations in a gradual manner.

What it means for citizens

For ordinary citizens, particularly Differently Abled Persons (Divyangjan), this report highlights ongoing efforts and challenges in improving railway facilities and services. The report indicates a push for Indian Railways to formally adopt the term 'Differently Abled Persons (Divyangjan)' in all its communications.

While some progress has been made, such as the provision of lifts and escalators at stations, the report calls for more action on essential infrastructure like ramps on Foot Over Bridges (FOBs) for easier movement with wheelchairs. Citizens can expect potential improvements in fare concessions if the cost-sharing issue between the Ministry of Railways and the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment is resolved. Furthermore, the report recommends the expansion of e-wheelchair booking facilities to more major stations and the availability of dedicated helpline numbers, already functioning in New Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai, to more railway stations across the country, which could provide crucial assistance during train journeys.
