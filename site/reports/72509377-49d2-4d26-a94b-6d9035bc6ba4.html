Interim Recommendations for Railway Dividend Rate and Financial Matters for 1992-93

Report date: 1992-02-24

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 10

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee (1991) was established on 25 November 1991, following a resolution adopted by Lok Sabha on 16 September 1991. The Committee was tasked with reviewing the rate of dividend payable by the Railway Undertaking to General Revenues and other related financial matters concerning Railway Finance.

Previous Railway Convention Committees (1985 and 1989) could not issue their final recommendations on dividend rates and related issues. This was because they ceased to function due to the dissolution of the Eighth Lok Sabha in November 1989 and the Ninth Lok Sabha in March 1991, respectively. Currently, the dividend is paid at 6% on capital invested in Railways (excluding Metropolitan Transport Projects) up to 31.3.80, and at 6.5% on capital invested thereafter.

The Ministry of Railways submitted an Interim Memorandum for the Committee's review, as their Eighth Five Year Plan had not been finalised, making it unfeasible to prepare a detailed memorandum. The Committee considered this Memorandum on 28 January 1992 and adopted its report on the same day. The discussion included proposals from the Ministry of Railways to continue existing dividend rates and concessions for 1992-93, and a recommendation from the Ministry of Finance for a provisional dividend of 7% on all capital. The report also covered appropriations to the Depreciation Reserve Fund and Pension Fund, and the interest rates on various Railway Funds.

What the report found

  • As of 28 January 1992, the Railways' Eighth Five Year Plan had not been finalised.
  • Budgetary support from General Revenues for Railways' Plan expenditure declined from 58% in the VI Plan to 42% in the VII Plan, and further to 32% in 1990-91 and 1991-92.
  • Funds raised through market borrowings by the Indian Railway Finance Corporation (IRFC) carried lease charges of approximately 14.5% per annum, which was higher than the 6.5% dividend rate on fresh capital provided by the General Exchequer. Lease charges paid to IRFC increased from Rs. 26 crores in 1987-88 to Rs. 626 crores in 1991-92 (Budget Estimates).
  • The Railways had an outstanding deferred dividend liability of Rs. 416 crores and an unpaid Development Fund loan of Rs. 476 crores. The Ministry of Finance's proposed 7% dividend rate would increase the Railways' dividend liability by about Rs. 101 crore, based on the 1991-92 budgeted Capital-at-charge.
  • Social burdens, such as carrying commodities like foodgrains, fodder, and salt at concessional rates and incurring losses on passenger transport, were estimated at Rs. 2222 crore for 1990-91, with no compensation provided to the Indian Railways. Market borrowings up to 1991-92 amounted to Rs. 5190 crores, creating an additional annual financial burden of Rs. 415 crore.
  • The rate of dividend was higher than the Government's average borrowing rates until 1969-70. From 1982-83 onwards, the average borrowing rate became much higher than the prevailing 6.5% dividend rate. The average borrowing rate for 1989-90 was 9.60%. Average borrowing rates for 1990-91 and 1991-92 were not available from the Ministry of Railways.
  • The balance in the Depreciation Reserve Fund was expected to be about Rs. 1,061 crores at the end of 1991-92. The Railway's Annual Plan for 1991-92 was fixed at Rs. 5,325 crores, and the contribution to this fund from revenues was Rs. 2,000 crores for that year.
  • The balance in the Pension Fund was Rs. 13 crores at the close of 1989-90, increased to Rs. 116 crores by the end of 1990-91, and was expected to be around Rs. 180 crores at the end of 1991-92.
  • Balances in various Railway Reserve Funds (other than Development Fund) carried the same interest rate as the dividend rate (currently 6.5%), while the interest rate on loans from General Revenues for developmental works was higher, exceeding 10%.

What was recommended

  • As an interim measure for the year 1992-93, the dividend rates to be paid by the Railways to the General Revenues should be:

- 6% on the capital invested up to 31.3.1980, including 1.5% on capital up to 31.3.1964 (less capital qualifying for subsidy) for payment to States as grants in lieu of passenger fare tax and for assisting States in financing Safety Works.

- 6.5% on the capital invested from 1.4.1980 to 31.3.1993.

  • All other existing concessions, including dividend on residential buildings, new lines, and subsidies from General Revenues, should provisionally continue on the existing basis for the 1992-93 Budget Estimates.
  • The contribution to the Depreciation Reserve Fund from Revenues should be increased to Rs. 2,300 crores in 1992-93, subject to adjustments based on the final size of the Annual Plan 1992-93 and the Railways' capacity to generate internal resources.
  • An Expert Committee must reassess and revalue the cost of assets held by the Railways, with results submitted to this Committee as early as possible.
  • The contribution to the Pension Fund, as part of working expenses, should be increased to Rs. 1,200 crores in 1992-93, subject to minor adjustment based on likely withdrawals and the Railways' financial position. Any excess contribution over withdrawals from the Pension Fund may be counted towards internal resource generation for the Railway Plan.
  • The balances in the various Railway Reserve Funds (other than Development Fund) should carry the same interest rate as the dividend rate. The interest rate on the balance in the Development Fund should be the same as the interest rate on loans from General Revenues for Development Fund Works, for the 1992-93 Budget Estimates.

What it means for citizens

The report's recommendations primarily concern the financial arrangements between the Indian Railways and the General Revenues for the financial year 1992-93. The continuation of existing concessions and subsidies (as recommended in paragraph 13) means that certain financial supports for railway services, such as grants to States in lieu of passenger fare tax and for assisting States in financing safety works, and subsidies for unremunerative branch lines and strategic lines, will remain in place.

The recommendations to increase contributions to the Depreciation Reserve Fund (paragraph 16) aim to ensure that the Railways have adequate funds for replacing and renewing old assets, which supports the maintenance of railway infrastructure. The call for an Expert Committee to revalue assets seeks more accurate financial planning for asset management. Similarly, the enhanced contributions to the Pension Fund (paragraph 19) are intended to help the Ministry of Railways meet its future pension liabilities to its employees.
