Committee Reviews OBC Representation and Welfare Measures in NHPC Ltd.

Report date: 2019-12-12

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

The Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes examined ‘Measures undertaken to secure representation of OBCs in employment and for their welfare in NHPC Ltd.’, which falls under the Ministry of Power. NHPC Limited, formerly known as National Hydroelectric Power Corporation Ltd., is a Mini Ratna Category-I Enterprise of the Government of India, incorporated in 1975. Its objective is the integrated and efficient development of hydroelectric power, later expanded to include conventional and non-conventional power sources in India and abroad.

In conducting its examination, the Committee obtained background notes and written replies from the Ministry of Power and NHPC Ltd. The Committee also took oral evidence from representatives of both the Ministry of Power and NHPC on 2019-09-09 and 2019-11-18. Additionally, the Committee undertook a study visit to Gangtok, Sikkim, on 2018-06-23 to gather feedback at the field level. The First Report on this subject was considered and adopted by the Committee at its sitting held on 2019-12-09.

What the report found

As of 2019-10-31, the Board of Directors of NHPC, comprising five whole-time Functional Directors, one Government Nominee Director, and five Independent Directors, does not have any member belonging to the Other Backward Classes (OBC) category. In the 2018-2019 period, NHPC provided 20,744 man-days of training to its employees.

The report notes that since the reservation orders for OBCs became effective on 1993-09-08, NHPC Limited recruited a total of 1184 OBC employees out of 4351 employees in Group A and B. As of 2019-08-01, out of a total of 6513 employees in NHPC, 912 were from the OBC category, representing 14% overall. Group-wise representation of OBCs as of 2019-08-01 was 21% in Group A (608 employees), 21% in Group B (150 employees), 6% in Group C (112 employees), and 5% in Group D (42 employees). Shortfall vacancies as of 2019-08-01 were 504 in Group A, 327 in Group B, 851 in Group C, and 266 in Group D. Reasons provided by the Ministry for non-recruitment in Group C and Group D include the acquisition of excess manpower through an R&R plan and from the Brahmaputra Board, reduced manpower requirements as most projects are commissioned, and high O&M expenses exceeding the CERC prescribed limit.

Between 1999 and 2014, a large number of backlog vacancies for OBCs accumulated in Group A and B, which reduced to 5 each in Group A and B by 2018. The report also notes that on 1997-07-02, when switching to a Post Based Roster, there was an overall manpower shortfall of 321 posts in Group A, 329 in Group B, and 2440 in Group C (including erstwhile Group D). Special Recruitment Drives in 2015 and 2016 filled 100 posts in Group A and 6 posts in Group B, but no posts in Group C and D were filled. No recruitment has been carried out by NHPC in 2017, 2018, and 2019.

NHPC has a monitoring mechanism that includes maintaining post-based, discipline-wise, grade-wise recruitment and promotion rosters, establishing an OBC Cell, and nominating Liaison Officers from the OBC category at corporate and regional/project levels. A multi-level Grievance Redressal Mechanism, called “NHPC Grievance Procedure,” has been in effect since 1979-03-01. The number of complaints received from OBC employees was 1 in 2016, 4 in 2017, 3 in 2018, and 1 in 2019, with most disposed of. Periodic meetings are held with the NHPC OBC Employees Welfare Association. An OBC representative is included in Selection Committees and Departmental Promotion Committees (DPCs). Advertisements for direct recruitment clearly indicate reserved posts for OBCs and are widely published.

Under Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR), NHPC undertakes activities for the general community in the vicinity of its projects, particularly in backward, hilly, and inaccessible areas where a majority of inhabitants belong to SCs, STs, and OBCs. The report states that NHPC has not created any scheme exclusively for OBC welfare, but 80% of CSR funds are spent in project regions, and the remaining 20% gives due consideration to backward classes. NHPC outsources non-core activities like security and maintenance. The report states that NHPC does not have a clause in its contracts regarding representation of OBCs in outsourcing. However, NHPC ensures that contractors adhere to government guidelines, including minimum wages, timely payment of wages, EPF, and ESI for medical benefits. Wage payments to contract workers are made through bank crediting, with certification by the Engineer In-charge (EIC) and their presence during disbursement if not paid directly to bank accounts.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry of Power should endeavor to appoint at least one Director belonging to the OBC category in the Board of NHPC, in compliance with DPE OM No. 2(18)/2011-GM dated 2011-04-18.
  • While selecting employees for sponsorship in training/skill development programmes, due consideration should be given to OBC employees, in accordance with DOP&T guidelines No. 3 (ii) & (iii) as enumerated in their OM No. 36038/1(i)/2013 - ESTT (Res) dated 2013-06-21.
  • NHPC should furnish details of training programmes conducted and the number of OBC candidates included in them.
  • NHPC should make concerted efforts to adhere to the stipulation of 27% reservation for OBC employees in all groups, particularly in Groups C and D, in all its forthcoming recruitment drives.
  • Whenever fresh recruitment is conducted, the shortfall of OBCs in Group C and D in NHPC should be identified and filled up to minimize the gap, and this should be monitored at the highest level in the Ministry of Power.
  • The practice of conducting separate job interviews for General and OBC categories needs to be re-examined in light of a recent Supreme Court judgment terming the process as "illegal." The Ministry should provide a detailed note on this matter.
  • The Ministry of Power and NHPC should follow the orders of the Hon'ble Courts in similar cases in letter and spirit.
  • Merit lists should be prepared by calling OBC candidates who secure marks equal to or more than the marks of the final cut-off list for the General category candidates for interviews, by preparing a combined list.
  • OBC candidates selected by fulfilling the criteria fixed for the general candidates should be adjusted against unreserved vacancies.
  • NHPC should hold regular consultations with OBC Employees' Welfare Associations located at all NHPC project areas, particularly in far-flung areas of the North Eastern region, and take appropriate measures for redressal of their grievances.
  • All Liaison Officers nominated by NHPC at corporate level and regional/project level should belong to the OBC category.
  • The OBC Cell of NHPC should be further strengthened by deputing adequate support staff, and similar OBC Cells should be set up at project/regional level.
  • Regular interactions/meetings between the management, Liaison Officers, Head of the OBC Cell, and the OBC Employees' Association should be further encouraged.
  • Copies of advertisements for direct recruitment should also be sent to Welfare Associations and Organizations of Other Backward Classes for better awareness of available opportunities.
  • NHPC should allocate a portion of CSR funds for the general welfare of socially and educationally backward classes in backward and hilly areas where its projects are located.
  • NHPC should strictly adhere to DOP&T instructions/guidelines for reservations for SC, ST, and OBCs in temporary appointments, industrial workers, work-charged posts, and appointments of casual workers to regular posts, as made applicable to Central Public Sector Enterprises through the Department of Public Enterprises. These include:

* O.M.No.8/7/1974-Estt.(SCT) dated 1975-08-22 regarding industrial workers.

* O.M. No.36036/3/2018 - ESTT. (Res.) dated 2018-05-15 regarding temporary appointments.

* O.M.No.36021/9/1976-Estt.(SCT) dated 1977-02-10 regarding work-charged posts.

* O.M.No.49014/12/1988-Estt(C)dated 1988-08-22 regarding regularization of casual workers.

  • Separate rosters should be prepared for regular and temporary appointments for OBCs in NHPC.
  • Ministries/Departments should insist on reservations (15% for SCs, 7.5% for STs, 27% for OBCs in direct recruitment on an all-India basis) as a pre-condition for sanctioning grants-in-aid to non-government organizations/voluntary agencies if they meet specific criteria (employ more than 20 persons regularly, at least 50% recurring expenditure from Central Government grants, registered society/cooperative, and receipt of annual general purpose grants of ₹2 lakh and above), as per O.M.No.27/12/73-Estt.SCT) dated 1974-10-07.
  • The provisions of the new Code on Wages for different categories of workers should be strictly adhered to by NHPC.
  • Measures such as wage payment to contract workers through bank crediting and appropriate certification by the representative of EIC (Engineer In-charge) should continue to be adhered to.

What it means for citizens

This report outlines measures and recommendations aimed at improving the representation and welfare of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in NHPC Ltd., a government enterprise. For OBC citizens seeking employment with NHPC, these recommendations could lead to increased opportunities, particularly in Group C and D posts, where current representation is low. The emphasis on filling backlog vacancies and the re-examination of interview practices may lead to a fairer selection process.

For OBC employees of NHPC, the strengthening of grievance redressal mechanisms, the establishment of OBC Cells at project/regional levels, and regular consultations with their welfare association could lead to more effective resolution of their problems and improved working conditions. Citizens residing in backward and hilly areas near NHPC projects may benefit from a dedicated portion of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) funds being allocated for their general welfare. Additionally, for individuals working as contract labourers for NHPC's outsourced functions, the report’s recommendations aim to ensure adherence to labor laws, including minimum wages and social security benefits, with transparency in wage payments.
