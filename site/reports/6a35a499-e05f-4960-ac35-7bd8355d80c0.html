Recommendations for MPLADS Guidelines on Animal Ambulances and Community Centres

Report date: 2015-05-07

Committee: Committee on MPLADS

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

The Committee on MPLADS examined proposals from the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation concerning modifications to the MPLADS (Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme) Guidelines. These proposals addressed two main topics: permitting the purchase of animal ambulances and regulating the construction of multiple community centres in the same village or local area.

Regarding animal ambulances, the Ministry forwarded a proposal received on 2014-07-05 from Smt. Maneka Sanjay Gandhi, Hon’ble Minister for Women and Child Development. This proposal requested allowing the purchase of animal ambulances under MPLADS for animal welfare organizations that run charitable shelters for homeless animals. The Ministry noted that ambulances for animals were permitted under the MPLADS Guidelines in 2002 but this provision was removed in the revised Guidelines issued in 2005 and 2012. While vehicles for transporting animals in Wildlife Sanctuaries and National Parks were later allowed by incorporating Para 3.25.1, there was no provision for trusts or societies managing charitable animal shelters to purchase ambulances for animals.

The second proposal, submitted by the Ministry on 2014-05-08, was based on a letter from the Principal Secretary (Planning), Government of Gujarat, dated 2014-01-24. This proposal suggested adding conditions to the MPLADS guidelines regarding the construction of community centres. It noted that Members of Parliament sometimes recommend constructing community halls in villages that already have one or more community halls built under MPLADS or other government schemes. The Committee considered these proposals at its sitting held on 2014-11-11 and adopted the draft report at a sitting held on 2015-04-28.

What the report found

The Committee noted that current MPLADS guidelines (Para 3.25) allow for the purchase of ambulances for human beings. However, there is no provision specifically for animal ambulances under the Scheme, though Para 3.25.1 permits the procurement of vehicles to transport animals in Wildlife Sanctuaries and National Parks. The Committee also observed that there is no existing provision to allow trusts or societies managing charitable animal shelters to purchase ambulances for animals, even though such organizations are permitted MPLADS funds for community infrastructure and public utility building works under certain conditions (Para 3.21).

Regarding community centres, the Committee found that the construction of community halls is a permissible work under the current MPLADS guidelines (Annexure IV-E, Sl. no. VII (1)). However, the guidelines do not specify a limit on the number of community halls that can be built in the same village or municipality.

What was recommended

  • Ambulances for animals should be permitted under the MPLADS (Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme).
  • To prevent possible misuse, ambulances for animals purchased using MPLADS funds should be kept under the control of government or government-aided veterinary centres.
  • These animal ambulances can be used by organizations engaged in animal welfare.
  • The Ministry should prepare detailed guidelines for animal ambulances and submit them for the Committee's approval.
  • Unnecessary expenditure on the construction of more than one community hall in a village or municipality should be avoided.
  • Community halls should be utilized by all communities without discrimination, and there should be no caste-based consideration in their use.
  • There is no need for two or more community halls in a compact village.
  • In cases of geographical necessity, such as hilly areas or remote places, where a Member of Parliament (MP) deems it necessary, the discretion to recommend more than one community hall in the same village or municipality can be left to the MP concerned.
  • The MPLADS guidelines should be amended accordingly regarding community centres, and the Committee should be informed of these changes.

What it means for citizens

Based on these recommendations, citizens might see changes in how Members of Parliament use their MPLADS funds. Animal welfare organizations could potentially gain access to ambulances for homeless animals, but these vehicles would be managed by government or government-aided veterinary centres. This means that while animal rescue and care efforts might receive support, the control over these assets would remain with government bodies.

For community development, the report suggests that new rules will aim to prevent multiple community halls from being built in the same village or municipality unless there's a clear geographical reason, like in remote or hilly areas. This could lead to more efficient use of funds for public facilities and aims to ensure that existing or new community centres are accessible and used by all communities without discrimination.
