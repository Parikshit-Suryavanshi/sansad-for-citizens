Committee Reports on Indian Railways' Internal Resource Generation and Performance

Report date: 2018-08-03

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

This Twenty Fifth Report by the Railway Convention Committee examined the subject of ‘Internal Resource Generation by Indian Railways’ pertaining to the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board). Internal resource generation by Indian Railways is defined as the excess of railway receipts over its expenditure on Ordinary Working Expenses, Miscellaneous Expenditure, Appropriation to Pension Fund, and Dividend Payment on Capital-at-charge. These generated resources are then appropriated to various Railway funds, including the Depreciation Reserve Fund (DRF), Capital Fund (CF), Development Fund (DF), Rashtriya Rail Sanraksha Kosh (RRSK), and Debt Service Fund (DSF). While DRF, CF, DF, and RRSK funds are utilized for annual Capital Expenditure, the DSF is allocated for future debt service obligations from loans (e.g., JICA, World Bank) and for arrears resulting from Central Pay Commission recommendations.

A significant change noted was the waiver of Indian Railway’s Dividend liability for 2016-17. Subsequently, with the merger of the Railway Budget into the Union Budget, effective from the Financial year 2017-18, the Indian Railway’s Capital-at-charge was removed, and its Dividend liability was discontinued by the Government of India. The committee selected this subject for in-depth examination due to the varying quantum of internal resource generation each year, influenced by factors such as traffic earnings growth, increases in Ordinary Working Expenses (OWE) and pension liability, and the implementation of periodic Pay Commission recommendations.

The committee obtained background material and written information from the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board). Oral evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) was taken on April 26, 2017, and April 20, 2018. The report was considered and adopted at the committee's sitting held on August 2, 2018.

What the report found

The report found that the Indian Railways experienced shortfalls in achieving its internal revenue generation targets in four out of five years between 2013-14 and 2017-18. Specifically, shortfalls were Rs. 2,828 crore in 2013-14, Rs. 796 crore in 2015-16, Rs. 2,782 crore in 2016-17, and Rs. 8,238 crore in 2017-18. The year 2014-15 saw an exception, exceeding the target by Rs. 386 crore. Reasons for these shortfalls included reduced earnings from passenger and goods services, higher Ordinary Working Expenses (OWE) and Miscellaneous Expenditure, increased appropriation to the Pension Fund, and delays in clearing traffic outstandings. For 2017-18, a major factor was lower than targeted revenue from land monetization within sundry earnings.

Passenger and freight earnings contribute about 92 percent of the Railways’ total receipts. Initiatives to boost passenger earnings in 2017-18 led to approximately five crore additional passengers compared to 2016-17, resulting in about Rs. 2,400 crore more in revenue. The flexi-fare scheme, introduced on September 9, 2016, in premium trains, saw earnings (inclusive of flexi fare) increase from Rs. 3916.45 crore in 2015-16 to Rs. 4763.70 crore in 2017-18. Measures against revenue leakage resulted in Rs. 956.46 crore detected and recovered as undercharges by Accounts and Audit from 2014-15 to 2016-17, and Rs. 3097.96 crore realized from ticketless travel/improper tickets/unbooked luggage from 2015-16 to 2017-18.

Freight earnings experienced a decrease in 2016-17 due to low incremental loading and a drop in average freight lead. However, concerted strategies led to the highest-ever coal, iron ore, cement, and container cargo loadings in 2017-18, yielding a freight revenue of Rs. 1,17,000 crore, Rs. 13,000 crore more than in 2016-17. The rationalization of coal tariff, effective from August 22, 2016, reduced rates for long leads (beyond 700 km) and provided graded concessions for short lead traffic. As of March 31, 2018, Rs. 604.66 crore was outstanding under Traffic Suspense from Power Houses/State Electricity Boards, with some amounts being sub-judice.

Regarding non-fare earnings, the Ministry established a Non-Fare Revenue Directorate and introduced new policies for train advertising, out-of-home advertising, content on demand, and a Railway Display Network. Scrap disposal generated Rs. 2801 crore in 2015-16, Rs. 2718 crore in 2016-17, and Rs. 3143 crore in 2017-18. Commercial publicity earnings were Rs. 263.48 crore in 2015-16, Rs. 219.68 crore in 2016-17, and Rs. 203.40 crore (provisional) in 2017-18. Ordinary Working Expenses (OWE) rose from Rs. 33,389 crore in 2004-05 to Rs. 1,38,000 crore in 2018-19 (Budget Estimates), primarily due to staff costs (55% of OWE), price escalation, and Pay Commission recommendations. Miscellaneous receipts dropped significantly from Rs. 4046.10 crore in 2015-16 to Rs. 150 crore in 2016-17 (Revised Estimates) and Rs. 500 crore in 2017-18 (Budget Estimates) due to the waiver of dividend payments and the merger of the Railway Budget, which discontinued government subsidies.

Key constraints to internal resource generation include huge Social Service Obligations (estimated at Rs. 30,000 crore in 2016-17 and Rs. 34,000 crore in 2017-18) and a substantial pension liability (Rs. 45,000 crore to Rs. 50,000 crore annually) borne solely by the Ministry of Railways, unlike other government ministries. Historically, the Railways have not received the required level of investment. The National Institute of Public Finance and Policy (NIPFP) submitted its final report in November 2017 on computing Social Service Obligations.

What was recommended

  • Intensify efforts to optimally meet annual internal resource generation targets, addressing shortfalls in passenger and goods services, and controlling Ordinary Working Expenses (OWE) and Miscellaneous Expenditure, as well as expediting the clearance of traffic outstandings.
  • Uninterruptedly persist with existing measures to leverage passenger earnings and explore other innovative ways to ensure more earnings from passenger services.
  • Undertake gradual/periodical fare rationalization, in addition to other requisite measures, to address the loss of revenue in passenger services and the operating ratio of 98.5 percent.
  • Make a segregated assessment of the actual revenue benefits accrued through the introduction of the flexi-fare system to enable the Railways to take corrective measures, if warranted, to retain the dedicated train passenger base.
  • Further strengthen the monitoring/inspection mechanism to curb revenue leakage/pilferage, including undercharges and ticketless travel, to the maximum extent.
  • Persist unabatedly with the directed and focused measures initiated to increase the Railways' share in seamless freight movement to mop up more revenue from goods services.
  • Continue paying focused attention towards the rationalization of coal tariff to retain and leverage the Railways' market share in transport, especially since coal constitutes about 50 percent of total freight loading.
  • Make concerted efforts to complete the ten Dedicated Freight Corridors (EDFCs and WDFCs) strictly as per their respective targeted timelines (between November 2018 and March 2020).
  • Step up measures to improve the utilization of parcel space in Brake Vans of mail/express trains and make both leased and non-leased parcel traffic more customer-friendly and market-oriented to ensure more revenue earnings.
  • Strengthen monitoring and coordination mechanisms to recover pending outstanding dues of Rs. 604.66 crore from State Electricity Boards within a definite time frame.
  • Expedite the implementation of innovative non-fare revenue sources (Train Advertising, Out of Home Advertising, Content on Demand, Railway Display Network, Integrated Mobile App-based Services, and integrating stations with rural areas for Wi-Fi and events) and explore newer streams for timely financial benefits.
  • Accord focused attention towards commercial development of vacant land, station development, development of Railway colonies, and Public-Private Partnership (PPP) projects for substantial revenue generation.
  • Strengthen monitoring mechanisms and field inspections for scrap disposal to promptly identify, mobilize, and dispose of scrap in a transparent manner.
  • The Railway Board should impress upon its Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) to leverage their performance to contribute appreciably to the Railway Funds, and the Ministry should continue providing all requisite support to PSUs for national/international projects and constraint mitigation.
  • Further intensify expenditure control measures to rationalize and contain Ordinary Working Expenses (OWE).
  • Look at other avenues, such as receipts from Railway Recruitment Boards and penalties from contractors, to increase miscellaneous receipts, now that subsidy payments from General Revenues have been discontinued.
  • Continue focused endeavors towards containing OWE, maintaining Capital Output Ratio, and enhancing Staff Productivity to keep the railway system healthy and competitive.
  • The Railway Board should take up the matter with the Ministry of Finance for getting higher budgetary support for capital investment, especially for modernization and expansion of the railway network.
  • The Ministry of Railways should approach NITI Aayog and the Ministry of Finance to take the matter forward for determination of a policy by the Government on the Social Service Obligation of the Railways, given the NIPFP report submission.
  • The Railway Board should take up the matter with the Ministry of Finance and other appropriate authorities so that at least a part of the pension liability of the Railways, if not full, is borne by the Finance Ministry, considering its huge impact on internal resource generation.

What it means for citizens

The report indicates that Indian citizens may experience gradual or periodical increases in passenger fares for train travel, as the committee noted significant losses in passenger services and recommended fare rationalization. However, ongoing efforts to enhance passenger services, such as increasing train frequencies, introducing new train types, and improving speed, could lead to more available and potentially faster train services. The completion of Dedicated Freight Corridors, targeted between November 2018 and March 2020, is expected to improve the supply chain for industries and logistics players.

Measures to curb revenue leakage, pilferage, and ticketless travel indicate a focus on ensuring fair collection of fares and charges from all users of railway services. Initiatives to generate non-fare revenue, including advertising, content on demand, and mobile app-based services, could lead to new services and facilities for passengers at stations and on trains. The concept of integrating railway stations with local communities for activities like marriage functions implies new potential community uses for railway property, particularly in rural areas. The focus on efficient resource management and containing Ordinary Working Expenses aims to maintain the financial health and competitiveness of the railway system, which could indirectly benefit citizens through sustained and potentially improved services. However, the report also highlights that the substantial social service obligations and pension liabilities borne by the Railways, impacting their capacity for internal resource generation, could affect the pace of future modernization and expansion.
