Summary of Report on Scrap Disposal System in Indian Railways

Report date: 2016-12-15

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee (2014) examined the Scrap Disposal System in Indian Railways. The Committee's Tenth Report, titled 'Scrap Disposal System in Indian Railways,' was presented to the Lok Sabha and laid in the Rajya Sabha on December 15, 2016. The Committee obtained background material and written information from the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board).

The Committee also took oral evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) on June 05, 2015, and October 24, 2016. The report details the mechanism for identification, segregation, and disposal of scrap, a review of the scrap disposal policy, e-auction and transparency in scrap disposal, and revenue generation from scrap sales. The Committee considered and adopted this report at its sitting held on December 14, 2016.

What the report found

The report found that Indian Railways is a significant generator of scrap, which includes categories like Cast Iron, Permanent Way Scrap (Rails and Associated Components), condemned rolling stock (wagons, coaches, locos), and non-ferrous scrap. Scrap disposal is a key function of the Stores Department, generating over Rs. 3000 crore annually. A Survey Committee, comprising officers from Stores, Technical, and Finance Departments, physically examines materials before they are classified as scrap for disposal.

The report noted sporadic instances of non-verification of stock due to an inadequate number of stock verifiers and the voluminous nature of materials. E-auction was introduced on a pilot basis on March 06, 2012, and Indian Railways fully switched over to e-auction as the normal mode of scrap disposal from June 01, 2013, with exceptions for certain low-value miscellaneous items. This system was observed to have eliminated direct human interface, reduced cartel formation, and increased transparency. However, the report also observed a declining trend in revenue generation from scrap disposal, from Rs. 3535 crores in 2012-13 to Rs. 2801 crore in 2015-16, attributed to decreased scrap quantum and prevailing market prices. Instances of irregularities in scrap disposal amounted to Rs. 3.37 crores in 2012, Rs. 90 lakhs in 2013, and Rs. 1.02 crores in 2014.

What was recommended

  • The Railway Board should ensure that Zonal/Divisional Railways strictly follow the schedule for stock verification for proper scrap disposal.
  • More efforts should be made to dispose of scrap as quickly as possible, given that delays can cause environmental issues, lead to corruption, and result in lost revenue.
  • A reasonable timeframe should be set for the Commissioner of Railway Safety to complete inquiries into accidental rolling stock, so that the Railways can condemn and dispose of these items sooner.
  • Efforts should be made to reuse more serviceable scrap, which can save money, building on the current practice where about 80,000 MT of scrap, valued at approximately Rs. 200 crore, has already been reused by railway manufacturing units.
  • The Railways should strengthen its monitoring to ensure all Zonal/Divisional Railways comply with guidelines from the Ministry of Environment and Forests and State Pollution Control Board norms for disposing of non-recyclable hazardous waste, to prevent environmental harm.
  • The Railway Board should investigate the matter and provide weighing facilities at as many scrap generation locations as possible, as selling weighable scrap without weighing it at the sender's location could lead to revenue loss.
  • The Railway Board should regularly monitor how Zonal Railways and Production Units are implementing the recommendations of the Committee of four officers, which was formed on March 11, 2015, submitted its report on May 07, 2015, and whose recommendations were accepted and instructions issued in January, 2016. The Committee also wants to be informed of the main points of these recommendations.
  • The Railway Board should protect against collusion, interference, and cyber crimes in the e-auction process to encourage more bidders and ensure the Railways get the best prices. The Railway Board should also look into a reported case where a bid from the Government of Karnataka was allegedly blocked to favor another bidder.
  • The Railway Board should have zero tolerance for irregularities in scrap disposal, especially after the introduction of e-auction, and take all necessary steps to prevent revenue leakage.
  • The Railways should continue to keep the reserve price for e-auctions confidential to prevent manipulation of the established procedure and ensure fair auctioning results in the best market price.
  • The precise contribution of e-auction to improved price realization should be determined to the extent possible, given that transparency and wider competition are expected to create ideal conditions for achieving the best prices, despite a noted decline in revenue generation since its introduction.

What it means for citizens

The efficient disposal of scrap by Indian Railways helps to keep railway workplaces clear and contributes to the Swachh Bharat (Clean India) initiative, which aims to improve cleanliness across the country. The revenue generated from scrap sales contributes to the Railways' finances, which can support its operations and development that serve citizens. The report emphasizes transparency in the e-auction system and internal controls to prevent irregularities, theft, and pilferage. This means public resources and revenue generated from scrap are managed with greater accountability and less wastage. Additionally, the proper disposal of non-recyclable and hazardous scrap materials, following environmental guidelines, helps protect the environment and public health.
