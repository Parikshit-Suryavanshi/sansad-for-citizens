Government Action on Railway Resource Mobilization through Public Borrowing

Report date: 1987-04-22

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 08

What was discussed

This report, the Eighth Report of the Railway Convention Committee (1985), discusses the actions taken by the Government on the recommendations from the Committee's Sixth Report (Eighth Lok Sabha). The Sixth Report focused on "Resource Mobilisation – Public Borrowing for Augmenting Railway Plan Finance."

The Sixth Report was presented to both Houses of Parliament on 2nd December 1986. The Committee received the Government's replies to all recommendations by 11th February 1987. The Committee considered these replies at sittings held on 24th March 1987, 2nd April 1987, and 15th April 1987, adopting the report on 2nd April 1987 and modifying it on 15th April 1987.

The Railway Budget for 1986-87, introduced on 26th February 1986, indicated that the Railways planned to raise up to Rs. 250 crores through market borrowings (taking loans from the public). Following this, the Ministry of Transport (Department of Railways-Railway Board) submitted a Memorandum on 2nd September 1986, proposing to establish the Indian Railway Finance Corporation Ltd. to initially raise Rs. 100 crores through bonds. Discussions with officials from the Railway Board and Ministry of Finance took place on 24th September 1986, and views from non-officials were heard on 6th November 1986. The Minister of Railways informed Parliament about the floating of the Indian Railway Finance Corporation Ltd. on 12th November 1986 in Rajya Sabha and on 13th November 1986 and 20th November 1986 in Lok Sabha. The Indian Railway Finance Corporation was incorporated on 12th December 1986.

What the report found

The Committee noted that the Indian Railway Finance Corporation was incorporated on 12th December 1986, before the Committee's doubts were clarified. The Committee had been apprehensive about the Railways' ability to pay back borrowed funds at a rate of 16% interest and had desired a single corporation for all Government borrowings. The Committee felt that it would have been appropriate for the Government to have taken prior or simultaneous concurrence of the Committee before making the announcement. The Committee also expressed its displeasure that, after bringing the matter before the Committee, the Government did not contemporaneously inform the Committee about the incorporation of the Indian Railway Finance Corporation Ltd.

The Committee did not desire to pursue any of the original recommendations from its Sixth Report, given the Government's replies. The Government stated that:

  • The additional annual burden on Railway finances from market borrowing of Rs. 250 crores would be about Rs. 10-11 crores, which is not large when compared to the Ordinary Working Expenses of Rs. 4643 crores in 1985-86.
  • Market borrowing would enable the Railways to secure a higher plan allocation for the year 1986-87 than would have otherwise been possible.
  • In 1985-86, the Railways earned a surplus of Rs. 178 crores and reduced deferred dividend liability from approximately Rs. 545 crores to Rs. 428 crores.
  • Raising funds through public borrowings exclusively for the Railways by the Ministry of Finance was not considered desirable by the Government due to limits on open market borrowings by the Central Government, which, if exceeded, could have the same economic impact as deficit financing (spending more money than the government has, often by printing more or borrowing from the central bank).
  • Public sector companies can offer higher interest rates and attract a wider class of investors.
  • The plan outlay for 1986-87 was fixed at Rs. 2650 crores, with the Indian Railway Finance Corporation's market borrowing of Rs. 250 crores accounting for about 9.4% of the plan outlay, or 19.5% of the budgetary support (Rs. 1280 crores), which is considered significant.
  • The additional revenues from the 1985-86 budget proposals were estimated at Rs. 495 crores, not Rs. 505 crores as previously stated. The proposal was to raise Rs. 250 crores through market borrowings for the full financial year, not Rs. 100 crores.
  • The recommendation for a bigger corporation under the Ministry of Finance was not favoured. The Indian Railways, with its unique organizational setup combining commercial functions with a Ministry, requires a separate finance corporation. The Government decided to raise funds through the Indian Railway Finance Corporation to give Railways control over the utilization of these funds.
  • The Rs. 250 crores raised in 1986-87 will be utilized by the Indian Railway Finance Corporation to finance the Railways' Rolling Stock Programme, specifically for purchasing wagons, once allotment formalities are completed.

What was recommended

  • The Committee expressed apprehension about the Railways' financial position if they borrow funds from the market at a cost of approximately 11% interest, compared to the 6% or 6.5% dividend rate they pay to General Revenues, especially since Railways already find it difficult to meet existing dividend liabilities.
  • The Committee apprehended that the additional burden from interest on bonds and administrative charges would further reduce the Railways' ability to pay normal dividends, and felt that the bond-floating scheme might increase the burden on the General Exchequer (the government's central fund).
  • The Committee were of the view that a part of the overall resources mobilized by the Ministry of Finance should be specifically allocated (earmarked) for the Railways.
  • The Committee suggested that market borrowings should be managed by a single Government agency, such as the Ministry of Finance or a single corporation.
  • The Committee believed that the problem of limiting government borrowing versus deficit financing (spending more money than the government has, often by printing more or borrowing from the central bank) was not insurmountable and ways to overcome it should be explored.
  • The Committee considered that efforts to mobilize an additional Rs. 100 crores for Plan expenditure would not require significant effort, given the additional revenues from fare and freight increases in 1985-86 (estimated Rs. 495 crores) and the November 1986 Supplementary Budget (estimated Rs. 320 crores).
  • If raising Rs. 100 crores through bonds is the first step towards generating larger resources for Railways, the Committee suggested establishing a bigger corporation under the Ministry of Finance to handle bond issues or market borrowings for all government corporations and companies, rather than creating a separate corporation for Railways.
  • The Committee also agreed to the Government issuing separate bonds specifically for Railways, or to a larger corporation under the Ministry of Finance raising funds for government companies and Railways, if repeated public borrowings are necessary for future railway development.
  • The Committee expressed displeasure that the Government did not keep them informed contemporaneously about the incorporation of the Indian Railway Finance Corporation Ltd.
  • The Committee felt it would have been appropriate for the Government to have taken prior or simultaneous concurrence of the Committee before announcing the floating of the Indian Railway Finance Corporation Ltd.

What it means for citizens

The report details how the Indian Railways planned to raise funds for its development, specifically through public borrowing (taking loans from the public by issuing bonds) via the newly formed Indian Railway Finance Corporation Ltd. The funds, amounting to Rs. 250 crores in 1986-87, are earmarked for the Railways' Rolling Stock Programme, such as purchasing wagons. This investment aims to augment railway plan finance, which could lead to improvements in railway infrastructure or services.

The report also highlights the financial implications for the Railways, noting the additional burden of paying higher interest rates on these market borrowings compared to traditional government loans. Citizens who invest in these bonds benefit from them being tax-free, making them attractive. The Government's decision to use a dedicated Railway finance corporation rather than a general government agency for borrowing is also noted, indicating a specific approach to managing railway finances.
