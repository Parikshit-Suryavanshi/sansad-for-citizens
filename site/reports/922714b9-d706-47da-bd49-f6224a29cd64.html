Railway Convention Committee Recommends Extending Dividend Concessions to Strategic Gauge Conversion Works

Report date: 2005-08-12

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 14

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee (2004) examined a Memorandum submitted by the Ministry of Railways. The Ministry of Railways requested that concessions currently available for constructing new railway lines on strategic consideration should also be extended to gauge conversion works taken up on strategic consideration. This request covered ongoing projects, future projects, and projects completed since 1992.

The Committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Railways on 27th January, 2005. After considering the information, the Committee adopted this Third Report at a sitting held on 3rd August, 2005.

What the report found

Historically, the Railways Separation Convention of 1924 established that Railways would not contribute or bear working losses for railway lines built for strategic reasons; these costs were borne by General Revenues. As per recommendations of the Railway Convention Committee (1960), Railways do not pay a dividend on capital advanced from General Revenues for new strategic lines, and operating losses are also covered by the General Exchequer. However, these concessions currently apply only to 'new lines' and not to 'gauge conversion' works undertaken for strategic reasons.

The Railway Convention Committee (1999) had previously recommended that the suggestion to extend these concessions to strategic gauge conversion works should be considered. From 1st April, 1992, Railways significantly increased gauge conversion efforts. The report states that 1467.6 kms of strategic gauge conversion works have been completed at an approximate cost of Rs. 825 crore. Additionally, approximately 2300 kms of strategic gauge conversion works are in progress, with an anticipated cost of Rs. 4431 crore, of which the Ministry of Railways is expected to fund Rs. 3858.85 crore. Another 390 kms of strategic gauge conversion works are proposed by the Ministry of Defence, costing Rs. 600 crore. The total capital involved for completed and ongoing strategic gauge conversion works is Rs. 4683.85 crore. If concessions are extended, the annual concession would be about Rs. 304.45 crore at the current dividend rate of 6.5%, potentially rising to Rs. 343 crore with future projects. The Ministry of Railways has spent more on completed strategic gauge conversion works (Rs. 825 crore) than the capital-at-charge provided by General Revenue for new strategic lines till March 2001 (Rs. 509.22 crore).

The Ministry of Defence, in letters dated 19th June, 2003, and 15th September, 2003, recommended extending concessions to strategic gauge conversion works, subject to the Ministry of Railways adhering to a time-bound programme, the projects being vetted by the Ministry of Defence, and the concessions applying to ongoing works, but not completed ones. The Ministry of Finance, in a letter dated 4th January, 2004, agreed to extend concessions with limitations: they would apply to specific projects vetted/certified as 'strategic' by the Ministry of Defence, be limited each year to the amount actually spent by Railways on such projects, only apply to ongoing and future projects, and be conditional on timely completion within approved cost estimates. The Ministry of Railways argued that concessions should also apply to projects completed since 1992, should not be linked to time-bound completion (as the Ministry of Defence does not provide separate funding), and that no further approval is needed as these projects are already based on Ministry of Defence recommendations. Six existing strategic lines currently receive dividend relief and operating loss coverage, with dividend relief being Rs. 36 crore annually from 1999-2000 to 2003-2004, and operating losses ranging from Rs. 226 crore to Rs. 307 crore during the same period.

What was recommended

  • Concessions in payment of dividend, as available for new lines constructed on strategic consideration, should also be extended to ongoing and future gauge conversion works taken up on strategic consideration.
  • Fresh vetting of the projects by the Ministry of Defence is not required because these projects are initiated based on the requests or recommendations of the Ministry of Defence only.
  • Due priority should be given for completion of the projects taken up on strategic consideration due to the paramount importance of national security.

What it means for citizens

If the recommendations of this report are accepted, the central government's General Revenues would bear the cost of dividend payments and operating losses for railway line conversion projects that are identified as strategically important for national defence. This would mean that the Railways would not have to use their own funds to pay dividends on the capital invested in these specific strategic gauge conversion projects. The report implies that these projects contribute to national security, suggesting an indirect benefit for citizens through improved infrastructure that supports the country's defence capabilities.
