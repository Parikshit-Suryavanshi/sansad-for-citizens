Railway Convention Committee Recommends Dividend Rate and Financial Arrangements for 1977-78 and 1978-79

Report date: 1977-11-17

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 06

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee (1977) examined the rate of dividend payable by the Railway Undertaking to General Revenues, along with other related financial matters, for the financial years 1977-78 and 1978-79. This report also took into account a previous report from the Railway Convention Committee (1973) concerning the dividend rate for 1977-78, which could not be presented to Parliament due to the dissolution of the Fifth Lok Sabha on 1977-01-18.

The Committee was constituted in pursuance of resolutions adopted by Lok Sabha on 1977-08-02 and concurred in by Rajya Sabha on 1977-08-04. Its mandate is to review the dividend rate and ancillary matters related to Railway Finance versus General Finance and to make recommendations. While the Committee decided on 1977-09-20 to examine other subjects such as Personnel Policy, Role of Railways in the Indian economy, Corruption and Malpractices, and Passenger Booking and Reservation, this specific report focuses on recommendations for the dividend payable by Railways for 1977-78 and 1978-79 and other ancillary matters. The purpose of this report is to assist the Ministry of Railways in preparing revised estimates for the current year (1977-78) and budget estimates for the next financial year (1978-79).

What the report found

The Committee observed a significant improvement in the financial performance of the Railways during 1976-77, achieving a net surplus of Rs. 87.33 crores, which was higher than the budgeted surplus of Rs. 8.98 crores. This contrasts with net deficits of Rs. 113.82 crores in 1974-75 and Rs. 61.11 crores in 1975-76. For 1977-78, the budget estimates presented in June 1977 projected a net surplus of Rs. 32.50 crores, an increase from the Rs. 26.45 crores projected in the Interim Budget presented in March 1977.

However, the Railways' indebtedness to General Revenues, under the Development Fund and Revenue Reserve Fund, increased from Rs. 208.01 crores at the end of 1973-74 to approximately Rs. 462 crores by the end of 1976-77. For 1977-78, despite the expected increased surplus, the report notes that the Railways' indebtedness to the General Revenues is expected to go down from Rs. 417 crores, as mentioned in the Interim Budget, to about Rs. 417 crores. The Committee highlighted that this continued indebtedness remains a major concern, requiring sustained efforts from the Railways to repay accumulated liabilities and reduce reliance on General Finances for budgetary support.

Regarding the Depreciation Reserve Fund, the Government decided to adopt "System II" from a Working Group's recommendations for assessing depreciation requirements. This system estimates the Fifth Plan's depreciation requirement at Rs. 658 crores, which closely aligns with the Rs. 650 crores already provided in the plan. Government also accepted in principle the recommendation to maintain at least 5% of the block value of wasting assets as a balance in the Depreciation Reserve Fund to meet unforeseen exigencies, noting that the actual balance will depend on the Railways' financial position.

What was recommended

  • The present method of paying a fixed dividend on the capital invested in the Railways, computed annually as interest charges plus a small contribution to General Revenues, should continue for financial discipline.
  • The current method of setting the dividend payment to General Revenues, specifically a fixed percentage of the Capital-at-charge (total capital invested in the Railways), excluding capital for strategic lines and providing special arrangements for certain ore lines, the Jammu-Kathua section, and the Tirunelveli-Kanyakumari-Trivandrum line, should continue during 1977-78 and 1978-79.
  • The current system of applying different dividend rates to capital invested in the Railways up to 1964-03-31 and thereafter should continue during 1977-78 and 1978-79.

* The existing dividend rate of 4.5% on capital invested up to 1964-03-31, with an additional 1% (in lieu of passenger fare tax and to assist State Governments with Railway Safety Works), should be retained.

* The dividend rate of 6% on capital invested after 1964-03-31 should also be retained.

* Out of the additional 1% dividend on capital invested up to 1964-03-31, Rs. 16.25 crores should be passed to the States as payment for passenger fare tax, with the remainder used to assist States in financing safety works.

* Losses from operating strategic lines should continue to be deducted from payments to General Revenues. Any earnings from such lines, after covering working expenses, depreciation, and other charges, should be paid to General Revenues up to the level of normal dividend.

* The Capital-at-charge of the non-strategic parts of the Northeast Frontier Railway, unremunerative branch lines, and over-capitalisation should continue to be exempt from dividend payments during 1977-78 and 1978-79.

* Railways should continue to receive credit for the difference between the 6% dividend rate and the average borrowing rate at which interest would accrue on their various Fund balances held with General Revenues during 1977-78 and 1978-79.

* The arrangement of deferring dividend payment on the Capital-at-charge of New Lines (chargeable at the average interest rate during construction and for the first five years after opening) should continue.

* The arrangement of closing the account of deferred dividend on New Lines after 20 years from their opening, thereby extinguishing any unliquidated liability for deferred dividend within that period, should continue during 1977-78 and 1978-79.

  • Reiterating previous recommendations, 50% of the capital outlay on works-in-progress (excluding specific categories like strategic lines, Northeast Frontier Railway (Commercial), over-capitalisation, ore lines, Jammu-Kathua and Tirunelveli-Kanyakumari-Trivandrum lines, New Lines, and P&T line wires) should remain exempted from dividend payment for three years in each case during the entire Fifth Plan period (1974-79).
  • Exemptions from dividend liability for capital works-in-progress should not be a reason to delay project completion, and all ongoing capital works and schemes should be completed quickly according to schedules.
  • The contribution to the Depreciation Reserve Fund should be raised to Rs. 140 crores during 1977-78 and Rs. 145 crores during 1978-79 to fulfill a covenant with the World Bank (aiming for an average of Rs. 130 crores per annum during the Fifth Plan) and align with Working Group assessments.
  • Temporary borrowing from Central Revenues should continue if the Development Fund's balance is insufficient to meet its obligations and pay interest on loans in 1977-78 and 1978-79.
  • Interest on Development Fund loans, whether for financing development works or paying interest on those loans, should continue to be levied at the average borrowing rate applicable to Commercial Departments.
  • Railways should be permitted to take temporary loans from General Revenues to meet the full dividend liability if net revenue or the Revenue Reserve Fund balance is insufficient during 1977-78 and 1978-79.
  • Interest on such loans to the Revenue Reserve Fund should be paid by the Railways at the current borrowing rate during 1977-78 and 1978-79.
  • The Committee noted Parliament's approval for the period 1974-79 for charging the cost of staff quarters construction to 'Capital', with dividend on such capital payable only if the Railways have a surplus after meeting other dividend obligations.

What it means for citizens

This report outlines the financial arrangements and recommendations for how the Indian Railways will manage its finances, including how it pays dividends to the general government treasury (General Revenues) and funds its operations and investments. The report details the Railways' financial performance, noting periods of surplus and deficit, and its ongoing debt.

The recommendations aim to ensure financial discipline within the Railways and help it manage its debt. Specific provisions are made to exempt certain types of railway lines, such as strategic lines, unremunerative branch lines, and new lines, from full dividend payments. This suggests that these parts of the railway network receive special financial support, which could help in their continued operation and development. The report also emphasizes the need for adequate funding for depreciation of assets and the timely completion of capital works. These measures are intended to maintain and improve the physical infrastructure of the railway system. Additionally, the report mentions arrangements for financing staff quarters, which supports the living conditions of railway employees.
