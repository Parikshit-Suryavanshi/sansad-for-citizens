Railway Convention Committee Report on Dividend Rate for Railways for 2014-15 and 2015-16

Report date: 2015-12-22

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee (2014-19), constituted on 2015-04-27, examined the rate of dividend payable by the Railways to the General Revenues for the financial years 2014-15 and 2015-16, and other related financial matters. The Committee received an Interim Memorandum on these topics from the Ministry of Railways on 2015-06-11, which included views from both the Ministry of Railways and the Ministry of Finance.

The Committee heard evidence from the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) on 2015-07-15. Further evidence was taken from both the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) and the Ministry of Finance (Department of Expenditure) on 2015-08-20. The discussions covered the historical separation of Railway Finance from General Finance in 1924, the principles guiding the dividend rate, the financial structure and current status of Indian Railways, its capacity augmentation needs, and existing liabilities.

The Ministry of Railways proposed an exemption from dividend payment for 2015-16 to 2019-20, and a reduced rate of 4% for 2014-15. Conversely, the Ministry of Finance proposed increasing the dividend rate to 7% for the 2014-15 fiscal year. Both ministries presented justifications for their respective positions regarding the dividend rate and the overall financial arrangements between Railways and the General Revenues.

What the report found

The Capital-at-charge (capital contributed to Indian Railways by General Revenues) increased from Rs. 827 Cr. in 1950-51 to an estimated Rs. 1,98,009.84 Cr. in 2014-15 and Rs. 2,35,328.79 Cr. in 2015-16. The annual dividend paid to General Revenues also grew significantly, from less than Rs. 100 Cr. until 1964-03-31, to Rs. 8,008.67 Cr. for 2013-14, with estimates of Rs. 9,174 Cr. for 2014-15 and Rs. 10,811 Cr. for 2015-16.

The Railways reported a decline in financial health after 2007-08, particularly due to increased staff costs and pension obligations following the 6th Central Pay Commission, leading to an operating ratio (income minus expenditure generated by Railways) of 95.3% in 2009-10. While freight earnings surpassed targets in 2013-14, passenger earnings fell short by Rs. 968 Cr. In 2014-15, both freight and passenger earnings were below revised targets by approximately Rs. 1,181 Cr. and Rs. 815 Cr. respectively, although an operating ratio of 91.3% was achieved due to savings in Ordinary Working Expenses.

The Ministry of Railways outlined a Five Year Investment Plan (2015-2019) aiming for Rs. 8.56 lakh Cr. in investment. It highlighted significant current liabilities, including financial assistance to Konkan Rail Corporation Ltd., a share in Kolkata Metro Rail Corporation (KMRCL) project costs, and increasing market borrowings for the Indian Railway Finance Corporation (IRFC). The Railways also anticipated a burden of around Rs. 36,000 Cr. from the 7th Pay Commission, effective from 2016-01-01, impacting its ability to generate resources. The total social obligations of Indian Railways, including passenger and coaching fares below cost, operating uneconomic lines, and concessions, amounted to over Rs. 30,000 Cr.

The Ministry of Finance argued that a 7% dividend rate is still concessional compared to the Government's borrowing cost of 8-8.5%, with the effective dividend rate (after reliefs) being 2-3%. It stated that the total support from the Budget to Railways in 2015-16 would be around Rs. 51,000 Cr. (Rs. 40,000 Cr. in Gross Budgetary Support, which is funding from the central government's budget, plus Rs. 11,000 Cr. in revenue foregone). The Finance Ministry also noted that budget support to Railways has increased, with Rs. 27,000 Cr. provided in 2013-14 and Rs. 40,000 Cr. in 2015-16, in addition to approximately Rs. 600 Cr. annually for reimbursement of losses on strategic lines.

What was recommended

  • For the year 2014-15, dividend to General Revenue may be paid at the rate of 5% on the entire capital (excluding dividend-free capital) invested on Railways from the General Revenues, irrespective of the year of investment.
  • For the year 2015-16, dividend to General Revenue may be paid at the rate of 4% on the entire capital (excluding dividend-free capital) invested on Railways from the General Revenues, irrespective of the year of investment.
  • The dividend for 2014-15 and 2015-16 should include the amount payable by Railways to General Revenues for grants to States in lieu of passenger fare tax and contributions for assisting States in financing safety works.
  • All other concessions currently available should continue on the existing basis for the years 2014-15 and 2015-16.

What it means for citizens

This report concerns how much money the Indian Railways pays back to the central government's general funds (called General Revenues) as a 'dividend', which is a return on the capital (funds) invested by the government into the Railways. The Committee recommended specific interim dividend rates for the Railways: 5% for the year 2014-15 and 4% for 2015-16. These rates apply to the capital (funds) invested by the central government into the Railways, excluding any capital already exempted from dividend payments. This dividend amount also includes funds that the Railways pays to the central government for onward payment to states for passenger fare tax and for railway safety projects.

A key point raised in the report is that any relief or reduction in this dividend amount is intended to be reinvested into the railway system. The Railways has significant plans for investment, aiming for Rs. 8.56 lakh Cr. between 2015 and 2019, to increase daily passenger capacity from 21 million to 30 million, extend track length by 20%, and boost freight carrying capacity. The report also highlights that the Railways incurs over Rs. 30,000 Cr. in "social obligations," such as providing passenger and certain goods services at below cost, operating non-profitable lines, and offering concessions. These financial considerations directly impact the Railways' ability to invest in infrastructure and services that ultimately benefit ordinary citizens.
