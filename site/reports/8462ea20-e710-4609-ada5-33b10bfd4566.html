Committee on OBC Welfare Reviews Government Action on Representation and Welfare in NFL

Report date: 2021-08-04

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

This Eleventh Report of the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes addresses the action taken by the Government on the observations and recommendations contained in the Committee's Fifth Report (Seventeenth Lok Sabha). The Fifth Report focused on "Measures Undertaken to Secure Representation Of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in Employment and for their Welfare in National Fertilizers Limited (NFL)" and pertained to the Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers.

The Fifth Report was presented to the Speaker of the Lok Sabha on 25 July, 2020. The Government's replies to all observations and recommendations from that report were received on 16 October, 2020. This current Eleventh Report, which analyzed those replies, was considered and adopted by the Committee at its sitting held on 13 July, 2021. It was then presented to the Lok Sabha and laid in the Rajya Sabha on 04 August, 2021.

What the report found

The Committee categorized the Government's responses to the 12 recommendations from its Fifth Report: 7 recommendations were accepted, 1 was not pursued, and 4 required reiteration because the Committee did not accept the Government's replies.

The Committee observed that as of the date of the Fifth Report, none of the officials in the Management Board of National Fertilizers Limited (NFL) belonged to the Other Backward Classes (OBC) category. The Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers, in its reply, merely detailed the appointment methods for Directors and did not specify efforts to adhere to the Department of Public Enterprises (DPE) Office Memorandum (OM) dated 18 April, 2011, which concerns preference for OBCs in Board appointments.

The report found that as on 31 December, 2019, the total strength of employees in NFL was 3343, with only 443 (13.25%) belonging to the OBC category, which is less than half of the mandated 27% quota. Furthermore, OBC representation was 12.16% in Group A and only 8.52% in Group B, with the latter being particularly concerning as these posts are typically filled through internal promotions. While the Ministry's reply indicated an increase in OBC representation from 4.25% (278 out of 6826) on 31 December, 1995, to 13.25% on 31 December, 2019, the Committee noted a persistent 14% shortage of OBC employees compared to the 27% quota over 25 years. The Ministry's reply did not provide concrete plans to increase OBC representation, particularly in Group B.

The Committee also observed a discrepancy, noting a shortfall of 459 OBC posts across ranks as on 31 December, 2019, while only 10 backlog vacancies in Group A were reported. This led the Committee to conclude that shortfall and backlog vacancies were not being calculated on a realistic basis. The Ministry's reply did not provide information on fulfilling the statutory obligation of achieving 27% reservations in NFL. Lastly, the Committee noted that NFL has not established a dedicated Grievance Redressal Mechanism for OBC employees, finding the existing avenues (personal efforts, Liaison Officers, trade unions) inadequate and potentially deterring employees from registering grievances. It noted that NFL had issued instructions encouraging the formation of OBC Employees' Welfare Associations, but as of the government's reply, no response or option had been received from employees for forming an association.

What was recommended

  • The Committee desires that Action Taken Notes on the Observations/Recommendations contained in Chapter I of this Report should be furnished to the Committee within three months of the presentation of this Report.
  • The Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers should adhere to the Department of Public Enterprises (DPE) Office Memorandum (OM) No. 2(15)/2011-GM dated 18 April, 2011, which stipulates that if persons from the categories of Scheduled Castes (SC), Scheduled Tribes (ST), Other Backward Classes (OBC), and Women come forward with the laid-down criteria for appointment as non-official Directors on the Board of Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSEs), they should be given preference.
  • The Ministry should make an honest effort to forward the names of eligible candidates from these specified marginalized sections of society to the DPE for placement before the Search Committee when filling vacant non-official Director posts.
  • National Fertilizers Limited (NFL) should appropriately address the overall low representation of OBCs and their cadre-wise under-representation, particularly in Group B.
  • NFL should take stock of inadequate representation of OBCs in the organization and devise a concrete plan to ensure 27% reservation for OBCs in NFL.
  • NFL should put in place a dedicated Grievance Redressal Mechanism for the redressal of grievances of OBC employees in an equitable and impartial manner.
  • Wider publicity should be given to the initiative for the formation of OBC Employees' Welfare Associations around places like canteens and workshops in NFL premises.
  • Since there are currently 443 OBC employees in NFL, they should be informed individually through letters, messages, etc., about the initiative to form a welfare association.

What it means for citizens

This report highlights ongoing concerns regarding fair representation and welfare for Indian citizens belonging to Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in National Fertilizers Limited (NFL), a Central Public Sector Enterprise. For OBC citizens seeking employment or currently working at NFL, it means the parliamentary committee is pressing the government to ensure:

  • Their fair representation, including in top management positions and promotions, especially in Group B posts, where their presence is noted to be particularly low.
  • That accurate calculations are made for vacant positions reserved for OBCs, to address existing shortfalls.
  • The establishment of a clear and accessible system for them to raise and resolve workplace grievances impartially.
  • Active encouragement and support for the formation of an OBC Employees' Welfare Association within NFL, which could provide a structured platform to address their concerns.
