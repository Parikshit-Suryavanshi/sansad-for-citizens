Government Action on Recommendations for OBC Representation and Welfare in Indian Oil Corporation Limited

Report date: 2017-03-31

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

This report by the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes details the action taken by the Government of India on recommendations and observations from the Committee's Fifth Report (Sixteenth Lok Sabha). The Fifth Report focused on "Measures undertaken to secure representation of OBCs in employment and for their welfare in Indian Oil Corporation Limited" (IOCL) and pertained to the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas.

The Committee's Fifth Report was presented to the Lok Sabha on 03.05.2016. Subsequently, the Government submitted its replies to all the recommendations contained in that report on 28.09.2016. This Seventh Report, which examined these government replies, was considered and adopted by the Committee at a sitting held on 22.03.2017.

What the report found

The Committee examined the Government's responses to the 10 recommendations and observations from its Fifth Report. Of these, one recommendation was accepted by the Government. Three recommendations were not pursued further by the Committee based on the Government's replies. However, the Committee did not accept the Government's replies for four recommendations and deemed them to require reiteration. Final replies from the Government were still awaited for two other recommendations.

The Committee noted that there was no representation of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) on the Board of Directors of IOCL. It also observed that the percentage of OBC employees in IOCL's total strength was a little more than 13% even after more than two decades of implementing OBC reservations. While the Committee initially identified a shortfall of 173 OBC vacancies in Group 'A' posts as on 30.06.2015, the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas clarified that the roster backlog in Group A was 43 as on 30.06.2015. The Ministry stated that no selections from campuses had been made from FY 2013-14 due to judicial pronouncements, but they were scheduled to commence from FY 2016-17. Regarding pre-recruitment training for OBC candidates, the Ministry cited practical difficulties in providing such training to a large number of reserved category candidates applying for engineer recruitment.

The Committee also noted that 27% reservation for OBC candidates in retail outlet (RO) dealerships was approved in July 2012, with new Dealer Selection Guidelines introduced in May 2014 (except in Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, Nagaland, and Mizoram). As on 31.12.2015, 249 candidates were selected under the OBC category for RO dealerships, but only 36 of these had been commissioned. For compassionate employment cases, out of 16 pending requests for OBC applicants, 13 wards were still studying as on 31.12.2015, and 3 proposals were under consideration. As per the Government's latest reply, employment has been provided to wards of two deceased employees, and final approval for one other case is under process.

What was recommended

  • Indian Oil Corporation Limited (IOCL) should make all efforts to appoint Liaison Officers for Other Backward Classes (OBCs) from the OBC community to address grievances and boost morale. (Accepted by Government)
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should take appropriate steps to ensure OBC representation on the Board of Directors in all Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), including upstream and downstream oil companies. (Reiterated by Committee)
  • The Ministry should make concerted efforts to fill all shortfall of vacancies in a time-bound manner, widely advertise vacancies in rural and remote areas, and provide pre-recruitment training to OBC candidates. (Reiterated by Committee)
  • All backlog OBC vacancies, particularly 42 in Group 'A' in 'other than open competition' as on 30.6.2015, should be fulfilled on a priority basis without further delay, especially after the Madras High Court verdict in favour of campus recruitment by PSUs. (Reiterated by Committee)
  • IOCL should take the initiative to start pre-recruitment training especially for OBC candidates on a priority basis to provide a level playing field, exploring the possibility of using its apex Training Institutes, Indian Oil Institute of Petroleum Management. (Reiterated by Committee)
  • IOCL should take concerted steps to provide employment to the three remaining compassionate appointment candidates without delay, and the Ministry, in consultation with IOCL, should expeditiously remove the pendency of compassionate appointment cases, including those of OBC candidates, in a time-bound manner. (Final reply awaited)
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should set up a proper and efficient monitoring mechanism to oversee the implementation of the reservation policy for OBCs in the allocation of Retail Outlet (RO) Dealerships and to resolve difficulties in timely commissioning of dealerships. The Committee also treats the Government's reply regarding seeking a separate undertaking from OBC candidates (even after a valid OBC certificate for non-creamy layer status) as interim and urges the Ministry to provide a final view on the issue. (Final reply awaited)

What it means for citizens

This report reflects the ongoing efforts by the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes to ensure that policies aimed at securing representation and welfare for OBC citizens are effectively implemented within Indian Oil Corporation Limited (IOCL) and related areas.

For OBC individuals seeking employment in IOCL, the Committee is pushing for the prompt filling of vacant reserved positions, the wider advertisement of job opportunities in rural and remote areas, and the provision of pre-recruitment training to help them compete effectively. For OBC individuals interested in obtaining retail outlet dealerships, the report highlights concerns about the slow pace of commissioning these dealerships and questions the requirement for additional undertakings, advocating for better monitoring and a review of application procedures to streamline the process. The report also addresses the importance of adequate representation of OBCs in decision-making bodies like the Board of Directors and advocates for Liaison Officers from the OBC community to address grievances, which could provide a more comfortable avenue for OBC employees to raise their concerns.
