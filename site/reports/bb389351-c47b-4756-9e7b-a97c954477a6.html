Committee Examines Public Borrowing to Augment Railway Plan Finance

Report date: 1986-12-02

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 08

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee examined the topic of "Resource Mobilisation-Public Borrowing for Augmenting Railway Plan Finance." This involved exploring how the Indian Railways could raise additional funds through public borrowing to increase their finances for development plans.

The Committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Transport (Department of Railways-Railway Board) and the Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs) jointly on September 24, 1986. They also sought views from non-official witnesses, Dr. H. K. Paranjpe, Shri C. Venkataraman, and Shri M. N. Buch, who furnished memoranda and provided oral evidence on November 6, 1986. The report was considered and adopted by the Committee at their sitting held on November 26, 1986.

What the report found

The Committee noted that the Indian Railways' capital requirements are currently met by the General Revenues of the Government of India through a perpetual loan, meaning a loan that does not need to be repaid but on which interest (dividend) is paid indefinitely. The dividend rate was 6% on capital invested up to March 31, 1980, and 6.5% thereafter. At the end of 1985-86, the Railways had a 'Deferred Dividend Liability' of Rs. 526.86 crores (unpaid dividend amounts), and an additional Rs. 336.36 crores due for development fund works, making their total indebtedness to General Revenues Rs. 863.22 crores.

The Railways face a "social burden" which refers to the cost incurred by providing services (like certain freight and passenger fares) at rates below actual cost for public benefit, amounting to Rs. 1121 crores in 1985-86. The Railways' projected requirement for the Seventh Five Year Plan was Rs. 18,500 crores, but the fixed outlay was only Rs. 12,334 crores. To address resource insufficiency, the Railways, in consultation with the Ministry of Finance, planned to resort to public borrowings by creating a new company, the Indian Railway Finance Corporation Ltd. (IRFC), to raise up to Rs. 250 crores through market bonds, starting with Rs. 100 crores. The Ministry of Finance cleared the establishment of IRFC as part of a larger plan to raise Rs. 1130 crores through public sector undertakings in 1986-87, and this does not require an amendment to the Railway Act.

The proposed IRFC bonds would have a maturity period of 10 to 20 years, carry a 10% tax-free annual interest rate, and be secured by assets (such as rolling stock and equipment) purchased by IRFC and leased to the Railways. The Railways would pay IRFC charges covering the bond interest, IRFC's service charges (making the effective cost to Railways 10.5%), and an annuity (regular payments) for bond redemption. The initial equity capital for IRFC (1/10th of the bond issue) would come from General Revenues. The Committee observed that the Railways' capital output ratio was 3.26% in 1984-85 and expressed apprehension that the total cost of this bond capital to the Government, factoring in interest, administrative costs, and investor tax benefits, could be around 17%. Given the Railways' existing difficulty in meeting lower dividend liabilities, the Committee felt this scheme might increase, rather than relieve, the burden on the General Exchequer. The Committee also noted that mobilizing Rs. 100 crores should not be a significant effort, considering prior fare and freight increases (estimated to generate Rs. 565 crores in 1985-86 and Rs. 320 crores in November 1985) and the larger total capital budget of the Government.

What was recommended

  • A certain part of the overall resources mobilized by the Finance Ministry through loans or bonds should be earmarked for the Railways.
  • Market borrowings should be conducted by a single government agency, either the Government of India in the Ministry of Finance or a single corporation as suggested later in the report.
  • Ways and means should be explored to overcome the problem of limits to Government borrowing (vis-a-vis deficit financing, which is when government spending exceeds its revenue).
  • If the current scheme of raising Rs. 100 crores through bonds is a first step towards generating much larger resources, then instead of creating a separate corporation for the Railways, a bigger corporation under the Ministry of Finance should be established to deal with bond issues or market borrowings of all Government Corporations and Companies. Such a step would increase efficiency, expertise, uniformity of approach, coordination, and the methodology of raising public borrowings.
  • In the event that repeated issues of bonds or public borrowings are needed to raise funds for future railway development, the Committee agrees to the Government issuing separate bonds specifically for Railways or setting up a bigger Corporation under the Ministry of Finance for raising funds for Government Companies and the Railways.

What it means for citizens

The report discusses plans to raise additional funds for Indian Railways through public borrowing, specifically by issuing tax-free bonds via a new entity, the Indian Railway Finance Corporation (IRFC). This initiative aims to secure money for railway development and expansion, which could lead to improvements in railway infrastructure and services. The report indicates that the Railways already bear a "social burden" by keeping passenger fares and freight charges for common goods below cost.

However, the Committee raised concerns that while the bonds offer a 10% tax-free interest to investors, the total cost of this capital to the government could be around 17%. Since the Railways have historically struggled to meet lower dividend payments on existing government loans, the Committee apprehended that this higher-cost borrowing might further strain railway finances and potentially increase the burden on the central government's budget (General Exchequer). A Ministry of Finance representative stated that if bond targets are not met, taxes through the budget would have to go up. A Railway Board representative also mentioned that raising tariffs is an alternative, but it would have an inflationary effect on the economy.
