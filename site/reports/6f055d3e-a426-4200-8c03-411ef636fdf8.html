Report on Government's Action Regarding OBC Representation and Welfare in GAIL

Report date: 2023-08-04

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

This Twenty-Third Report from the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes discusses the action taken by the Government on observations and recommendations from the Committee's Sixteenth Report (Seventeenth Lok Sabha). The previous report focused on measures undertaken to secure representation of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment and for their welfare in Gas Authority of India Limited (GAIL), pertaining to the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas.

The Sixteenth Report was presented to Lok Sabha and laid in Rajya Sabha on 2022-12-21. The Government's replies to the observations and recommendations in that report were received on 2023-04-11. The Committee then considered and adopted the Draft Action Taken Report at a sitting held on 2023-08-02. This Twenty-Third Report was subsequently presented to Lok Sabha and laid in Rajya Sabha on 2023-08-04.

What the report found

The Sixteenth Report contained 7 observations and recommendations. Out of these, 6 recommendations (Sl. Nos. 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7) were accepted by the Government. One recommendation (Sl. No. 2) had a reply from the Government that was not accepted by the Committee and requires reiteration. The report found no recommendations that the Committee decided not to pursue, and no final replies from the Government are still awaited.

Specifically, the report noted that as on 2022-04-30, GAIL had 1143 OBC employees out of a total of 4789, constituting 23.86% of the total, which is below the mandated 27% overall representation for OBCs. In Group 'A' service, OBC representation was 21.05%, also below 27%. However, representation in Group 'B' was 30.42%, in Group 'C' was 35.73%, and in Group 'D' was 27.90%, all of which were above the 27% mandate. The report also noted that there was no representation of OBC category in the Board of Directors of GAIL, with 4 director positions being vacant at the time.

Regarding backlog vacancies for OBCs, there were 31 in the Management category and 31 in the Non-Management category as on 2022-07-31, totaling 62 vacancies. GAIL advertised 13 and 5 of these for Special Recruitment Drives (SRD) in September 2022 for Management and Non-Management cadres respectively. Additionally, 13 backlog vacancies for OBC (NCL) in the Management Cadre were advertised through Campus Recruitment 22-23, and 26 for OBC (NCL) in Non-Management Cadre through Open Recruitment-2022 in August 2022, meaning 57 out of 62 backlog vacancies were advertised. The report also noted that OBC Employees’ Welfare Association in GAIL was in the process of constitution. It observed that out of 12 Liaison Officers nominated at regional levels, only 3 were from the OBC category.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should take steps and explore ways to expedite the recruitment process in Group ‘A’ posts in GAIL to achieve the mandated 27% reservation level for Other Backward Classes (OBCs), and the action taken in this regard should be conveyed to the Committee Secretariat at the earliest.
  • The Committee desires to know how many backlog vacancies have been filled through Special Recruitment Drives initiated by GAIL and the resultant OBC strength of the cadres after these drives. The action taken should be conveyed to the Committee at the earliest.
  • Further Action Taken Notes on the observations/recommendations contained in Chapter-I of the Report should be furnished to the Committee within three months of the presentation of this Report.

What it means for citizens

The report indicates that efforts are ongoing to improve the representation of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment at Gas Authority of India Limited (GAIL). For citizens belonging to OBCs, this could mean increased opportunities for employment, particularly in Group 'A' positions (Management category), as the Committee has reiterated the need to address the shortfall in this area. The Committee's focus on filling backlog vacancies through Special Recruitment Drives also suggests potential job openings for OBC candidates.

Additionally, the report highlights the formation of an OBC Employees' Welfare Association within GAIL, which could serve as a platform for OBC employees to raise their concerns and ensure their welfare is addressed. The ongoing support for skill development centers by GAIL, including in metro cities, means that socially and educationally backward families, including OBCs, may have access to vocational training to enhance their employability and secure livelihoods. The Committee's desire for OBC representation on GAIL's Board of Directors also points towards efforts for greater social inclusion at higher decision-making levels.
