Indian Railways to Pay 6.5% Interim Dividend to General Revenues for 2005-06

Report date: 2005-04-28

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 14

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee (2004) examined the rate of dividend (a fixed payment on capital advanced by the Government of India) payable by the Railway Undertaking to General Revenues (the central government's funds) and other related financial matters for the financial year 2005-06. The committee’s First Report, presented on December 21, 2004, had previously recommended an interim dividend rate of 6.5% for the year 2004-05.

The discussion was based on an interim Memorandum submitted by the Ministry of Railways on February 9, 2005. This memorandum included differing views: the Ministry of Finance proposed increasing the dividend rate to 7% for 2005-06, while the Ministry of Railways pleaded for a reduction to 6%. The Committee heard evidence from representatives of both Ministries on March 10, 2005. The committee also considered the financial health of the Indian Railways and the funding arrangements for various railway reserve funds, including the Depreciation Reserve Fund, Pension Fund, Development Fund, Capital Fund, Railway Safety Fund, and Special Railway Safety Fund. An additional issue discussed was the Ministry of Railways’ request for exemption from paying dividend on "capital-at-charge" (capital contributed by General Revenues in the form of interest-bearing loan capital) belonging to structural assets received 40 years ago.

What the report found

The report noted that the capital-at-charge of the Indian Railways increased from Rs.827 crore in 1950-51 to Rs.50821 crore in 2004-05 (Revised Estimates). The annual dividend payable to General Revenues rose from less than Rs.100 crore until March 31, 1964, to Rs.3638.00 crore for 2005-06 (Budget Estimates). In total, the Railways have paid Rs.36709 crore as dividend to General Revenues. The Ministry of Finance had permitted deferment of dividend payments totalling Rs.2823 crore by the end of 2001-02. Of this, the Ministry of Railways repaid Rs.650 crore by the end of 2004-05 and proposed to repay Rs.300 crore in 2005-06 (Budget Estimates).

The Ministry of Railways stated that while its financial position showed some improvement, it was "far from satisfactory" and significant funds were still required for essential safety and developmental needs. It also reported outstanding payments of approximately Rs.2963 crore as on November 30, 2004, owed by railway users, particularly State Electricity Boards. Additionally, the Railways incurred a burden of approximately Rs.940 crore due to increases in High Speed Diesel (HSD) oil prices. The Ministry of Finance observed that the effective dividend rate paid by Railways was only 3.6% after considering subsidies, and that the weighted average cost of all outstanding government borrowings remained at 9.3%, despite the average cost of new borrowings being 5.7% in 2003-04 and increasing to 6.2% in 2004-05. The Government had decided on a 9-year timeframe for Railways to clear the deferred dividend liability of Rs.2173 crore.

What was recommended

  • For the year 2005-06, dividend to General Revenues be paid, as an interim measure, at 6.5% on the entire capital invested on Railways from the General Revenues, regardless of the year of investment. This includes the amount payable to States as grants in lieu of passenger fare tax and contribution for assisting States for safety works during the year 2004-05.
  • All other existing concessions on residential buildings, new lines, and subsidies from General Revenues be continued for the year 2005-06.
  • The contribution to the Depreciation Reserve Fund (DRF) be made in line with the system's capacity to generate internal resources, and the committee desires to be informed of the actual contribution made to DRF in the fiscal year 2005-06.
  • The appropriation to the Pension Fund from Revenues be set at Rs.6950 crore in 2005-06, considering a possible higher requirement due to the merger of 50% Dearness Allowance (DA) with basic pension, subject to adjustments based on estimated withdrawals. This, along with Rs.100 crore from Production Units, would meet the pension requirement of Rs.7050 crore during the year.
  • The committee approved the proposed appropriation of Rs.1853 crore (Budget Estimate) to the Development Fund for 2005-06, which is made from the 'excess' left after paying dividend. The committee requested to be informed of the actual amount credited to this Fund at the end of the financial year 2005-06.
  • The Capital Fund (CF) may not be operated for the present.
  • The question of exempting Railways from paying dividend on capital-at-charge pertaining to structural assets received 40 years ago requires further in-depth study and should be addressed separately later. The Ministry of Railways should submit a comprehensive note with year-wise funds received and dividend paid on such capital-at-charge.
  • The existing principle for interest on various Railway Fund balances in the Public Accounts of India be continued for 2005-06.
  • The balances in the Railway Safety Fund and the Special Railway Safety Fund be treated as interest-free, as both funds are constituted solely from contributions from the General Revenue.

What it means for citizens

This report determines the interim dividend rate that Indian Railways will pay to the central government's funds for the financial year 2005-06. This dividend includes amounts allocated as grants to states for passenger fare tax and contributions to support railway safety work. The committee's recommendations ensure that crucial railway funds, such as the Depreciation Reserve Fund and Pension Fund, are appropriated according to the Railways' financial capacity and needs, which impacts the maintenance of railway assets and the welfare of railway employees.

The decision to treat the Railway Safety Fund and Special Railway Safety Fund as interest-free directly supports critical safety-related projects, such as converting unmanned level crossings and constructing over/under bridges. These projects are intended to enhance safety for both passengers and the general public. The report also highlights the financial challenges faced by the Railways, including significant outstanding dues from State Electricity Boards, which could affect the internal resources available for investments in railway modernization and development. The report does not specify how these financial arrangements will directly alter passenger fares or freight charges for citizens.
