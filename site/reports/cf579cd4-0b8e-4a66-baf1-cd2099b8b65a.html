Parliamentary Committee Reviews OBC Representation and Welfare in Coal India and its Subsidiaries

Report date: 2020-06-13

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

The Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes examined the measures undertaken to secure representation of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment and for their welfare in Coal India Ltd. (CIL), Northern Coalfields Limited (NCL), and South Eastern Coalfields Limited (SECL), all pertaining to the Ministry of Coal. CIL is a major coal producer and employer with seven wholly-owned coal-producing subsidiaries and one mine planning and consultancy company, including NCL and SECL.

The committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Coal, CIL, NCL, and SECL on October 25, 2019, and November 19, 2019. They also undertook a Study Visit to Mumbai on January 13, 2020, where they interacted with OBC representatives of the All India SC/ST/OBC Employees Co-ordination Council. The report was considered and adopted by the Committee at their sitting held on March 19, 2020.

What the report found

The report found that as of January 1, 2019, the overall representation of OBCs in CIL was 21.66%, which showed a decline from 24.97% in 2017 and 22.91% in 2018. CIL attributed this to retirements, deaths, resignations, and changes in technology, but the committee observed a sharper reduction in OBC manpower (20.18%) compared to the overall manpower decline (8%). In Group A (Executive Cadre) posts in CIL, OBC representation was 14.98% as of January 1, 2019, against a mandated 27%. CIL reported that 26.4% of executives appointed between 2007 and 2019 were OBCs, but could not provide data prior to 2007 due to office shifting.

In NCL, the overall OBC representation was 17.33% as of September 2019, which the company stated aligns with Madhya Pradesh's 15% reservation rules. However, 67.29% of OBC employees in NCL were concentrated in Group D (unskilled/semi-skilled jobs). The committee noted that NCL reported no backlog vacancies but observed that in 2019, only 12.78% of 892 filled vacancies were OBCs, and in 2018, only 14.46% of 159 filled seats were OBCs, both below the 15% state quota. This led the committee to conclude that backlog vacancies were not calculated on a realistic basis.

SECL showed an overall OBC representation of 23.4% as of September 2019. However, OBC representation in Group A was 16.55%, and it significantly decreased at higher management levels, such as Chief Manager (0.61%) and General Manager (1.96%). SECL had 36 backlog OBC vacancies as of January 1, 2019 (15 in Group C and 21 in Group D). For the Group D security posts, SECL was in the process of requisitioning security personnel from the Madhya Pradesh State Industrial Security Force.

The report noted that Liaison Officers for OBCs were appointed in CIL and its subsidiaries. CIL's Liaison Officer belonged to the General Category. NCL stated its Backward Classes Cell would be formed soon. CIL and its subsidiaries maintained rosters for direct recruitment and promotion, which were inspected without adverse comments. One OBC member was included in Selection Committees/Boards as per DoPT guidelines. CIL and its subsidiaries provided job-specific post-recruitment and pre-promotion training to employees, including OBCs.

Grievance redressal mechanisms were in place. CIL reported 32 pending grievances at its headquarters as of April 1, 2019, from 1195 received in 2018-19. NCL had 6 pending grievances as of September 2019, from 45 added in 2019. SECL reported nil complaints from 2016 to 2019 related to OBCs, stating that no differentiation is made on the basis of caste/category or religion for complaints. No separate OBC Employees' Associations existed in CIL or its subsidiaries; a combined All India SC/ST/OBC Employees' Co-ordination Council was active.

CIL and its subsidiaries outsourced non-core functions, engaging 73,562 outsourced employees in 2019 across the companies. NCL reported that 40.12% of its 14,179 outsourced workers in 2019 were OBC, while SECL reported 18.6% of its 13,198 contractual workers were OBC. The report stated that contractors were the employers for these workers, and no guidelines currently bind contractors to provide reservation for OBCs. CIL and its subsidiaries have implemented systems like CLIP (Contract Labour Information Portal) to ensure contractors pay minimum wages and statutory deductions. The decline in SECL's total manpower from 85,627 in 2006 to 52,992 in September 2019 was attributed to increased mechanization and retirements, with new employment largely for dependents and against land.

What was recommended

  • The declining trend of OBC employees in CIL needs to be checked immediately, and steps should be taken to ensure no prejudice to OBC interests and representation in the long term.
  • NCL should ensure that OBCs are not concentrated in lower levels and are offered adequate promotional opportunities without discrimination. CIL, as the holding company, should ensure that at least 27% OBC executives are placed in each subsidiary through rotation.
  • SECL should aim to not only meet quantitative OBC reservation requirements but also endeavor to broaden the benefits across all levels of the hierarchy.
  • CIL should create a roadmap to increase the representation of OBCs in Group A posts and share it with the committee at the action taken stage.
  • CIL must strictly adhere to all regulations related to OBC reservation in the future, including reserving 27% of fresh seats without adjusting them against backlog vacancies.
  • NCL should meticulously earmark OBC seats for reservation according to the existing State stipulations during each recruitment process without fail.
  • SECL should fill the existing 21 OBC backlog vacancies for security personnel by conducting a special recruitment drive, without compromising the quota for future policy decisions. The committee should be apprised of the recruitment status for all 36 backlog vacancies (15 in Group C and 21 in Group D), which SECL stated would be filled by April 2020.
  • CIL should immediately appoint an officer from the OBC category as the Liaison Officer. NCL's Backward Classes Cell should be set up without any further delay.
  • Reservation Rosters should be published on the website of the concerned company to allay any concerns regarding non-adherence to reservation guidelines.
  • CIL and its subsidiaries, including NCL and SECL, should examine the demands and issues raised in the memorandum submitted by the All India SC/ST/OBC Employees' Co-ordination Council. They should also provide necessary support and infrastructure for OBC employees to establish a separate OBC Employees’ Welfare Association and hold periodical meetings with its representatives.
  • CIL and its subsidiaries should find a way to ensure representation for backward sections when engaging any workforce on an outsourced basis and issue necessary guidelines. The Ministry of Coal should ensure strict compliance with the Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT) Office Memorandum dated May 15, 2018, regarding reservation for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and OBCs in temporary appointments lasting 45 days or more. Payment of minimum wages and social security benefits (health, education, insurance, provident fund) for outsourced employees should also be ensured.
  • CIL, NCL, and SECL should follow DoPT Office Memorandum No. 49014/12/1988-Estt(C) dated August 22, 1988, regarding the regularization of casual workers, applying all statutory reservation requirements for direct recruitment. Separate rosters should be prepared for regular and temporary appointments for OBCs in CIL and its subsidiaries.
  • CIL, NCL, and SECL are reminded to strictly adhere to various DoPT instructions/guidelines on reservations for SC, ST, and OBCs in: Industrial Workers (O.M.No.8/7/1974-Estt.(SCT) dated August 22, 1975), Temporary Appointment (O.M. No.36036/3/2018 - ESTT. (Res.) dated May 15, 2018), Work-Charged Posts (O.M.No.36021/9/1976-Estt.(SCT) dated February 10, 1977), and Voluntary Agencies (O.M.No.27/12/73-Estt.SCT) dated October 7, 1974).

What it means for citizens

This report highlights the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes' review of how major coal companies, Coal India Ltd. (CIL) and its subsidiaries Northern Coalfields Limited (NCL) and South Eastern Coalfields Limited (SECL), are implementing policies for OBC employment and welfare. For ordinary citizens, particularly those belonging to OBC communities, the report's findings indicate potential challenges in securing fair representation, especially in higher-level positions within these companies.

The recommendations aim to ensure that these public sector enterprises strictly follow government reservation policies, fill backlog vacancies, and provide equitable promotional opportunities. It also emphasizes the importance of transparency through public disclosure of reservation rosters and the establishment of dedicated support mechanisms like separate OBC employees' associations and properly staffed Liaison Officers. Furthermore, the report calls for better protection and fair treatment, including minimum wages and social security benefits, for the large number of contractual workers, many of whom are from OBC communities, even though the companies do not directly employ them on a reserved basis. These measures, if implemented, could lead to more equitable employment practices and improved welfare for OBC citizens seeking jobs in the coal sector.
