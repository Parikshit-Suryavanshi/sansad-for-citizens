Railway Convention Committee Reports on Maintenance of Rolling Stock

Report date: 2018-08-03

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee examined the subject of ‘Maintenance of Rolling Stock’ for Indian Railways. Rolling stock includes passenger trains (conventional coaches, EMUs, DEMUs, MEMUs), goods trains (freight wagons), and locomotives. The Committee obtained background material and written information from the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) and took oral evidence from the Ministry's representatives on 11th September, 2017. The Committee also undertook Study Visits to various Zonal Railways to gather feedback at the field level.

The discussion covered various aspects of rolling stock maintenance, including the types of coaching stock (Integral Coach Factory or ICF type and Linke Hofmann Busch or LHB design coaches), the categories of maintenance activities for coaching stock, and the maintenance procedures for freight wagons, DEMUs, EMUs, MEMUs, and Metro Rolling Stock. It also addressed the Periodic Overhaul (POH) process, condemnation of rolling stock, assessment of future requirements, physical targets and achievements for induction, and fund allocation and utilization for repair and maintenance.

The Committee considered and adopted this Twenty Fourth Report at its sitting held on 02nd August, 2018.

What the report found

The report found that Indian Railways operates two primary types of coaching stock: ICF type and LHB design coaches, with four types of maintenance activities: Rolling-in examination, In-service Running Maintenance, Intermediate and Periodic Overhaul, and Mid-Life Rehabilitation. The maintenance schedule and pattern for passenger trains are defined based on train type, stock type, and kilometers earned. Neutral Train Examiners (NTXR) from the Indian Railways Conference Association (IRCA) are noted to perform a "third party vigil" on the quality of coach repairs in major workshops, generally preventing coaches from being released after Periodic Overhaul (POH) without NTXR approval. However, instances of "Local Passing" (coaches released without Neutral passing) were noted. Quality audits and super checks are conducted by RDSO and non-railway officers to ensure adherence to maintenance procedures and address resource deficiencies related to safety.

For freight wagons, Indian Railways has adopted a philosophy of Preventive Maintenance, including various examinations (Rolling-in, Rolling-out, Safe to Run, Terminating Load, Intensive Examinations, Guard and Driver check) and scheduled maintenance like Periodic Overhaul (POH) and Routine Overhauling (ROH). The report noted that 49663 wagons, 30433 coaches, 760 DEMUs, 4556 EMUs, and 276 electric locos underwent POH during 2016-17. Between 2014-15 and 2016-17, 6921 wagons and 237 coaches not officially due for POH were sent to workshops, which the Ministry justified as beneficial for combining body repairs with POH. The time taken for wagon removal after POH varies by workshop due to operational requirements.

The report also found that 15875 wagons, 5082 coaches, 616 EMUs/MEMUs, 194 electric locos, and 3 diesel power cars were condemned from 2014-15 to 2016-17 based on age-cum-condition or completion of codal life. As of 31 July, 2017, 827 wagons not fit for use were lying in sick line yards. Additionally, on 31 July, 2017, around 4400 repaired and fit-for-use wagons were idle in various Zonal Railways, constituting 1.64% of the total holding of these wagon types. Rolling stock requirements are projected centrally by the Traffic Directorate on a Pan-India basis, not through indents from Zonal Railways. For 2016-17, 10718 wagons were inducted against a target of 11000, and 4321 coaches, DMUs, and EMUs/MEMUs were inducted against a target of 4302. Fund utilization for repair and maintenance work showed shortfalls in 2015-16 and 2016-17, particularly for DEMU/EMU and Electrical General Services (Train Lighting, fans & Air-conditioning). The Ministry noted constraints due to capacity shortfalls and infrastructural deficiencies in some maintenance units/workshops.

What was recommended

  • All maintenance schedules and activities for coaching stock, including repairs, replacement, refurbishing, washing, and cleaning, should be further streamlined and strengthened for overall security and passenger convenience.
  • The Ministry should ensure that no coach is removed from the workshop after Periodic Overhaul (POH) without the strict third-party vigil of the Neutral Train Examiners (NTXR).
  • Deficiencies detected during quality audits and super checks in coaching depots and workshops should be rectified unfailingly, and suggestions for system improvements should be complied with promptly.
  • The Ministry should continue to prioritize timely maintenance and repair of wagons to prevent defects/failures in service and ensure optimum performance of the wagon fleet.
  • Indian Railways should strive to continuously upgrade the technology of freight wagons to enable higher speeds, maximize throughput, and effectively compete with other transporters, as goods transportation is a major source of earnings.
  • Periodic inspections, super checks, and safety drives should also be carried out for DEMUs, EMUs, MEMUs, and Kolkata Metro rolling stock in their maintenance sheds and workshops to ensure proper maintenance of critical components/sub-systems and alertness towards safety.
  • The Ministry should revisit its decision to send wagons and coaches not due for Periodic Overhaul (POH) to workshops on the plea of special cases.
  • The Railways should ensure that wagons and coaches due for Periodic Overhaul (POH) are attended to/repaired within the prescribed cycle time so they can be timely pressed into service to generate potential revenue.
  • The Ministry should prescribe a uniform time limit for the removal of wagons from workshops after Periodic Overhaul (POH), irrespective of operational requirements.
  • Along with maintenance activities, the Railways should ensure proper handling and careful operation of freight wagons to minimize damage during loading/unloading and the subsequent need for repairs.
  • The system for condemning rolling stock should be further strengthened to ensure that wagons/coaches not fit for use are promptly condemned with the approval of the competent authority.
  • The Railways should implement other appropriate measures to ensure that no condemned/rejected coach or wagon is put into service for safety reasons.
  • The detention of fit-for-use wagons, particularly those held as a cushion for rejected wagons, should be kept to the barest minimum period to avoid delays in freight operations and consequential revenue loss.
  • The Railway Board should associate Zonal Railways, as the ultimate users, in the consultation process for their respective rolling stock requirements to establish a scientific and pragmatic approach, without impeding the central procurement system.
  • The Ministry should make a realistic projection for the induction of wagons, which are a major source of earnings for Indian Railways, to ensure optimum achievement of physical targets in the future.
  • The Ministry should take all necessary steps for the optimum utilization of allocated funds for repair and maintenance work of rolling stock.
  • The Railways should prioritize and pay focused attention to removing infrastructural deficiencies and bottlenecks in workshops/depots to enable maintenance units to effectively cater to current and future maintenance requirements.

What it means for citizens

For ordinary people, the report's findings and recommendations relate to the safety, reliability, and efficiency of the railway system. Improved maintenance of passenger coaches, including stricter quality checks and prompt rectification of deficiencies, aims to enhance overall security and convenience during train travel. Ensuring that only fit and safe coaches are in service, through vigilant inspections and proper condemnation procedures, directly contributes to passenger safety.

The emphasis on timely and effective maintenance of freight wagons, alongside efforts to upgrade their technology, seeks to improve the speed and efficiency of goods transportation. This can have broader implications for the economy, such as potentially smoother supply chains and better availability of goods. The call for optimal utilization of allocated funds and addressing infrastructural deficiencies in maintenance facilities is intended to improve the overall functioning of the railway system, which, in turn, supports more reliable and safer train services for both passengers and freight.
