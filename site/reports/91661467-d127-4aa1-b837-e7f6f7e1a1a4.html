Committee on MPLADS Reviews Action Taken on Tsunami Relief, Audit Findings, and Scheme Guidelines

Report date: 2008-12-15

Committee: Committee on MPLADS

Lok Sabha: 14

What was discussed

This Fourteenth Report of the Committee on Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme (MPLADS) focuses on the action taken by the Government on the recommendations from the Committee's Thirteenth Report. The Thirteenth Report, which was presented to the Lok Sabha on 2006-12-07, covered various representations and proposals related to the MPLAD Scheme, including suggestions to amend its Guidelines.

The replies from the Government regarding these recommendations were received on 2008-02-13. The Committee then reviewed these replies, and the current report was considered and adopted at their sitting held on 2008-10-24.

What the report found

The report categorizes 39 observations and recommendations from the Thirteenth Report: 32 were accepted by the Government, one was not pursued by the Committee, five were not accepted by the Government and required reiteration, and one received an interim reply.

The Committee expressed deep concern over the "tardy progress" of rehabilitation and reconstruction work in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands following the Tsunami. They noted a "lack of proper planning, initiative, poor administrative decisions, and heavy dependence on Central Government for decision making" which resulted in "all round dissatisfaction" among camp residents who were still awaiting permanent shelters. The Ministry's response of merely forwarding suggestions was deemed "casual," with no follow-up. The report noted that as of 2007-02-28, Rs. 67 lakh out of a total of Rs. 854.81 lakh for the Andaman and Nicobar Islands was yet to be transferred by Nodal District Authorities. Construction of a community hall at Joginder Nagar, Campbell Bay, Nicobar District was "in progress" as of 2007-02-28, but its completion was uncertain more than three years after the tragedy, and the site was still not accessible.

Regarding Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) Reports on MPLADS, the Committee was dismayed that even after a decade, Action Taken Notes on audit paras had not been submitted to the Director General of Audit. For the 1997 report, complete replies were received from only 10 out of 32 States/Union Territories (UTs), with 22 providing partial replies. Similarly, for the 2000 report, only 6 out of 33 States/UTs provided complete replies, with 27 providing partial replies.

The Committee noted concerns about poor implementation of the scheme, including sanctioning works without MP recommendations, huge unspent balances, non-submission of utilization certificates, incomplete/abandoned works, and weak monitoring. The Ministry stated that revised MPLADS Guidelines were issued in 2005-11, the scheme was brought under the Right to Information Act, 2005, and a software for online monitoring was launched in 2004-11. However, the Committee found the Ministry's reply on identifying reasons for poor implementation and details of the monitoring agency (NABARD Consultancy Service, or NABCONS) to be "too general."

The Committee reiterated its recommendation that the definition of "family" for MPLADS purposes (which included MP, spouse, parents, brothers, sisters, children, grandchildren, their spouses, and in-laws) should be restricted to "blood relations only." The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation had adopted a broad definition due to "criticism in the media and elsewhere" and noted that the Ministry of Law and Justice found no legal objection to continuing with it. The Committee considered the Ministry's stand "too rigid."

The report also noted that the recommendation to increase the annual allocation per MP from Rs. 2 crore to Rs. 5 crore was "under examination of the Government."

What was recommended

  • The Andaman and Nicobar Administration should develop innovative designs and concepts for rehabilitation and employment, focusing on available regional strengths, assets, resources, and involving local people.
  • The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation should immediately submit a status report on the action taken by the Andaman and Nicobar Administration regarding rehabilitation and employment.
  • The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation should promptly engage with relevant authorities to provide all necessary information, including details of transferred funds and work-wise/MP-wise utilization and completion reports for tsunami rehabilitation works, without further delay.
  • A completion report for the construction work of the Community Hall at Joginder Nagar should be submitted to the Committee without further delay.
  • A status report concerning the transfer of the outstanding amount of Rs. 67 lakh to the Andaman and Nicobar Islands should be communicated to the Committee immediately.
  • The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation should actively pursue with the concerned authorities to ensure that complete replies to the audit paras, including remedial measures, are resolved according to procedure without delay, and the Committee should be regularly informed of the progress.
  • A detailed report covering the implementation and monitoring aspects of the MPLADS Scheme should be provided to the Committee within three months of this report's presentation.
  • The Ministry should conduct a comprehensive study to identify loopholes and errors in the scheme's implementation based on feedback from States/Union Territories and propose necessary amendments to the MPLADS Guidelines, submitting the action taken report promptly.
  • The definition of "family" for MPLADS purposes should be limited to blood relatives, and the Government is urged to reconsider its decision on this matter.
  • The Government should reconsider the Committee's non-accepted observations and recommendations regarding the MPLAD Scheme Guidelines when amending the existing Guidelines.
  • The Ministry should prioritize taking action on the Committee's long-standing recommendation to increase the allocation for each MP from Rs. 2 crore to Rs. 5 crore, especially given the rising cost of works, and keep the Committee informed of the progress.

What it means for citizens

This report highlights that the rehabilitation and reconstruction efforts in Tsunami-affected areas, particularly the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, faced significant delays and coordination issues. This means that citizens impacted by the tragedy may have experienced prolonged waits for permanent housing and essential facilities, as the progress of work was described as "tardy."

The report also points to ongoing challenges in the implementation and monitoring of the MPLADS scheme across the country. Issues such as unspent funds, non-submission of utilization certificates, and delays in addressing audit concerns suggest that developmental projects intended for local communities might not be executed efficiently or effectively. This could hinder the timely delivery of intended benefits to the public. The Committee's continued emphasis on restricting the definition of "family" for MPLADS purposes points to concerns regarding transparency in how projects are recommended, especially if they are linked to the broader family networks of Members of Parliament. If the recommendation to increase the annual MPLADS allocation from Rs. 2 crore to Rs. 5 crore per MP is accepted, it could potentially mean more funds would be available for a larger number of local projects in constituencies, addressing basic community requirements.
