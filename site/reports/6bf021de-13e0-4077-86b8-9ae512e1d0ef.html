Government Implements OBC Reservations in Admissions and Acts on Employment Gaps in Educational Institutions

Report date: 2021-08-04

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

This report, the Tenth Report, details the action taken by the Government on observations and recommendations from the Committee's Second Report (Seventeenth Lok Sabha). The Second Report, which focused on "Measures undertaken to secure representation of OBCs in admissions and employment in Kendriya Vidyalayas/Navodaya Vidyalayas and Sainik Schools/Rashtriya Military Schools," was presented to the Hon'ble Speaker, Lok Sabha on 30th December, 2019, and laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha on 13th December, 2019. The full replies from the Ministry of Education to the observations and recommendations were received on 13th April, 2021. The Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes considered and adopted this Draft Action Taken Report at its sitting held on 13th July, 2021.

What the report found

The Committee's Second Report contained 7 observations/recommendations. The current report found that 6 of these (Sl. Nos. 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, and 7) were accepted by the Government. There were no recommendations that the Committee decided not to pursue, nor any where the Government's replies were rejected and required reiteration. One recommendation (Sl. No. 4) had final replies still awaited from the Government.

Regarding admissions, the Ministry of Education issued instructions in March, 2020, to Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS) and Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti (NVS) to implement reservation provisions for Other Backward Classes (OBC) students from the academic session 2020-21 onwards. KVS updated its Admission Guidelines, and NVS notified 27% reservation for OBC candidates for admission to Class VI from the 2020-21 session. The Sainik Schools Society introduced 27% reservation for OBCs (Non-creamy layer) for admissions in Sainik Schools from the academic session 2021-22, following a letter dated 13th October, 2020. Similarly, 27% reservation for OBCs (Non-creamy layer) for admissions in Rashtriya Military Schools (RMSs) was introduced from the academic session 2021-22, with orders issued on 18th January, 2021.

For employment, the overall representation of OBC employees in KVS was 26.26% and in NVS was 30.02%, which the Committee considered fair. However, specific posts had lower OBC representation: in KVS, Assistant Commissioner (20.58%), Assistant Section Officer (17.03%), and Subordinate Staff (18.12%); in NVS, Stenographers (17.02%), Junior Secretariat Assistant (19.69%), and MTS (6.57%). The Ministry stated that KVS, since 1993, had counted OBC candidates selected on their own merit under the unreserved category. The Ministry committed to undertaking a fresh exercise to enumerate OBC employees in KVS and NVS, excluding those who qualified on their own merit, and to make necessary corrections and take proactive steps to fill backlog vacancies.

In RMSs, Group 'A' OBC representation was 3 out of 41 total employees, and Group 'B' was 8 out of 40. The Ministry reported that 3 Group 'A' OBC posts were held out of a calculated 5.4 direct recruitment posts, and recruitment for 2 remaining OBC posts was underway. For Group 'B', 8 OBC vacancies were filled out of a calculated 16.2 posts, with recruitment for 8 remaining vacancies under process. For Sainik Schools, OBC representation among Academic & Administrative Staff and General Employees was 20.70%, and 18.62% within Academic and Administrative Staff. The Sainik School Society noted the recommendations for further action.

Regarding temporary posts, the Ministry of Education informed KVS and NVS of the Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT) Office Memorandum (O.M.) dated 15th May, 2018, which mandates reservation for SC/ST/OBC in temporary appointments lasting 45 days or more. Separate Liaison Officers for OBCs were appointed in KVS and NVS for enforcing reservation provisions and welfare measures.

The report also noted that out of 661 sanctioned Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas (JNVs), 24 were non-functional as on 25th October, 2019. Since then, 11 more JNVs have become functional between December 2019 and March 2021, and efforts are ongoing to make the remaining 13 functional.

What was recommended

  • Start the 24 sanctioned but non-functional Navodaya Vidyalayas without further delay.
  • Open more schools in the four categories (Kendriya Vidyalayas, Navodaya Vidyalayas, Sainik Schools, Rashtriya Military Schools) in backward regions of the country to meet rising demand for quality education and increase outreach.
  • Provide 27% reservation in admission to Socially and Educationally Backward Classes of citizens in all private educational institutions (aided or unaided by the State, affiliated by CBSE and other authorities, excluding minority educational institutions referred to in clause (1) of Article 30).
  • A decision on the reservation of OBCs in admissions to KVs/NVs should be arrived at within three months and implemented to ensure benefits for OBC students in the 2020-20 session.
  • The matter of reservation in admission of OBC students in Sainik Schools/Rashtriya Military Schools should be decided favorably in the upcoming Board Meeting without fail.
  • Take proactive steps to ensure fair representation of OBCs in specific posts in KVS (Assistant Commissioner, Assistant Section Officer, Subordinate Staff) and NVS (Stenographers, Junior Secretariat Assistant, MTS) where representation is below par.
  • Strictly follow all Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT) orders and guidelines regarding OBC reservation in recruitment to ensure that OBC candidates selected on their own merit are not adjusted within the reserved category.
  • Undertake a fresh exercise of enumerating OBC employees in KVS and NVS, after excluding those who got selected on their own merit, and fill fresh vacancies based on this exercise.
  • Scrupulously implement OBC reservation in Sainik Schools/Rashtriya Military Schools employment and advertise and fill all unfilled OBC vacancies immediately as per relevant DoPT Orders and Guidelines.
  • Prepare separate roster registers for temporary appointments (45 days or more) for OBCs in strict compliance with the relevant DoPT O.M. and Guidelines by the Ministry of Human Resources for all its allied institutions/autonomous organizations.
  • Designate a separate Liaison Officer for OBCs for enforcement in respect of matters relating to the representation and other welfare measures of OBCs, and the Liaison Officer should be designated from the OBC category only.
  • Further Action Taken Notes on Chapter-I and the final reply on recommendation contained in Chapter V should be furnished to the Committee within three months of the presentation of this Report.
  • The Committee would like to be apprised of the number of admissions of OBC candidates made in KVS/NVs and Sainik Schools/Rashtriya Military Schools during the academic sessions of 2020-21 and 2021-22.
  • The exercise for enumerating OBC employees in KVS and NVS, after excluding those selected on their own merit, should be completed at the earliest.
  • The Committee would like to be apprised of the steps taken for filling the specific posts in KVS and NVS where OBC representation is not up to the level of stipulated 27%.
  • The Committee would like to be apprised of the specific action taken by the Sainik School Society to increase OBC representation to the stipulated reservation level.
  • The direct recruitment of 02 posts of OBCs in Group 'A' and 08 posts in Group 'B', which are reported to be under process in Rashtriya Military Schools, should be completed soon and the Committee be apprised thereof.

What it means for citizens

Citizens belonging to Other Backward Classes (OBCs) can now expect reservations in admissions in Kendriya Vidyalayas and Navodaya Vidyalayas starting from the academic session 2020-21, and in Sainik Schools and Rashtriya Military Schools starting from the academic session 2021-22. This means increased opportunities for OBC students to access quality education in these institutions.

In terms of employment, there is a focus on improving OBC representation in various posts within Kendriya Vidyalaya Sangathan (KVS), Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti (NVS), Sainik Schools, and Rashtriya Military Schools. This includes efforts to fill existing vacancies and ensure that OBC candidates selected on their own merit are not incorrectly counted against reserved seats. The appointment of dedicated Liaison Officers for OBCs in KVS and NVS is intended to ensure proper implementation of reservation policies and welfare measures. Additionally, the government's efforts to operationalize more Navodaya Vidyalayas mean expanded access to quality education, particularly for talented children from rural areas.
