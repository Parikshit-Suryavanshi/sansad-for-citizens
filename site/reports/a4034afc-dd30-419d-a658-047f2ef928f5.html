Railway Convention Committee's Fourth Report on Delegation of Powers, Zonal Railway Organisation, and Railway Board's Office

Report date: 1978-12-22

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 06

What was discussed

This report examines the delegation of powers to General Managers, the organisation of Zonal Railways, and the organisation of the Railway Board's Office. The subject was initially selected for examination by the previous Railway Convention Committee (1973), which also looked into the restructuring of the Railway Board and the feasibility of converting railway production units into autonomous corporations. However, that committee could not finalise its report due to the dissolution of the Lok Sabha on January 18, 1977.

The current Railway Convention Committee (1977) continued the examination. Based on evidence and information gathered, the Committee decided to present a separate report covering the aspects of delegation of powers to General Managers, organisation of Zonal Railways, and organisation of the Railway Board's Office. Other subjects, such as the restructuring of the Railway Board and the feasibility of converting railway production units into autonomous corporations, were left for an in-depth examination by a Sub-Committee. The draft report was adopted by a Sub-Committee on December 8, 1978, and by the full Committee on December 15, 1978.

What the report found

The Ministry of Railways stated that the Railway Board functions as a top policy-making body and the professional and managerial apex for Indian Railways, which necessitates a certain measure of coordination and control from the Board. While General Managers are intended to have full powers for day-to-day management within policy frameworks, the report noted that there is often an unavoidable overlap of functions between the Railway Board and Zonal Railways, partly due to a high volume of public and employee representations. The Administrative Reforms Commission had previously recommended maximum possible delegation of administrative and financial powers from the Board to General Managers, and further redelegation to lower authorities.

The report found that powers delegated to General Managers were liberalised in 1954 and 1964, and additional powers were delegated on June 29, 1974. Further additional administrative and financial powers were delegated to General Managers on December 8, 1977, with necessary orders issued on December 7, 1977. Despite claims that General Managers had sufficient powers, the report observed that 58 matters still required prior sanction from the Railway Board, indicating a tendency towards centralisation.

The Committee noted that on average, 33,158 references from rail users and 50,403 from railway personnel were received annually by the Railway Board between 1975-76 and 1977-78. In 1977 alone, 79,613 petitions from non-gazetted Railway personnel were received by the Railway Board. The report found that the Railway Board does not keep records of cases where General Managers refer matters within their full powers or of the Board's interventions in such cases. The Committee observed that these large numbers of representations indicate that rail users and employees do not receive prompt attention at the field level.

Regarding Zonal Railways, the report stated there are nine Zonal Railways divided into 53 Divisions, based on operational and administrative requirements, not regional or linguistic considerations. Some Zonal Railways (e.g., Northeast Frontier, North Eastern) are not economically viable. The headquarters of two Zonal Railways (Central and Western) are located in Bombay, and two (Eastern and South Eastern) in Calcutta, due to operational/administrative convenience and historical background. Additionally, five cities (Bombay, Calcutta, Lucknow, Nagpur, Secunderabad) house the headquarters of more than one Division. The Committee found that some Divisional headquarters are located far from commercial and industrial centres, causing hardship to the public.

For the Railway Board's Office, the report noted a significant increase in staff strength, from 651 total staff in 1951 to 2,565 total staff by March 31, 1978. The Ministry attributed this growth to increased developmental activities, the Railways' pivotal role in Five Year Plans, and the implementation of government policies and modern management techniques. The Committee, however, found the increase in staff strength at the Railway Board to be disproportionate to the increase in Railway activities, noting that targets were not achieved during the Fourth Plan period.

What was recommended

  • The entire matter of delegation of powers to General Managers and the exercise of such delegated powers should be remitted to a small team of experts in management and finance, not connected with the Railways, for an in-depth study. The team should submit its report within six months.
  • The Committee should be apprised of the findings of the team of experts on delegation of powers and the action taken thereon at the earliest.
  • When re-organisation of Zonal Railways is necessitated in the future, the Headquarters of a Railway traversing multiple States should, as far as possible, be located in the State with the largest kilometreage of Railway line within that Zone's jurisdiction.
  • The desirability and feasibility of locating Divisional headquarters at more central places, considering the growth of commercial and industrial activities, should be kept in mind when reviewing the set-up in each Zonal Railway to improve service to users.
  • The Divisional Headquarters located in metropolitan cities (Bombay, Calcutta, Lucknow, Nagpur, and Secunderabad) should be shifted to other central locations as soon as conditions permit.
  • In future decisions regarding the location of Divisional Headquarters, it should be ensured that not more than one Divisional Headquarters is located in any one place.
  • The Railway Board should take a comprehensive view of the entire organisation of the Railway Board's Office, including its strength, and undertake a thorough review in consultation with a team of experts in administrative reforms. The team of experts should submit its recommendations within six months.
  • The Committee should be apprised of the findings of the team of experts on the Railway Board's Office organisation and the action taken thereon.

What it means for citizens

The report indicates that ordinary citizens and railway employees frequently experience difficulties in getting their issues resolved promptly at the local or Zonal railway levels, leading them to approach the central authority, the Railway Board, directly. This is evidenced by the thousands of representations received annually by the Railway Board regarding issues such as wagon supply, delays in transit, compensation claims, refunds, and employee grievances.

The Committee's recommendations aim to address these issues by suggesting a re-evaluation of how powers are delegated to railway managers and how the Zonal Railways and the Railway Board's office are organised. If these recommendations are implemented, it could lead to faster decision-making and more efficient resolution of problems at the local and Zonal levels, potentially improving the quality of railway services and the responsiveness of railway authorities to public and employee concerns. The suggestion to relocate some Divisional headquarters to more central, commercially active locations could also make it easier for citizens, traders, and industries to interact with railway officials.
