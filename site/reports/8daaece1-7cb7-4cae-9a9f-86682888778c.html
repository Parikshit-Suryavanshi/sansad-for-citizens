Government's Action on Recommendations for MPLADS Funds for Natural Calamities

Report date: 2013-08-20

Committee: Committee on MPLADS

Lok Sabha: 15

What was discussed

This Twelfth Report of the Committee on Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme (MPLADS) focuses on the action taken by the Government on the recommendations contained in the Committee's Ninth Report (15th Lok Sabha). The subject of the Ninth Report was "Procedures on provision of MPLADS funds for Natural Calamities."

The Ninth Report, which contained 17 observations/recommendations, was presented to Lok Sabha on 9 August, 2012. The Ministry of Home Affairs and the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation submitted their Action Taken Notes on these recommendations on 30 November, 2012, and 23 May, 2013, respectively. This Twelfth Report was considered and adopted by the Committee at its sitting held on 13 August, 2013.

What the report found

The Committee categorized the government's replies to the 17 recommendations from its Ninth Report. It noted that 6 recommendations were accepted by the Government, 4 were not desired to be pursued in view of the Government's replies, 5 were not accepted by the Committee and require reiteration, and final replies for 2 were still awaited.

Regarding MPLADS fund ceilings for natural calamities, the Ministry stated that the current ceilings of Rs. 10 lakh for permissible works outside the constituency but within a State and Rs. 50 lakh for "calamity of severe nature" were "sufficient and appropriate at present." The Committee, however, noted that the Ministry did not provide reasons for this stand, especially since these ceilings have existed since 2005, when the annual MPLADS allocation was Rs. 2 Crore, which has since increased to Rs. 5 Crore.

For the 2001 Gujarat earthquake, the Committee found that out of 305 Anganwadis (a type of rural child care centre) approved for construction, the Government of Gujarat had provided Utilization and Completion Certificates for 239 Anganwadis, costing Rs. 6,11,34,910/-. Information for the remaining 66 Anganwadis was still pending. The report also noted that Rs. 9.42 crore from funds committed for Gujarat earthquake rehabilitation was still available with the Gujarat Government as of 11 August, 2011, and a new proposal for Rs. 1557.96 lakh (Rs. 15.57 crore) for rehabilitation works in earthquake-affected districts was under examination/consideration as of 6 April, 2013. The Committee observed that about Rs. 15 crore remained unutilized even after twelve years.

For the 2008 Kosi floods in Bihar, out of Rs. 44.86 crore (Rs. 22.05 crore from Lok Sabha MPs and Rs. 22.81 crore from Rajya Sabha MPs) committed by Members of Parliament, only Rs. 10.62 crore had been received by the affected districts (Saharsa, Supaul, and Madhepura) as of 8 February, 2013. Land for constructing disaster shelters and cattle shelters had only been identified in many cases, even five years after the calamity. For Cyclone Aila in West Bengal (May, 2009), the Ministry provided information up to August, 2011, stating that 51 out of 81 sanctioned works in North 24 Parganas district and 17 out of 73 sanctioned works in South 24 Parganas district had been completed. The Committee noted that the Ministry's information was not updated.

The Ministry of Home Affairs stated that establishing fixed criteria for declaring a "calamity of severe nature" would reduce flexibility and that assessing damages immediately after some calamities, like floods, is difficult. The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation noted that new provisions (Para 6.2(viii), Para 6.3(xi), Para 6.4(x)) were incorporated into the MPLADS Guidelines via a circular dated 12 March, 2013, to address monitoring and timeframes for rehabilitation works. For the Leh cloudburst (6 August, 2010), 5 out of 8 recommended works were completed as of 16 November, 2012. For the Sikkim earthquake (18 September, 2011), MPs committed Rs. 586 lakh (Rs. 430 lakh by Rajya Sabha MPs and Rs. 156 lakh by Lok Sabha MPs). The State Government sent revised proposals for 8 schools at a total cost of Rs. 402.35 lakh, which were approved by the Rajya Sabha Committee on MPLADS. Proposals for the balance Rs. 183.65 lakh were requested.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry should increase the provision in para 2.7 of the MPLADS guidelines to Rs. 50 lakh to allow Lok Sabha MPs to recommend MPLADS funds anywhere outside their constituency but within the State for rehabilitation works.
  • The Ministry should increase the provision in para 2.8 of the MPLADS guidelines for "calamity of severe nature" to Rs. 1 crore, applicable anywhere in the country.
  • The Ministry should issue the Minister's appeal for MPLADS contributions within one week from the date the Ministry of Home Affairs declares a natural calamity as severe.
  • MPLADS guidelines should stipulate that rehabilitation works in calamity-affected areas be identified by the nodal department within one month of pooling funds.
  • The concerned District Authority should complete these rehabilitation works within eight months, with more time allowed by the nodal department in consultation with the Ministry for works requiring it.
  • The Ministry should closely monitor the progress of these works and take corrective steps for timely completion.
  • The Ministry should periodically review the physical and financial progress of MPLADS funds for calamity rehabilitation and address issues with State Governments.
  • State-level monitoring committees should specifically review the physical and financial progress of MPLADS funds used for rehabilitation works.
  • The nodal department in the State should ensure monthly progress reports on MPLADS fund utilization for rehabilitation works are submitted by affected districts to the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation.
  • Quarterly reviews of physical and financial progress of sanctioned works in calamity-affected areas should be conducted at the Secretary level by the Ministry.
  • These quarterly reviews should use video conferencing with Chief Secretaries/Secretaries of nodal departments and District Authorities of affected States/UTs to address execution issues.
  • Specific reference should be made to NABCONS (NABARD Consultancy Services) to monitor the physical and financial progress of rehabilitation works in calamity-affected areas.
  • Officers of the nodal department should be assigned clear responsibility through guidelines to monitor timely utilization of MPLADS funds by district authorities for rehabilitation works.
  • For the Gujarat earthquake, the Ministry should take immediate steps to complete work on all 305 Anganwadis within 2013.
  • The Ministry should ensure that proposals for utilizing the remaining Rs. 9.42 crore for Gujarat rehabilitation works are approved on time and executed within one year from approval.
  • If MPLADS funds contributed by MPs for rehabilitation works remain unutilized with State Governments beyond a stipulated period from pooling, these funds should be transferred back to the respective MPs' constituencies.
  • The Government should make necessary amendments to MPLADS guidelines for this transfer of unutilized funds.
  • For Kosi floods, the Ministry should immediately reconcile all information regarding recommendation, release, and receipt of funds, and ensure funds are released immediately by nodal districts.
  • MPLADS guidelines should include a provision for immediate release of consented funds for natural calamities by nodal districts upon receiving Ministry information, or within one month.
  • Effective steps should be taken by the Ministry to construct all disaster and cattle shelters for Kosi floods within 2013.
  • For Cyclone Aila in West Bengal, the Ministry should obtain updated progress information and work with the State Government to complete all rehabilitation works before the end of 2013.
  • MPs' consent letters for MPLADS funds for natural calamities should be addressed directly to either the Minister or the Secretary of the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation.
  • The Ministry, as the nodal executing agency, should examine State Government proposals and approve them within two weeks, without referring them to Parliamentary Committees.
  • If required, the Ministry may send a team of officers for on-the-spot assessment of rehabilitation proposals.
  • For all severe natural calamities, the Ministry should submit periodic progress reports of rehabilitation works for the Committee's automatic awareness (suo-moto cognition).
  • Periodic progress reports should also be furnished to the MPs who contributed from their MPLADS funds.

What it means for citizens

This report highlights that funds meant for relief and reconstruction after natural calamities, contributed by Members of Parliament through the MPLADS, have often been significantly delayed or remain unspent. For example, substantial funds for the Gujarat earthquake and Kosi floods were still unutilized years after the events, meaning that essential Anganwadis, disaster shelters, and other rehabilitation projects were not completed on time.

The Committee's recommendations aim to ensure that money allocated for disaster relief reaches affected areas and is used efficiently. If adopted, these changes could lead to quicker approval and release of funds for calamity-affected areas, faster completion of rehabilitation works like shelters and community buildings, and more consistent monitoring by both central and state governments. This could mean that citizens impacted by natural disasters would receive support and rebuilt infrastructure more promptly, and there would be greater accountability from government bodies regarding the use of these crucial funds.
