Railway Convention Committee reviews government action on Autokast factory project in Cherthala

Report date: 2013-05-08

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 15

What was discussed

This Seventh Action Taken Report of the Railway Convention Committee (2009) examines the government's actions on recommendations outlined in the Committee's Fourth Report (Fifteenth Lok Sabha). The Fourth Report, presented on 14th August, 2012, focused on "Pending on-going Projects of the Ministry of Railways – Commissioning of the Autokast Factory at Cherthala, Kerala - A Case Study."

The core issue discussed was the delayed implementation of a proposed Joint Venture between the Ministry of Railways and the Government of Kerala. This venture aimed to establish a facility at Autokast and Steel Fabrication Units (SFU) in Cherthala, Kerala, for manufacturing wagon and coaching components for the Railways. The Committee considered and adopted this Seventh Report at its sitting on 7th May, 2013.

An initial proposal for this Joint Venture was put forward in 2007. Following a RITES study in 2007, a budget provision of Rs. 10 lakh was allocated in the Railway Budget for 2007-08, and a shareholders' agreement was signed in 2009. However, the project's execution has faced significant delays and complications, including a policy shift by the Railways from conventional ICF coaches (Integral Coach Factory coaches, a conventional type of railway passenger coach) to modern LHB coaches (Linke Hofmann Busch coaches, a modern type of railway passenger coach), which impacted the initially planned product mix for the manufacturing units.

What the report found

The report categorised the government's action taken replies to the 5 recommendations from the Fourth Report. Two recommendations (Para Nos. 36 & 40) were accepted by the Government, while three recommendations (Para Nos. 37, 38 & 39) had government replies that were not accepted by the Committee and required reiteration.

The Committee found that the Ministry of Railways had not shown sufficient enthusiasm in implementing the proposed Joint Venture for component manufacturing at Autokast and Steel Fabrication Units (SFU) in Cherthala, Kerala. It noted that despite a budget provision in 2007-08 and a shareholders' agreement signed in 2009, the project remained unfulfilled after five years. The Committee also found it difficult to believe that the Railways' modernization plan to shift from ICF bogie frames to LHB bogie frames was not under consideration in 2009 when the agreement for ICF design components was signed, especially since LHB coaches were introduced in December, 2003. This indicated a lack of coordination within the Railways, causing "inordinate delay".

The report observed that as a follow-up to the 2009 shareholders' agreement, the Government of Kerala had already delinked Autokast Ltd. and SFU from Steel Industrial Kerala Limited (SILK), halted their production activities, and shifted their liabilities to SILK. This resulted in audit objections regarding losses for SILK and employees receiving salaries without work. The Committee questioned the need for a second RITES study in 2011, just two years after the first in 2007, for the same project. It viewed this as a duplication of efforts, time, and financial burden, also reflecting on the competency of RITES, as the findings of the second study were contrary to the first. The Ministry of Railways did not respond to the Committee's query on why necessary inputs were not provided to RITES during its first pre-feasibility study for the Autokast Project.

The 2011 RITES Report recommended proper investment in the Autokast unit for wagon castings manufacture and that the Railways commit to an assured off-take for 5 years for annual quantities of 3000 CASNUB Bogies (a type of undercarriage for railway wagons) and 2000 Couplers (devices used to connect railway rolling stock). The Committee noted that the Government of Kerala had already fulfilled its commitments, such as providing land and delinking the units. Despite this, the Ministry of Railways decided on 1st November, 2012, to continue with an earlier decision of providing an assured off-take for 5 years, instead of forming a Joint Venture. The Committee found this reply unclear regarding the mode of operation and the basis of the assured off-take, expressing apprehension that this commitment might also be unhonoured, similar to the initial Memorandum of Understanding (MoU).

What was recommended

  • The Railways should bring out the revised business plan as announced by the Minister of Railways and implement it at the earliest, and the Committee would like to be apprised of this business plan and its implementation.
  • The Committee desired to be apprised as to why necessary inputs were not provided to RITES (during the first RITES study) at the time of the pre-feasibility study for the Autokast Project, and requested this information within three months of the report's presentation.
  • The Railways should work out the assured off-take for at least 15-20 years instead of 5 years.
  • The Ministry of Railways should honour its proposal of assured off-take and not leave it midway, and should apprise the Committee with clarity about the mode of operation and the basis of the assured off-take.
  • The Railways should review its ongoing projects based on parameters such as project planning/implementation, technology upgradation/absorption, optimal utilization of funds, incorporation of latest technology/quality concepts, fixing and following timelines, and establishing a system for fixing responsibilities for delays, non-performance, and ad-hoc decisions, and should apprise the Committee accordingly within 3 months.

What it means for citizens

This report highlights significant delays in a railway component manufacturing project intended for the Autokast factory in Cherthala, Kerala. For citizens, this means that a planned "productive infrastructure unit" has not yet materialized, resulting in unutilized potential for local job creation and economic contribution. The Government of Kerala had already taken steps like separating units and shifting financial responsibilities, which led to employees receiving salaries without active work, indicating an inefficient use of resources and public funds.

Furthermore, the report raises concerns about accountability and the prudent use of taxpayer money, citing issues such as repeated studies by RITES and unclear commitments from the Ministry of Railways. The lack of clarity on a long-term "assured off-take" plan creates ongoing uncertainty about the future viability of the Autokast unit. Citizens are directly impacted by the non-delivery of a project that could have provided stable employment and contributed to the national railway infrastructure, emphasizing the need for transparent decision-making and timely execution of government agreements.
