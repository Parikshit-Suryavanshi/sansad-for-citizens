Parliament Committee Recommends 6.5% Dividend Rate for Railways for 2004-05

Report date: 2004-12-21

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 14

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee examined the rate of dividend payable by the Railway Undertaking to General Revenues and other related financial matters for the financial year 2004-05. This report is the first on this subject from the Railway Convention Committee (2004).

The separation of Railway Finances from General Finance was established by a Resolution of the Central Legislature on September 20, 1924, to ensure stability for Civil estimates through an assured contribution from Railway Revenues and to provide flexibility in Railway Finance administration. Under this "Separation Convention," Railways are required to pay a dividend at a fixed rate on capital advanced by the Government of India, and these rates are determined periodically by the Railway Convention Committee.

The Railway Convention Committee (1999) had previously recommended, as an interim measure in their Eighth Report presented on July 24, 2003, that the dividend for 2003-04 be paid at 7% to General Revenues. For 2004-05, an interim Memorandum containing views from both the Ministry of Railways and the Ministry of Finance was furnished by the Ministry of Railways on December 26, 2003. The Ministry of Finance suggested continuing existing rates and modalities, while the Ministry of Railways proposed reducing the dividend rate to 6%. The Committee heard evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Finance on October 29, 2004, and from the Ministry of Railways on November 16, 2004.

What the report found

The first Convention Committee, established in April 1949, had fixed a definite dividend rate that included a contribution to General Revenues beyond the bare interest paid by the Government on capital provided for Railways, based on the principle that the general taxpayer is the owner. This approach of a dividend rate higher than the average government borrowing rate continued until 1981-82, after which it was set lower due to the financial health of the Railways.

The Capital-at-charge of the Indian Railways increased from Rs.827 crore in 1950-51 to Rs.45701.91 crore in 2003-04 (Revised Estimates) and Rs.50161.13 crore in 2004-05 (Budget Estimates). The Railways have paid a total of Rs.36709 crore as dividend to General Revenues, which is 80.38% of the Capital-at-charge. Dividend payments of Rs.1823 crore in 2000-01 and Rs.1000 crore in 2001-02 were deferred, totaling Rs.2823 crore outstanding by the end of 2001-02. The Ministry of Railways repaid Rs.300 crore by the end of 2003-04.

The Ministry of Railways reported signs of financial improvement, including the repayment of a Rs.249 crore loan in 2001-02 and clearing Rs.50 crore of deferred dividend liability in 2002-03. However, they noted difficulties in increasing earnings due to competition from the road sector and a need for over Rs.30000 crore for modernization, development, expansion, and safety-related renewals. Outstanding payments from railway users, particularly State Electricity Boards, amounted to approximately Rs.2979 crore as of September 30, 2004. The Railways also faced a liability of Rs.2773 crore from deferred dividends and financial assistance to the Konkan Railway Corporation (KRCL), lending KRCL Rs.1950 crore as of March 31, 2004. For 2003-04, the Railways experienced a shortfall of Rs.603 crore in overall earnings compared to the budget target.

The Ministry of Finance stated that the weighted average cost of all outstanding Government debt was 10.84%, and considering various subsidies and concessions, the effective dividend rate paid by Railways was about 3.8%. They also noted that budgetary support for Railways' capital expenditure increased by 300% from Rs.2788 crore in 1999-2000 to an estimated Rs.8457 crore in the current year. The Ministry of Finance suggested that Railways index fares and freight rates to staff and fuel costs to improve financial performance, a suggestion not yet accepted by the Ministry of Railways.

For the Depreciation Reserve Fund (DRF), Rs.2976 crore was appropriated in 2003-04, and Rs.2267 crore (from Revenues) and Rs.100 crore (from Production Units) were estimated for 2004-05 (Budget Estimates). The estimated balance in DRF by the end of 2004-05 was Rs.2587 crore. For the Pension Fund (PF), Rs.6500 crore was appropriated in 2003-04, and Rs.6300 crore (from Revenues) and Rs.100 crore (from Production Units) were estimated for 2004-05 (Budget Estimates). The estimated balance in PF by the end of 2004-05 was Rs.1262 crore, with a likely pension liability of Rs.6400 crore for the year. For the Development Fund (DF), the appropriation for 2004-05 (Budget Estimates) was Rs.715 crore. The Capital Fund (CF) was not proposed to be operated for 2004-05 due to insufficient internal resources.

The Railway Safety Fund (RSF), created on April 1, 2001, is financed by transfers from the Central Road Fund (from diesel cess) and a contribution from the dividend. Rs.436 crore was appropriated to RSF in 2003-04, and Rs.404 crore for 2004-05 (Budget Estimates). The Special Railway Safety Fund (SRSF), set up on October 1, 2001, to address arrears in vital safety equipment with an assessed cost of Rs.17000 crore, is funded by a Railways' safety surcharge on passenger traffic (expected Rs.5000 crore over 6 years) and additional financial assistance from the Ministry of Finance (Rs.12000 crore). In 2003-04, Rs.2594 crore was appropriated to SRSF, and for 2004-05 (Budget Estimates), Rs.2933 crore.

The Udhampur-Srinagar-Baramullah New Line Project, costing about Rs.3600 crore, is financed by budgetary support outside the Railway Plan and is exempt from dividend payment. Additional budgetary support of Rs.350 crore and Rs.500 crore was provided in 2002-03 and 2003-04 (Budget Estimates) respectively. For the Mumbai Urban Transport Project (MUTP), a joint venture project estimated at Rs.3125 crore, a MUTP surcharge was imposed from September 15, 2003, on Mumbai suburban rail users. The revenue from this surcharge is shared 50:50 between the Ministry of Railways and the Government of Maharashtra, with the Railways' share going to the Ministry of Finance for World Bank loan repayment. A proposal for a "Loan Repayment Reserve Fund – Mumbai Suburban Rail System" has been sent to the Ministry of Finance.

The Ministry of Finance agreed to the proposal that dividend on the capital-at-charge of new lines may be computed at the least of the rate of dividend or average borrowing rate charged to commercial departments.

What was recommended

  • For the year 2004-05, the dividend to General Revenues should be paid at 6.5% on the entire capital invested on Railways from the General Revenues, irrespective of the year of investment. This rate includes the amount payable to States as grants in lieu of passenger fare tax and contribution for assisting States for safety works during 2003-04.
  • All other concessions currently available on residential buildings, new lines, and subsidies from General Revenues should continue on the existing basis for the year 2004-05.
  • The Ministry of Railways should, from now on, provide information to the Committee in monetary terms about these concessions, categorized by head.
  • Contribution to the Depreciation Reserve Fund (DRF) should be allowed in line with the system's capacity to generate internal resources.
  • The Committee desires to be informed of the actual amount contributed to the DRF in the fiscal year 2004-05.
  • The appropriation to the Pension Fund (PF) from revenues should be kept at Rs.6300 crore in 2004-05, subject to adjustments for any variations in estimated withdrawals during the year. This amount, combined with Rs.100 crore from Production Units, will enable the Railways to meet the pension requirement of Rs.6400 crore for the year.
  • The proposal to appropriate Rs.715 crore (Budget Estimates) to the Development Fund (DF) from the excess left after meeting the dividend liability for 2004-05 is approved.
  • The Ministry of Railways should inform the Committee in their action taken notes about the actual amount credited to the Development Fund at the end of the financial year 2004-05.
  • The Capital Fund (CF) should not be operated for the present, as the Railways cannot generate enough internal resources for appropriation to this fund.
  • The dividend on the capital-at-charge of new lines may be computed at the least of the rate of dividend or the average borrowing rate charged to the commercial departments.
  • The Ministry of Railways should inform the Committee about the exact monetary benefit expected to accrue to Railways from this change, acknowledging that the extent of future benefits will depend on the capital-at-charge of new lines completing the moratorium period.
  • The Committee reserves its views on the creation of a "Loan Repayment Reserve Fund – Mumbai Suburban Rail System" for the Mumbai Urban Transport Project (MUTP) and requests comments from the Ministry of Finance on this matter.
  • The existing principle governing interest on various Railway Fund balances placed in the Public Accounts of India should continue for the year 2004-05.
  • The balances in the Railway Safety Fund and the Special Railway Safety Fund should be treated as interest-free, as both funds receive contributions from the General Revenue.

What it means for citizens

The report's recommendations directly relate to how Indian Railways manages its finances, which can influence railway services and future development. The Committee recommended that the dividend paid by the Railways to the central government for the financial year 2004-05 be 6.5% of the capital invested. This dividend rate affects the amount of money the Railways contribute to the general government budget and also the funds available for the Railways' own operations.

The report also discusses how the Railways manage various funds dedicated to specific purposes, such as the Depreciation Reserve Fund for asset replacement, the Pension Fund for pension payments, and the Railway Safety Fund and Special Railway Safety Fund for safety improvements. The Committee's recommendations on these funds, including the decision to keep the Capital Fund inoperative due to insufficient resources, indicate how much money is allocated for maintaining infrastructure, ensuring safety, and providing pensions.

Additionally, the report addresses the financing of new railway lines and major projects like the Mumbai Urban Transport Project (MUTP). The recommendation for calculating dividend on new lines at a potentially lower rate could make it easier to fund new rail connectivity. For Mumbai citizens, the MUTP surcharge imposed from September 15, 2003, directly contributes to funding urban rail infrastructure upgrades aimed at increasing transport capacity and reducing overcrowding. The report highlights that financial challenges, such as outstanding payments from railway users, particularly State Electricity Boards, and the costs of social service obligations, impact the Railways' ability to generate internal resources for these vital services.
