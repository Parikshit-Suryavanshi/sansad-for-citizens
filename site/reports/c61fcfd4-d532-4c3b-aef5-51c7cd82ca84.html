Railway Convention Committee's Tenth Report on Railway Dividend Rates and Related Matters for 1988-89

Report date: 1988-02-23

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 08

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee (1985) was constituted on May 21, 1985, following a Resolution adopted by Lok Sabha on March 20, 1985, and concurred in by Rajya Sabha on March 28, 1985. Its purpose is to review the rate of dividend, which is a payment from the Railway Undertaking to the General Revenues (the central government's main funds), and other related financial matters concerning Railway Finance.

The Committee received a third interim memorandum from the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) regarding the dividend payable by Railways to the General Revenues, contributions to the Railway Depreciation Reserve Fund (a fund for replacing old assets), and the Railway Pension Fund (a fund for paying pensions to retired railway employees) for the financial year 1988-89. The Committee considered this memorandum on February 15, 1988, and adopted this report on the same day. Previously, the Committee had made recommendations for the financial year 1987-88 in its Seventh Report, which was presented to Parliament on February 24, 1987, and approved by Lok Sabha on March 1, 1987.

The Ministry of Railways had also submitted two memoranda with proposals for dividend payment for the entire five-year period from 1985-86 to 1989-90, which the Committee plans to consider for its final report. In the interim, the Ministry proposed applying the recommendations used for the 1987-88 budget to the 1988-89 budget as well.

What the report found

The Committee noted that out of 31 new railway lines that receive financial relief (exemption from dividend liability), 18 lines showed positive returns (profits) in the financial years 1984-85 and 1985-86. The Railway Reforms Committee had observed that capital investment in all new lines should not attract dividend liability, but if these lines show a surplus (profit) based on the marginal cost principle (a method of assessing profitability), this surplus should be transferred to General Revenues up to the normal dividend level for that year. The Committee agrees with this observation.

Regarding the restoration of the "element of contribution" in the dividend rate, which the Committee had asked about in its Seventh Report, the Ministry of Railways only stated that the recommendation had been 'noted'. The report highlighted that the Ministry of Railways established the Indian Railway Finance Corporation Ltd. in December 1986 to raise funds through public borrowing. This corporation collected Rs. 560 crores in March 1987 at interest rates of 10% for Rs. 250 crores and 9% for the remaining Rs. 310 crores. A second issue of bonds floated in January 1988 carried an interest rate of 9%.

What was recommended

  • The dividend to General Revenues and subsidy (financial support) from General Revenues to the Railways for the year 1988-89 should be calculated at 6% on capital invested up to March 31, 1980. This 6% includes 1.5% on capital invested up to March 31, 1964 (minus capital qualifying for subsidy) for payments to States in place of Passenger Fare Tax and for assisting States in financing Safety Works. For capital invested after March 31, 1980, the rate should be 6.5%.
  • The balances in the various Railway Reserve Funds (excluding the Development Fund) should earn interest at the same rate as the dividend. The interest rate on the balance in the Development Fund should be the same as the interest rate on loans from General Revenues for Development Fund Works for the 1988-89 Budget Estimate.
  • The contribution from Railway Revenue to the Depreciation Reserve Fund should be increased to Rs. 1500 crores in 1988-89.
  • The contribution to the Pension Fund, which was Rs. 450 crores in 1987-88, should be suitably increased in 1988-89. This increase should account for the growing number of Railway pensioners and reliefs granted over time, including those sanctioned based on the Fourth Pay Commission's recommendations for pensioners.
  • The recommendation by the Railway Reforms Committee should be implemented. This means that if new railway lines, after opening for traffic, show a surplus (profit) based on the marginal cost principle, this surplus should be transferred to General Revenues up to the level of the normal dividend for that year.
  • Given that the Ministry of Railways raises funds through public borrowings at higher interest rates (e.g., 10% and 9%), the Government should carefully consider and include a suitable "element of contribution" in the rate of dividend paid by Railways to General Revenues. This is to compensate the General Revenues for the higher cost of public borrowing if railway earnings permit.

What it means for citizens

For ordinary citizens, these recommendations mean changes in how Indian Railways' finances are managed, potentially impacting public services and government funds. The Committee's recommendations on dividend payments determine how much of the Railways' earnings contribute to the central government's overall funds (General Revenues), which are used to finance various public services. Conversely, subsidies from General Revenues provide financial support to specific railway operations.

The recommendation to increase the contribution to the Depreciation Reserve Fund to Rs. 1500 crores for 1988-89 implies that more money will be set aside for replacing and renewing railway infrastructure and equipment. This could lead to better-maintained trains and tracks, potentially improving safety and reliability for passengers and freight services. Additionally, enhancing the contribution to the Pension Fund means that financial provisions for retired railway employees will be strengthened, helping ensure the payment of their pensions, especially considering the increasing number of pensioners and various relief measures. The implementation of the recommendation for new railway lines that show a profit on the marginal cost principle to contribute to General Revenues means that these profitable lines would financially support the broader government finances.
