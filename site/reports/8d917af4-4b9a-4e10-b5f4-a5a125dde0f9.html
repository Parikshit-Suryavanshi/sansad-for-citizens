Committee Reports on OBC Representation in Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL) and Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL)

Report date: 2023-12-18

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

The Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes examined measures undertaken to secure representation and welfare of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment within the Ministry of Steel, Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL), and Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL). The Committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Steel, SAIL, and RINL on October 5, 2023.

The discussion covered the status of OBC reservation, their representation in different cadres, and steps taken for their welfare in these organizations. The Committee adopted its Twenty-Seventh Report at a sitting held on December 5, 2023.

What the report found

The report found that in the Ministry of Steel, as of August 31, 2023, the total staff in position was 184 out of a sanctioned strength of 245. Out of these, 45 employees were from the OBC category, making up 24.45% of the total. OBC representation was 17.77% in Group ‘A’, 31% in Group ‘B’, and 21.53% in Group ‘C’. Initially, the Liaison Officer for OBCs in the Ministry belonged to the Scheduled Tribe (ST) category, but an OBC Deputy Secretary was appointed to the role on October 10, 2023. The Ministry stated that no complaints from OBC employees were received in the last two years.

In Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL), the total existing strength was 57,669 employees, with 9,508 (16.48%) belonging to OBCs. In Group ‘A’ (Executives), OBC representation was 18.41%, and in Group ‘B’ and ‘C’ (Non-Executives), it was 16.05%. The report noted a backlog of 76 OBC posts in Group ‘A’ and 19 in Group ‘C’ as of September 1, 2023, attributed to the non-availability of suitable candidates or selected candidates not joining. SAIL has not conducted any special recruitment drives in the last five years but includes backlog vacancies in subsequent recruitment advertisements. Out of 15 SAIL units, 13 have OBC Liaison Officers from the OBC category. SAIL had 68,792 contractual workers as of September 1, 2023, whose caste details are not maintained.

In Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL), as of September 1, 2023, there were 2,967 OBC employees out of a total of 14,051, constituting 21.08% of the workforce. OBC representation was 20.37% in the Executive cadre and 21.43% in the Non-Executive cadre. There were no OBC backlog vacancies identified in RINL. No recruitment or special recruitment drives have occurred in RINL in the last three years. An OBC Liaison Officer has been appointed from the OBC category. One complaint from an OBC employee was received on October 6, 2022, and disposed of on July 6, 2023. RINL had 15,774 contractual workers as of August 31, 2023.

For both SAIL and RINL, the report noted that there is no provision for reservation in the appointment of Directors to their respective Boards. The statutory Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) budget for RINL was nil for FY 2022-23 and FY 2023-24 due to the company's financial position.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry of Steel should be apprised every six months, and in Action Taken Notes, about steps taken to fill OBC vacancies until the mandated 27% representation is achieved.
  • A separate list should be maintained of OBC candidates who qualify on their own merit in each Group of service in SAIL, without availing reservation benefits.
  • Backlog vacancies for OBCs in SAIL should be advertised separately with wide publicity in the press and recruitment sites to attract suitable candidates.
  • Campus recruitment should be organized by SAIL with premier educational institutions for specialized cadres to fill OBC backlog vacancies, potentially inducting trainees/apprentices before regularizing their services.
  • Names, addresses, contact numbers, and email IDs of all Liaison Officers of each plant/unit in SAIL should be uploaded on the organization’s intranet/website.
  • Roster Registers, duly authenticated by Liaison Officers, should be uploaded on the intranet/website of SAIL to enhance transparency and inform employees.
  • SAIL should maintain a separate complaint register and data for complaints lodged by OBC employees and upload this information on its intranet/website.
  • Minutes of discussions on issues and their resolutions from bi-partite committees in SAIL should be prepared, maintained, and uploaded on the intranet/website.
  • SAIL should encourage OBC employees in its other units/plants to form OBC Welfare Associations.
  • Efforts should be made to ensure that more persons/officials from the OBC category are appointed/nominated to the Board of Directors in both SAIL and RINL, especially for cases that fit the prescribed criteria, drawing attention to DPE OM No. 2(15)/2011 GM dated April 18, 2011.
  • The Ministry should take up the matter at the highest level to address the low representation of OBC employees in RINL and make concerted efforts to implement the reservation policy scrupulously to reach the mandated 27% OBC reservation in both Executive and Non-Executive posts in a time-bound manner.
  • Roster Registers, duly authenticated by Liaison Officers, should be uploaded on the intranet/website of RINL.
  • Name, address, contact number, and email of the Liaison Officer in RINL should be uploaded on its intranet/website.
  • A separate Grievance Redressal Mechanism should be set up for OBC employees in RINL, with proper records of grievances and their redressal maintained and uploaded on the intranet/website.
  • RINL should encourage contract agencies/contractors to voluntarily give preference to persons belonging to Other Backward Classes when employing contract labour.

What it means for citizens

The report outlines efforts and calls for further action to ensure that Indian citizens belonging to Other Backward Classes (OBCs) receive their due representation in employment within the Ministry of Steel and two major public sector undertakings, Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL) and Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL). It highlights that the mandated 27% reservation for OBCs is not yet fully met across various employment groups in these organizations, affecting employment opportunities for OBC individuals. The recommendations aim to enhance transparency in recruitment and grievance redressal processes, such as publishing roster registers and contact details of Liaison Officers online, making it easier for OBC employees and job seekers to access information and support.

Furthermore, the report suggests improving strategies for filling vacant OBC positions, including specialized recruitment drives and campus recruitment, which could create more employment avenues for OBC individuals seeking roles in the steel sector. The emphasis on strengthening OBC welfare associations and separate grievance mechanisms is intended to provide dedicated platforms for OBC employees to voice their concerns and ensure their welfare, contributing to a more inclusive workplace environment. The report also touches upon fair treatment for contractual workers, many of whom are from backward communities, by ensuring they receive minimum wages and basic welfare facilities, though it notes that mandating OBC engagement for contractors is not currently possible.
