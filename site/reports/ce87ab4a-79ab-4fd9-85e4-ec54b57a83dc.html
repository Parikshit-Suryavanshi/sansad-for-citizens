Evaluation of Indian Railways' Traffic Share Compared to Road and Air Transport

Report date: 2016-08-11

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee examined the share of Indian Railways' traffic relative to road and air transport. The committee aimed to evaluate trends, reasons for changes in traffic share, and measures taken or proposed by the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board).

To gather information, the Committee obtained background material and written documents from the Ministry of Railways. The Committee also took oral evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) on January 8, 2016, and June 1, 2016. The report was considered and adopted by the Committee at its sitting held on August 8, 2016.

Key areas discussed included passenger traffic and fares, freight traffic and tariffs, investment and capacity building, loading and unloading detention times, network congestion, and Dedicated Freight Corridors (DFCs).

What the report found

The report found that the Indian Railways' share of passenger traffic declined from 74.3% in 1951 to 12.9% in 2004-05. More recently, the passenger movement percentage decreased from 19.0% in 1999-2000 to 14.1% in 2011-12. The actual number of passengers also showed a decline from 8421 million in 2012-13 to 8151.90 million in 2015-16. Reasons cited included capacity constraints, improved road infrastructure, growth of metro rail projects, and train cancellations.

The Indian Railways incurs significant losses in passenger services, approximately Rs. 28,000 crore, because passenger fare increases (which grew at a Compound Annual Growth Rate, or CAGR, of 2.87% from 2004-05 to 2013-14) have been lower than cost growth. This loss is cross-subsidized by freight business, making rail freight less competitive. The Ministry stated that China charges 2.7 times, Germany 6.2 times, and Japan 9.4 times the passenger fares of Indian Railways, but also noted that India's socio-economic conditions are not comparable. A dynamic fare system for passenger traffic was introduced from December 24, 2013, with Premium trains, and later with Suvidha trains from July 1, 2015, but the Chairman, Railway Board, stated that currently, they do not have dynamic fares which they had for Suvidha and earlier versions of such trains.

For freight traffic, the Indian Railways' share (in originating tonnage) decreased from 89% in 1951 to 30% in 2007-08. It further declined from 39.5% in 1999-2000 to 35.5% in 2011-12. The exact percentage share for freight movement from 2012-13 onwards was not available with the Ministry. Contributing factors to this decline include high freight rates (which grew at a CAGR of 6.54% from 2004-05 to 2013-14 and are "almost the highest in the world"), under-investment in capacity, a focus on a narrow basket of nine bulk commodities (with coal constituting 46%), and a compulsion to accept only 'train load' consignments.

Slow growth in network capacity (Total Route kilometers grew by 1.23 times between 1950-51 and 2013-14) has led to increasing congestion, particularly on arterial trunk routes. This congestion prevents the Railways from guaranteeing delivery times.

In 2015-16, the loading detention time for freight was 16.08 hours and unloading detention time was 18.07 hours, which the Ministry admitted was still high. Demurrage charges collected from 2010-11 to 2014-15 totaled Rs. 5652.92 crore. The wagon turn-around performance improved from 4.97 days in 2010-11 to 4.98 days in 2014-15.

The Railways have drawn an investment plan of Rs. 8.56 lakh crore (approximately 130 Billion US dollar) over the next five years, to be financed through Gross Budgetary Support (Rs. 2.56 lakh crore), Internal Generation (Rs. 1.0 lakh crore), State Joint Ventures (Rs. 1.2 lakh crore), Public-Private Partnership (PPP) (Rs. 1.3 lakh crore), and Extra Budgetary Resources (EBR) (Rs. 2.5 lakh crore). The Ministry stated that Railways' capacity to generate adequate internal resources is limited due to huge Public Service Obligations and inflexibility of expenditure. Two Dedicated Freight Corridors (DFCs), Eastern and Western, spanning around 3300 km, are under implementation and are likely to be completed by December 2019. Three more DFCs (East-West Corridor, North-South Corridor, East Coast Corridor) are proposed.

What was recommended

  • More effective and innovative steps should be taken, with continuous monitoring at all levels, to retain and attract more passenger traffic despite existing constraints.
  • The Ministry of Railway should look into the feasibility of gradually increasing passenger fares based on feedback from stakeholders and public opinion, and after a comprehensive re-evaluation of social commitments, to reduce cross-subsidy and utilize additional revenue for passenger amenities, while not causing undue hardship on non-premium segments.
  • Concerted efforts should be made to put in place a system of rationalized variable fare structure to compete with airline pricing and improve Railway earnings, while protecting the overall interest of the travelling public and guarding against pilferage/leakage of revenue in passenger services.
  • Systemic deficiencies and contradictions need to be addressed to enable the Railways to meet both social commitments and commercial targets.
  • The Ministry should assess Railways’ share in freight movement from 2012-13 onwards to enable timely measures for improving performance in freight traffic.
  • Government should intensify and focus on multi-pronged strategies to make Indian Railways’ freight movement more competitive, better, and faster, aiming to stabilize its share in the short run and reverse the declining trend in the long run.
  • The Ministry should further intensify efforts to raise requisite funds through market borrowings by IRFC, Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs), Joint Ventures, and internal generation to support the ambitious investment plan of Rs. 8.56 lakh crore.
  • The Ministry should continue to focus on rigorous monitoring of expenditure, gainful utilization of existing assets, minimizing operating expenses, and checking leakages to maximize internal generation of funds for supplementing plan investments.
  • The Railways should continue with initiated measures and contemplate additional steps to attract non-bulk piecemeal traffic and sustain competition from the road sector in small distance and door-to-door delivery capabilities.
  • Measures to reduce loading and unloading detention time should be further streamlined and the monitoring mechanism tightened to the barest minimum, to win customer trust and earn more revenue.
  • The free time prescribed for wagon detention should be further tightened to improve wagon turn-around performance and motivate customers to invest in more efficient handling systems.
  • The Ministry should expedite sanctioned projects for doubling, expansion, and traffic facility works to effectively tackle congestion and assure a delivery time to dedicated and prospective rail freight consumers.
  • The Ministry should ensure the completion of under-implementation and proposed Dedicated Freight Corridors (DFCs) within targeted timelines, as they will augment carrying capacity and positively impact revenue generation.

What it means for citizens

This report suggests that ordinary people may experience changes in train travel and the movement of goods. The committee recommended potentially gradual increases in passenger fares, which could mean higher ticket prices for citizens, particularly if additional revenue is used for improving passenger amenities. Efforts to improve passenger services, such as adding coaches and special trains, along with various ticket booking options like Automatic Ticket Vending Machines (ATVMs) and mobile ticketing, are intended to make travel more convenient.

For the movement of goods, the report highlights the need for Indian Railways to become more competitive. If recommendations for improving freight services are implemented, it could lead to faster and more reliable transportation of goods, which might indirectly affect the availability and cost of products in the market. Plans to address network congestion and reduce the time taken to load and unload goods could result in more predictable delivery times for businesses, potentially benefiting consumers. The completion of major infrastructure projects like Dedicated Freight Corridors (DFCs) could expand the rail network and lead to improved services for both passengers and freight across the country.
