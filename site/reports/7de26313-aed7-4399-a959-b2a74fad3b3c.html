Report on Inquiry into Improper Conduct of a Member Regarding Parliament House Estate Security

Report date: 2016-12-08

Committee: Committee to Inquire into the Improper Conduct of a Member

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

The Committee to Inquire into the Improper Conduct of a Member examined the improper conduct of Shri Bhagwant Mann, MP, concerning the unauthorized video recording of the security arrangements of the Parliament House Estate. On 21 July, 2016, Shri Mann reportedly recorded his entry into Parliament House, showing security arrangements and explaining their operation, and live-streamed it on social media. Following this, on 22 July, 2016, several Members of Lok Sabha gave notices of question of privilege against Shri Mann, alleging a breach of Parliament security, exposure of security arrangements, and conduct unbecoming of a Member. Shri Bhartruhari Mahtab, MP, also alleged violation of Rule 334A and Direction 124A by Shri Mann for giving prior publicity to his Zero Hour notice.

The Adhoc Committee was constituted by the Speaker, Lok Sabha, on 25 July, 2016, to inquire into the security implications of this incident, suggest remedial measures, and recommend appropriate action. The Committee held 13 sittings, starting from 25 July, 2016, and received extensions of time from the Speaker to submit its report. During its deliberations, the Committee took evidence from Shri Bhagwant Mann, MP, Commissioner of Police, Delhi (Shri Alok Kumar Verma), Joint Secretary (Security), Lok Sabha Secretariat (Shri Yogesh Deshmukh), Additional Director (Security) (Shri G.S. Guleria), Parliamentary Security Staff, and Deputy Secretary of the Parliamentary Notice Office (Shri C. Babu Rao). The Committee also took evidence from Shri Vikram Srivastava, IPS (Retd.), a security expert, on 10 November, 2016, regarding the likely damage caused by the incident. On 4 August, 2016, the Committee undertook an on-the-spot inspection of the route taken by Shri Mann on 21 July, 2016.

What the report found

The report found that on 21 July, 2016, Shri Bhagwant Mann, MP, live-streamed a video on Facebook showing his entry into the Parliament House, including security arrangements and a commentary on their operation and regulation of vehicle entry. On 22 July, 2016, Shri Mann submitted a written unconditional apology to the Speaker, stating his purpose was to make people aware of the procedure for asking questions and that the act happened unknowingly. The Speaker, on 25 July, 2016, constituted a nine-member Inquiry Committee and advised Shri Mann not to attend House sittings until a decision was taken.

During his evidence, Shri Mann reiterated his unconditional apology and stated his intention was to make an educational video for his constituents, not to breach security. He claimed he was not aware that prior permission was needed for videography and had not seen any prohibitory boards. Security officials, including Additional Director (Security) Shri G.S. Guleria and Security Officers Shri Krishan Kumar and Shri Rajnish Kumar Rana, stated they did not notice Shri Mann videographing or did not have specific instructions to stop an MP from doing so. Shri C. Babu Rao, Deputy Secretary, Parliamentary Notice Office, stated he requested Shri Mann not to take photographs, and Shri Mann turned his camera, but Shri Rao later learned the videography continued. Joint Secretary (Security) Shri Yogesh Deshmukh stated that such a video could be misused by terror organizations and noted the absence of specific videography instructions for MPs in the "Blue Book" (security instructions). He also mentioned that the Parliament's security systems required urgent upgradation. Shri Mann later partially withdrew earlier submissions to the Committee from 28 July, 2016, and 01 August, 2016, which contained extraneous facts and references, and affirmed his unconditional apology as his final submission on 28 November, 2016.

What it means for citizens

The report details the parliamentary process of investigating the conduct of a Member of Parliament regarding potential security breaches. For citizens, this indicates the seriousness with which Parliament views actions that could compromise the security of the Parliament House Estate, which is described as the "sanctum sanctorum of democracy." The incident prompted a detailed inquiry involving various security agencies and experts, highlighting a review of existing security protocols and the need for possible enhancements, such as issuing strict prohibitions on videography to Members of Parliament. It also suggests that improvements to security systems, including technological upgrades like automatic number plate reading and tamper-proof Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) cards, and clarification of instructions for security personnel regarding the conduct of MPs, might be considered. The Speaker's action of advising the concerned Member not to attend House sittings until a decision is taken illustrates the Parliament's commitment to addressing such matters thoroughly.
