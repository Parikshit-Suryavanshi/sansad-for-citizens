Review of Government Action on OBC Representation and Welfare in Airports Authority of India

Report date: 2023-08-04

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

This Twenty-Fourth Report, presented by the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes, reviews the action taken by the Government on observations and recommendations from the Committee's Seventeenth Report. The Seventeenth Report focused on 'Measures undertaken to secure representation of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment and for their welfare in Airports Authority of India (AAI)', and pertained to the Ministry of Civil Aviation.

The Seventeenth Report (Seventeenth Lok Sabha) was presented to Lok Sabha and laid on the Table of Rajya Sabha on 21st December, 2022. The Government's replies to all 7 observations/recommendations contained in that report were received on 06th March, 2023.

The Committee examined these replies and considered and adopted this Draft Action Taken Report at its sitting held on 02nd August, 2023.

What the report found

Out of 7 observations/recommendations in the Seventeenth Report, 5 (Sl. Nos. 2, 4, 5, 6, and 7) were accepted by the Government, representing 71.42%. The Committee did not desire to pursue any (0%) of the recommendations. Replies for 2 recommendations (Sl. Nos. 1 and 3), representing 28.57%, were not accepted by the Committee and require reiteration. No final replies were still awaited.

Regarding the representation of OBCs in the AAI Board of Directors (Recommendation Sl. No. 1), the Committee noted that appointments are made based on guidelines from the Department of Personnel & Training (DoPT) and Department of Public Enterprises (DPE). The Board includes two women (one from the Scheduled Caste (SC) category) and two members each from SC and Scheduled Tribe (ST) categories, but no OBC official. For Group 'D' vacancies (Recommendation Sl. No. 3), the Committee was informed that no recruitment has occurred since 2013 due to reduced manpower requirements following the privatization of Delhi, Mumbai, and Greenfield Airports. Six AAI airports transitioned to Public-Private Partnership (PPP) mode in 2020 and 2021, and employees have the option to join the PPP operator or return to AAI, potentially leading to excess manpower.

The report also noted that as of 01.05.2022, OBC employees constituted 25.43% of the total 16076 AAI employees. Their representation was 22.23% in Group 'A', 29.06% in Group 'B', 35.21% in Group 'C', and 14.50% in Group 'D'. The Government stated there are no backlog vacancies in Group 'A'. An AAI OBC Employees Welfare Association has received a Registration Certificate. Liaison Officers for OBCs appointed at Corporate Headquarters (CHQ) and Regional Headquarters belong to the OBC category. AAI has implemented Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) schemes worth Rs. 380.26 crore since 2014, with some funds contributed to general government funds and common health requirements benefiting vulnerable populations, including OBCs. AAI accepts OBC certificates in scheduled languages if in the prescribed format, and the DoPT has circulated a letter dated 13th April, 2023, regarding this and the availability of digital copies online.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry should take up the matter at an appropriate level to ensure representation of the OBC category in the Board of Directors of Airports Authority of India, based on Department of Public Enterprises (DPE) Office Memorandum (OM) No. 2(18)2011-GM dated 18 April, 2011.
  • The Ministry should conduct an exercise to ascertain the current strength of Group 'D' employees available in AAI and inform the Committee of these facts.
  • Efforts should be made for reaching the stipulated 27% OBC reservation in Group 'A' posts in a time-bound manner, and ensure that the overall representation of OBCs does not fall short of the mandated 27% at any given point of time. The Ministry may consider initiating special recruitment drives to fill the backlog of vacancies in Group 'A' posts.
  • An OBC Employees Welfare Association should be formed at the earliest, and the Committee's suggestion to work in coordination with the Association for redressal of grievances of OBC employees was noted by the Ministry.
  • Liaison Officers nominated for looking after matters relating to reservation for OBCs should belong to the OBC category only. The Ministry of Civil Aviation should ensure strict compliance of Department of Personnel & Training (DoPT) Office Memorandum (OM) No. 42011/18/96.Estt. (Res.) dated 06.03.1997 by AAI.
  • AAI should by its own volition earmark funds for development of marginalized sections of society under Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR). The suggestion of the Committee was noted for future compliance.
  • Department of Personnel & Training (DoPT) should circulate guidelines to be followed by all Ministries/Departments and Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) regarding acceptance of OBC caste certificates in scheduled languages. An approved format of OBC certificates in all scheduled languages should be circulated along with necessary guidelines for maintaining uniformity. Digital copies of OBC certificates should also be made available online to verify their authenticity by recruiting organizations.
