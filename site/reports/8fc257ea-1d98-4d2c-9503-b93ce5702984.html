Report on OBC Representation and Welfare in Steel Ministry, SAIL, and RINL

Report date: 2023-12-18

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

The Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes examined the measures undertaken to secure representation of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment and for their welfare in the Ministry of Steel, Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL), and Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL). These organizations fall under the administrative control of the Ministry of Steel.

The Committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Steel, SAIL, and RINL on October 5, 2023. The report outlines facts gathered, analyses, observations, and recommendations concerning the implementation of reservation policies for OBCs, their representation in various positions, and welfare initiatives within these entities. The report was considered and adopted by the Committee at its sitting held on December 5, 2023. It was presented to the Lok Sabha on December 18, 2023, and laid in the Rajya Sabha on December 14, 2023.

What the report found

Ministry of Steel:

The Ministry of Steel had a sanctioned strength of 245 employees, with 184 in position as on August 31, 2023. Out of these, 45 employees (24.45%) were from OBCs. Group ‘A’ had 17.77% OBC representation, Group ‘B’ had 31%, and Group ‘C’ had 21.53%. The Committee noted low representation of OBCs in overall strength, and particularly in Group ‘A’ and ‘C’. The Ministry stated it is the cadre controlling authority only for Multi Tasking Staff (MTS), with other posts filled by various All India and Central Services. Reservation for OBCs does not apply in promotions, as per Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT) Office Memorandum (OM) dated July 2, 1997. For MTS, 12 vacancies exist; 3 OBC vacancies were intimated to the Staff Selection Commission (SSC) on April 7, 2022, and 1 more OBC vacancy is to be sent. The roster for MTS is maintained by a Deputy Secretary and was last upgraded on September 30, 2021.

Initially, the Liaison Officer for OBCs belonged to the ST category, but a Deputy Secretary from the OBC category was subsequently appointed as a separate Liaison Officer for OBC employees on October 10, 2023. OBC candidates selected on merit are not adjusted against the reservation quota. No complaints were received from OBC employees in the last two years.

Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL):

As of September 1, 2023, SAIL's Board of Directors included one Chairman, 7 Full-time Directors, 2 Government Directors, and 6 Independent Directors. One Director belonged to the OBC category. SAIL follows Presidential Directives for OBC reservation: 27% for direct recruitment on an All India basis through open competition, and 25.84% for other All India basis direct recruitment. For regional recruitment (Group C posts), percentages vary by state (e.g., 6% in Chhattisgarh, 22% in West Bengal, 27% in Tamil Nadu).

SAIL had 9,508 OBC employees out of a total of 57,669 (16.48%). OBC representation in Executive (Group A) was 18.41%, and in Non-Executive (Group B & C) was 16.05%. Reasons cited for low representation included a significant portion of employees recruited before 1993 when OBC was not a specified category, and varying state-specific reservation percentages for Group C posts. As of September 1, 2023, there were 76 backlog posts for OBCs in Group A and 19 in Group C, attributed to the non-availability of suitable candidates for specialized positions or selected candidates not joining. No special recruitment drives had been launched in the last 5 years; backlog vacancies are included in subsequent recruitment advertisements. Concessions for OBCs include a 3-year relaxation in the upper age limit, relaxation in qualifying marks for written tests, and separate merit lists. OBCs selected on merit are not adjusted against the reservation quota.

Post-based Roster Registers are maintained as per Presidential Directives, with central recruitment rosters at Corporate Office and plant/unit level rosters at respective locations. These are inspected annually or as required, with no adverse comments recorded. Liaison Officers (LOs) for OBCs have been appointed in SAIL plants/units; 13 out of 15 units have LOs from the OBC category. Training is provided to LOs and dealing hands.

SAIL has an internal grievance redressal mechanism, including Joint Grievance Committees. However, separate complaint registers/data exclusively for OBC employees are not maintained. Separate Grievance Committees for OBCs have been constituted across Plants/Units. Most units do not have an OBC Employees Welfare Association, except for Salem Steel Plant where one was recently registered.

SAIL engaged 68,792 contract labourers as on September 1, 2023. The report notes that details about castes or religion of contract labour are not maintained, but a majority are from local/peripheral areas comprising predominantly backward communities. SAIL, as the Principal Employer, ensures compliance with the Contract Labour (Regulation & Abolition) Act, 1970, and other applicable acts, including payment of minimum wages and provision of basic welfare facilities.

For Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR), SAIL earmarks at least 2% of its Average Net Profit, allocating funds even during adverse financial periods. CSR initiatives primarily focus on backward areas and aspirational districts in states like Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, and Odisha, covering areas like education, healthcare, and livelihood generation.

Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL):

RINL, a Schedule 'A' Navratna company, had 14,729 regular employees as on March 31, 2023. As of September 25, 2023, the Board of Directors included a Chairman-cum-Managing Director, three Functional Directors, two Part-time Official Directors, and two Part-time Non-Official Directors; no members belonged to the OBC category.

OBC reservations were implemented in RINL from September 8, 1993. The policy reserves 27% for direct recruitment on an All India basis through open competition and 25.84% for other All India basis direct recruitment. OBC-NCL candidates selected on merit are not counted against the reserved quota. Since September 8, 1993, 3,298 OBC candidates joined RINL, with 978 selected on merit.

As on September 1, 2023, RINL had 2,967 OBC employees out of 14,051 total (21.08%). In the Executive cadre, OBC representation was 20.37%, and in the Non-Executive cadre, it was 21.43%. The report states that no backlog vacancies for OBCs were identified. No recruitment or special recruitment drives had taken place in the last three years.

Separate Roster Registers are maintained for each post at RINL Headquarters, with common rosters for certain posts, and these are inspected/authenticated annually by the Liaison Officer for OBCs. No discrepancies have been highlighted. Shri G Appa Rao, General Manager (MM), an OBC, serves as the Liaison Officer for OBCs.

RINL uses its existing grievance redressal machinery for all employees, including OBCs; there is no separate system exclusively for OBCs. One complaint from an OBC employee received on October 6, 2022, was addressed and disposed of on July 6, 2023. Representatives of the VSP OBC Employees Welfare Association meet with relevant General Managers at least monthly.

RINL engages 15,774 contract workers as on August 31, 2023. As the Principal Employer, RINL ensures that contractors comply with the Contract Labour (Regulation & Abolition) Act, 1970, and pay minimum wages as notified by the Regional Labour Commissioner (Central), Visakhapatnam, and comply with other statutory provisions like EPF&MP Act, 1952 and ESI Act, 1948. RINL cannot insist contractors engage OBC workers due to the absence of a mandatory provision.

The statutory requirement for RINL's CSR budget has been 'Nil' since FY 2016-17 due to financial performance. Consequently, no budget was allocated for CSR activities in FY 2022-23 and FY 2023-24.

What was recommended

  • For the Ministry of Steel (and implicitly, the Government of India):

* All-out efforts may be done and ways and means found to come to a solution whereby if more persons/officials from OBC category be appointed/nominated to the Board of Directors of SAIL and RINL. Such appointments may be considered especially for cases that fit the prescribed criteria.

  • For Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL):

* Take up the matter at the highest level to address the issue of low representation of OBC employees in SAIL.

* Make concerted efforts to implement the reservation policy for OBCs scrupulously to reach the mandate of 27% OBCs reservation both in Executive and non-Executive posts in a time-bound manner.

* Apprise the Committee once in six months, and at the time of furnishing Action Taken Notes, about the steps taken to fill such vacancies meant for OBCs until the mandated 27% percentage is achieved.

* Maintain a separate list of OBC candidates who qualify on their own merit in each Group of service.

* Advertise backlog vacancies separately and give wide coverage/publicity to the same in press and recruitment sites to attract suitable OBC candidates for the vacancies.

* In addition, campus recruitment be organized with the help of premier educational institutions in the relevant field to attract the best talent to fill backlog vacancies for OBCs in specialized cadres. Initially, Trainees/Apprentices may be inducted to give them proper exposure and experience in the field which is relevant to their job profile before regularizing their services in SAIL.

* Names, addresses, contact numbers, and e-mail IDs of all Liaison Officers of each plant/unit may be uploaded on the intranet/website of SAIL.

* Roster Registers duly authenticated by the Liaison Officers be uploaded on the intranet/website of SAIL in order to bring transparency and for information of all employees.

* Separate complaint registers and data with reference to complaints lodged by OBC employees be maintained henceforth, and this information may also be uploaded on the intranet/website for employees of SAIL.

* Minutes of discussions on various issues for their resolution be prepared/maintained, and these minutes may also be uploaded on the intranet/website for information of employees of SAIL.

* Encourage OBC employees in other units/plants to form OBC Welfare Associations.

  • For Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL):

* Draw attention of the Ministry of Steel to Department of Public Enterprises (DPE) OM No. 2(15)/2011 GM dated April 18, 2011, wherein it has been mentioned that if persons from the categories of SC, ST, and OBC, including women, come forward with the criteria laid down for appointment of non-Official Directors on the Board of Central Public Sector Enterprises (CPSE), they should be given preference.

* Take up the matter at the highest level to address the issue of low representation of OBC employees in RINL.

* Make concerted efforts to implement the reservation policy for OBCs scrupulously to reach the mandate of 27% OBCs reservation both in Executive and non-Executive posts in a time-bound manner.

* Roster Registers duly authenticated by the Liaison Officers be uploaded on the intranet/website of RINL in order to bring transparency and for information of all employees.

* Name, address, contact number, and e-mail of the Liaison Officer may be uploaded on the intranet/website of RINL.

* A separate Grievance Redressal Mechanism may be set up for OBC employees to lodge their grievances, and proper records of such surfaced grievances and their redressal be maintained and uploaded on the intranet/website of RINL.

* RINL may encourage the contract agency/contractor to give preference, on its own volition, to the persons belonging to the other backward classes while employing contract labour.

What it means for citizens

This report outlines efforts to improve the representation and welfare of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment within the Ministry of Steel, Steel Authority of India Limited (SAIL), and Rashtriya Ispat Nigam Limited (RINL). For OBC citizens seeking employment or currently working in these organizations, the report's findings highlight existing shortfalls in their representation, particularly in higher-level positions and specific employee groups. The recommendations aim to address these disparities by calling for concerted efforts to fill vacancies, advertise backlog positions widely, and explore specific recruitment drives, including campus recruitment for specialized roles.

Furthermore, the report seeks to enhance transparency and accountability within these organizations. Recommendations include uploading roster registers and contact details of Liaison Officers for OBCs on company websites, and establishing specific grievance redressal mechanisms with proper record-keeping for OBC employees. This could mean easier access to information on reservation policies and a more structured way for OBC employees to raise and resolve work-related issues. The report also acknowledges that a majority of contract labourers are from backward communities and notes that the organizations ensure contractors comply with minimum wage laws and provide basic welfare facilities, which is relevant for citizens employed through outsourcing contracts.
