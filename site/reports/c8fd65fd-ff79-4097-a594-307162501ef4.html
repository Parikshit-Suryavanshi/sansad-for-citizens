Report on Representation and Welfare of Other Backward Classes in Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation

Report date: 2023-03-27

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

This Nineteenth Report of the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes examined the measures taken to ensure representation and welfare of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment within the Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO), which falls under the Ministry of Labour and Employment. EPFO is described as one of the world's largest Social Security Organisations, established by the Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952.

The Committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Labour and Employment and EPFO on 23.06.2022. The discussions covered various aspects, including the total staff strength, the number of OBC employees at different levels, backlog vacancies, monitoring mechanisms for reservation policy, and issues related to outsourced jobs and contractual appointments. The report also addresses the composition of EPFO's top management body, the Central Board of Trustees, Employees' Provident Fund (CBT, EPF). The Committee considered and adopted this report at its sitting held on 22.03.2023.

What the report found

The report found that none of the 41 whole-time functional Members (besides the Chairman and Central Provident Fund Commissioner cum Member secretary) in EPFO's top management body belong to the OBC category as on the report date. As of 31.03.2022, there was a significant gap between the total sanctioned strength (24073) and the actual strength (14484) of EPFO employees. The overall representation of OBC employees in EPFO was 17.07% of the total strength (2473 out of 14484 employees) as on 31.03.2022.

Specific group-wise representation percentages for OBCs as of 31.03.2022 were 15.91% in Group A, 10.89% in Group B, and 19.88% in Group C posts. These percentages are below the prescribed 27% for OBCs. The Ministry stated that OBC reservation applies only to direct recruitment quota vacancies in 8 cadres, while other cadres are filled through promotions or deputation where OBC reservation is not applicable. Recruitment efforts were reportedly impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 and 2021. For example, the last recruitment for Assistant Provident Fund Commissioner (APFC), a Group A post, was in 2016. Efforts are now being made to fill 160 APFC vacancies up to the year 2022, with 38 posts reserved for OBCs. Vacancies for Assistant Section Officer (ASO) and Social Security Assistant (SSA) were notified in 2019, reserving 64 and 631 seats for OBC candidates respectively.

Roster Registers, which track reservation implementation, are maintained at Zonal and Head Office Levels, with no discrepancies highlighted by Liaison Officers (LOs) during inspection. All Liaison Officers for OBCs are currently from the OBC category, following instructions to appoint them from the OBC category only, even from Group B if Group A OBC officers are unavailable. Despite LOs also functioning as Grievance Officers, no complaints from OBC employees were formally received during the last two years. However, two specific representations from OBC employees were brought to the Committee's attention during a sitting. EPFO uses contract agencies for non-core services like housekeeping and security, employing 1618 contractual employees. These contractual employees receive minimum wages and ESI/PF benefits, but persons belonging to OBCs are not given preference by these agencies.

What was recommended

  • If persons from the OBC category fulfill the criteria for appointment as a Member of the Central Board of Trustee, they should be given preference in appointment.
  • The Ministry should present data clearly, classifying direct recruitment posts, deputation posts, and promotional posts.
  • OBC candidates appointed on their own merit should be shown separately and not adjusted against reserved vacancies.
  • OBC representation in the organization chart must count only those candidates recruited on relaxed standards as per reservation policy.
  • All such data (representation data) should be uploaded on the organization's website.
  • All pending recruitment procedures for appointment to various posts and cadres as per reservation policy for OBCs in employment should be expeditiously completed.
  • The Roster Registers, duly authenticated by Liaison Officers (LOs), should be uploaded on the organization's website to make them accessible to all employees.
  • The names of all Liaison Officers, along with their contact details (mobile numbers and email IDs), should be uploaded on the organization's website.
  • The Ministry should revisit two specific grievance cases from OBC employees, taking into consideration the relevant provisions issued by the Department of Personnel & Training (DoPT).
  • EPFO may encourage contract agencies to give employment, by their own volition, to persons belonging to the OBC Category on humanitarian grounds.
  • If any temporary appointment is made by EPFO that is to last for 45 days or more, the benefit of reservation should be provided to persons belonging to the OBC Category, in light of DoPT OM.No.36036/3/2018-Estt(Res.) dated 15.05.2018.
  • The management of EPFO may work in coordination with the OBC Employees’ Welfare Association, once formed, for the redressal of grievances of OBC employees.

What it means for citizens

This report focuses on how the Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO), which manages provident fund, pension, and insurance schemes for a large workforce, implements policies for Other Backward Classes (OBCs). For ordinary OBC citizens, particularly those seeking employment or currently employed within EPFO, the report's findings highlight a low representation in jobs and top management.

The recommendations aim to create more transparent and equitable opportunities. They suggest that EPFO should make job data clearer and faster recruitment processes to fill vacancies for OBCs. The report also calls for better access to information about reservation registers and contact details of grievance officers, which could help OBC employees address issues. While it doesn't mandate reservation in outsourced jobs, it encourages agencies to consider OBC candidates. If EPFO makes temporary appointments of 45 days or more, OBC citizens could benefit from reservation in those roles as per a DoPT guideline dated 15.05.2018. Overall, the report seeks to ensure that the reservation policy for OBCs is properly implemented and that their welfare and grievances are effectively addressed within this key social security institution.
