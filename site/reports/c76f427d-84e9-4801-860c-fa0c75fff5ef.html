Committee on OBC Welfare Reviews Government Action on Reservation and Welfare in EPFO

Report date: 2017-02-08

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

This report, the Sixth Report (Sixteenth Lok Sabha) of the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes, focuses on the action taken by the Government regarding recommendations from the Committee's Second Report. The Second Report specifically addressed "Reservation in employment and welfare measures for OBCs in the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation" (EPFO) and pertained to the Ministry of Labour & Employment.

The Committee's Second Report (Sixteenth Lok Sabha) was presented to the Lok Sabha on 2015-08-13 and laid in the Rajya Sabha on the same day. The Government's replies to all recommendations in that report were received on 2015-11-17. This current draft report was considered and adopted by the Committee at a sitting held on 2017-01-27.

What the report found

The Committee's Second Report contained 7 recommendations or observations. The Government submitted action taken notes for all of them. Of these, 2 recommendations (Sl. Nos. 2 and 3) were accepted by the Government, while the Committee did not desire to pursue any (Nil). However, the Committee did not accept the Government's replies for 5 recommendations (Sl. Nos. 1, 4, 5, 6, and 7) and stated that these require reiteration. Final replies for no recommendations were still awaited (Nil).

The Committee observed a significant disparity between the sanctioned strength of 24,659 and the actual strength of 18,901 officials in the EPFO as on 2014-03-31, resulting in a shortage of 5,758 officials, including 249 Group ‘A’ officers, 1691 Group ‘B’ officers, and 3818 Group ‘C’ staff. It was noted that no recruitment for Group ‘B’ posts had occurred since 1998-99. The Committee also found that the EPFO was not maintaining year-wise data on recruitments and the implementation of reservation policy for Other Backward Classes (OBCs) since 1993, and had not developed a centralised data maintenance system even 10 years after the Right to Information Act, 2005 came into force. The Committee described the Ministry of Labour & Employment's replies on some matters as "routine, casual and academic," "insufficient," and repetitive.

What was recommended

  • The recruitment process should be expedited by pragmatically resolving issues with the Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) and other recruitment agencies, and all pending Other Backward Classes (OBC) vacancies should be filled in a time-bound manner.
  • The EPFO should develop a computerised data maintenance system for all recruitments made and reservation enforced at Regional, Zonal, and Headquarter levels since at least 1993, and connect them through a network to ensure easy, smooth, and quick access to such records, on priority.
  • The Ministry of Labour and Employment should take the initiative to ensure representation of OBC persons as employee representatives in the Central Board of Trustees (CBT) of the EPFO to promote and protect the interests of OBC employees. This could be achieved by encouraging OBC employees to form associations for their welfare.
  • The Ministry should also make efforts to include an OBC member in the Executive Committee among the three employee representatives.
  • The Employees’ Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) should take the initiative to start pre-recruitment training, especially for OBC candidates, on a priority basis to provide a level playing field. The possibilities and feasibility of using Zonal Training Institutes and/or the National Academy for Training and Research in Social Security (NATRSS) for this purpose should be explored.
  • The organisation should adopt a just and equitable approach for the weaker sections of society in the future and accommodate more OBC officials in foreign training programmes, assignments, and symposia.
  • The EPFO should make all efforts to include at least one OBC officer in the Departmental Promotion Committee (DPC) on the lines of the inclusion of Scheduled Caste (SC) / Scheduled Tribe (ST) officers, to ensure OBC representation.
  • The EPFO should consider recognising the formation of an OBC Employees’ Welfare Association in their organisation to provide an appropriate platform for OBC employees to voice their sentiments, problems, grievances, and complaints before the management. The EPFO may also arrange to hold periodical meetings with representatives of such an association to ensure their grievances are addressed as early as possible.
  • Action taken notes on the recommendations/observations contained in Chapter I of this report should be furnished to the Committee within three months of the presentation of this report.

What it means for citizens

This report pertains to the efforts to ensure fair representation and welfare measures for Other Backward Classes (OBC) citizens within the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO). For OBC individuals seeking employment with the EPFO, the report highlights that many vacancies, including those reserved for OBCs, have remained unfilled for a long time, potentially delaying or limiting their employment opportunities. The report also suggests that introducing pre-recruitment training could help OBC candidates compete more effectively.

For OBC individuals currently employed in the EPFO, the report addresses concerns about their representation in important decision-making bodies like the Central Board of Trustees and Departmental Promotion Committees. It recommends ensuring more equitable opportunities for them in foreign training programmes and calls for the official recognition of OBC Employees' Welfare Associations, which would provide a formal way for OBC employees to raise their concerns and grievances within the organisation. The report also points out that the absence of proper data collection on recruitments and reservations makes it difficult to assess how effectively reservation policies for OBCs have been implemented since 1993.
