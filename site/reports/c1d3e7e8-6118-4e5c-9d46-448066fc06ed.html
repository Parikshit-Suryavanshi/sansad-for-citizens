Parliamentary Committee Calls for Greater OBC Representation and Support in Reserve Bank of India

Report date: 2014-02-07

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 15

What was discussed

The Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes examined the topic of reservation in employment and welfare measures for Other Backward Classes (OBCs) within the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). The RBI, established on April 1, 1935, and nationalized in 1949, functions to regulate bank notes, maintain reserves, and manage India's currency and credit system.

The Committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services) and the Reserve Bank of India on March 12, 2013, regarding these matters. The Committee's report was considered and adopted at a sitting held on December 17, 2013.

What the report found

The Committee observed that as of December 11, 2013, no Director on the RBI's Central Board belonged to the OBC category. The RBI stated that Central Government appoints/nominates Directors under Section 8 of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. The Department of Financial Services explained that official directors are selected from existing executives, and non-official director nominations are part-time appointments not covered by the Government of India's reservation policy for other appointments. However, guidelines approved by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) stipulate that representation "as far as possible" be given to women and persons belonging to Scheduled Caste (SC) and Scheduled Tribe (ST) communities.

The RBI has been implementing guidelines on reservation in recruitment for OBCs since September 8, 1993, when the OBC quota was introduced. For Class I officers (Grade 'A' and 'B') in All India open competitive recruitments, the reservation is 27%. For Class I Officers (Grade 'B') recruited otherwise than by open competition (e.g., campus recruitment), the reservation is 25.84%. This adjusted percentage is to ensure total reservation does not exceed the 50% rule, considering the 16.66% reservation for SCs in such recruitments. For Class III (Clerical/Non-clerical Staff) and Class IV (Subordinate Staff), the reservation percentage varies from State to State as per Government of India guidelines.

Between 2009 and 2011, there was no regular recruitment in Class III and Class IV, except for some Security Guards in Class IV. In 2012, 1000 Assistants were recruited in Class III. The report noted six backlog vacancies for OBCs in the Assistant Manager (Security) post in 2010, which reduced to three by 2013. There was also one backlog vacancy in Grade B campus recruitment. Rosters, which are lists used to track reserved positions, are maintained at Regional Offices for Class III and Class IV staff, and at the Central Office for Class I officers. These rosters are inspected yearly by the Liaison Officer for OBCs and were recently inspected by the Ministry of Finance, which found them in order.

A Scheduled Caste (SC) or Scheduled Tribe (ST) member is included in Departmental Selection Committees/Selection Boards for recruitment to protect the interests of all reserved category candidates. The RBI stated there are no specific Government of India guidelines for including a representative of OBCs in these committees. The RBI has appointed a separate Liaison Officer for OBC employees, and the Statutory Requirements and Recruitment Section (SRRS) at the Central Office also addresses OBC employee interests. Half-yearly meetings are organized with the All India Reserve Bank OBC Employees' Association, chaired by the Executive Director holding charge of Administration, and similar meetings are held at Regional Offices. Minutes of these meetings are prepared and circulated. A complaint register is maintained, but no complaints were received during the two years prior to the report.

The RBI does not provide pre-recruitment or pre-promotion training to OBC candidates/employees, stating that no instructions have been received from the Government of India in this regard. However, the Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services) had circulated a letter dated October 31, 2012, to all Public Sector Banks (PSBs), Financial Institutions (FIs), Public Sector Insurance Companies (PSICs), and RBI, emphasizing the need for such training for SC/ST and OBC candidates/employees. Out of 2581 officers deputed for foreign training between 2009 and 2013, 200 were from the OBC community.

Regarding financial support, the RBI informed that credit support for weaker sections, including SCs, STs, and OBCs, exists under the Government's priority sector lending. However, reservations or concessions under specific Central Government sponsored schemes like the National Rural Livelihood Mission (NRLM), Swarna Jayanti Shahari Rozgar Yojana (SJSRY), Scheme for Rehabilitation of Manual Scavengers (SRMS), and Differential Rate of Interest (DRI) Scheme are currently not available to OBCs. The RBI also stated that there are no specific instructions from the Government of India for financial support to OBCs through Commercial Banks.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services) should take appropriate steps to ensure the representation of OBCs on the board of directors in all Public Sector Banks (PSBs), Financial Institutions (FIs), and Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) by amending existing guidelines, in consultation with the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions (Department of Personnel and Training).
  • The Reserve Bank of India should make all efforts to fill the remaining three backlog vacancies in the OBC category for the post of Assistant Manager (Security) and one vacancy in Grade B campus recruitment at the earliest. The Committee wishes to be apprised of the final results of the next recruitment process.
  • Regular recruitments should be made in Class III and Class IV posts regularly, and OBCs should be given their due share.
  • The Reserve Bank of India should include representatives of OBCs periodically on its selection committees/boards, in line with the Government's directive (Office Memorandum No. 42011/15/95-Estt. (SCT) dated July 11, 1995, issued by the Ministry of Personnel, P.G. & Pensions (Department of Personnel & Training)).
  • The Reserve Bank of India should start pre-recruitment/pre-promotion training for OBC candidates/employees without any further delay.
  • The Reserve Bank of India should adopt a just and equitable approach to accommodate more OBC persons in foreign training programmes, assignments, and symposia.
  • The Reserve Bank of India and the Department of Financial Services should take the initiative to include reservations/concessions for persons belonging to the OBC community in all Central/State Government sponsored schemes/programmes, similar to those extended to SCs/STs.
  • Financial support, such as loans at concessional rates, should be extended to students and entrepreneurs of the OBC community. The Committee wishes to be apprised of the action taken.

What it means for citizens

For ordinary citizens belonging to Other Backward Classes (OBCs), this report highlights several areas where their representation and access to opportunities within the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and the broader financial system are limited. Currently, OBC individuals do not have representation on the RBI's Central Board of Directors, and there are specific job vacancies reserved for OBCs within the RBI that remain unfilled.

Furthermore, OBC candidates and employees in the RBI are not receiving pre-recruitment or pre-promotion training to enhance their skills, despite government instructions emphasizing this need. The report also notes that fewer OBC officers are sent for foreign training compared to their overall representation. Critically, existing government-sponsored financial schemes designed for weaker sections of society, which offer reservations or concessions to Scheduled Caste (SC) and Scheduled Tribe (ST) beneficiaries, do not currently extend these benefits to OBC citizens. There are also no specific government instructions for commercial banks to provide targeted financial support to OBCs.

The Committee's recommendations aim to address these disparities. If implemented, these measures could lead to increased representation for OBC citizens on institutional boards, a greater number of filled job positions in the RBI, access to essential training programs to improve career prospects, and fairer inclusion in international training opportunities. Moreover, the recommendations advocate for equal financial support, including reservations and concessional loans, for OBC students and entrepreneurs under various government schemes, which could significantly aid their socio-economic development and self-reliance.
