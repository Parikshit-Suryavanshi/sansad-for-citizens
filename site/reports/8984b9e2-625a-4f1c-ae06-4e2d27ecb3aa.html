Committee Report Highlights Delays and Monitoring Gaps in MPLAD Scheme Implementation

Report date: 2011-08-11

Committee: Committee on MPLADS

Lok Sabha: 15

What was discussed

This Fourth Report by the Committee on MPLADS examined the "Effective Monitoring of the MPLAD Scheme to avoid delays in execution of MPLADS works," pertaining to the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation. The Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme (MPLADS) is a Centrally sponsored Plan Scheme, fully funded by the Government of India, launched on 23 December 1993. Its objective is to enable Members of Parliament to recommend developmental works, emphasizing the creation of durable community assets based on locally felt needs, such as drinking water, primary education, public health, sanitation, and roads.

The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation administers the Scheme, formulating policy, releasing funds, and prescribing monitoring mechanisms. State/Union Territory Nodal Departments are responsible for overall implementation, while District Authorities execute works recommended by MPs and manage funds, selecting implementing agencies like Line Departments, Local Self Governments, or other Government agencies, and in some cases, Non-Government organizations. The Committee on MPLADS (2008-09) initially selected this subject for examination, which remained inconclusive. The Committee on MPLADS (2009-10) re-selected it, taking oral evidence from Ministry representatives on 1 April 2010 and 9 September 2010. The Committee on MPLADS (2010-11) then selected the subject again to finalize the report, adopting the Draft Report on 7 March 2011. The report was presented to the Speaker, Lok Sabha on 4 May 2011 and to Lok Sabha on 11 August 2011.

What the report found

The Committee on MPLADS took strong exception to remarks by the Secretary, Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, stating that the Parliamentary Committees on MPLADS were constituted as "advisory Committees," finding this derogatory to the status of Parliament.

The report found that the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation uses Utilization Certificates and Audit Certificates as monitoring tools for fund release, but delays in furnishing these documents can lead to delays in fund release and scheme implementation. The Ministry uses Monthly Progress Reports (MPRs) for fund release eligibility but does not analyze them to identify reasons for delays in work completion because MPRs do not contain individual work details. The Online Monthly Progress Reporting System (OMPRS), introduced in February 2009, was only used by about 40% of districts for online data submission. The MPLADS Work Monitoring Software, launched in November 2004, still had data for 1,270 Lok Sabha MPs (across the 10th to 15th Lok Sabha) yet to be uploaded as of the report date, hindering meaningful analysis. The Ministry stated that it was examining the feasibility of integrating the MPR system and Work Monitoring Software.

Regarding Bi-annual Review Meetings held by the Ministry, the Committee noted that a meeting on 2 February 2010 reviewed all States within a single day, and representatives from several States (Nagaland, Assam, Tripura, Puducherry, Chandigarh, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Daman and Diu, Goa, Assam, and Sikkim) were absent or left early, indicating a lack of seriousness in these meetings. Ministry officers conducted field visits, but the Ministry's follow-up information on shortcomings and actions taken was routine and lacked specific examples.

NABARD Consultancy Services (NABCONS) conducted physical monitoring in selected districts, starting in 2006-07. Its first phase findings indicated that MPLADS had an overall positive impact, creating good quality assets, with 74% of works having a positive economic impact, 86% a positive social impact, and 63% a positive physical environmental impact, according to local communities. However, NABCONS also identified significant violations of guidelines:

  • Delays: In approximately 45% of inspected works, the time lag between MP recommendation and sanction exceeded 45 days. In about 40% of works, the completion period exceeded 365 days.
  • Poor Maintenance: About 30% of assets lacked proper maintenance. 50% of cases had no formal system for handing over assets to user agencies, and 60% had no undertaking for upkeep.
  • Plaques: Approximately 50% of work sites lacked plaques, and those erected often did not indicate required project details.
  • Inspection by District Authority: In 60% of cases, no structured inspection was carried out by District Authorities, and no proper records were maintained.
  • NGO/Society/Trust Works: Many cases lacked 100% inspection by District Authorities. Some agreements with trusts/societies did not include an 18% interest clause, and some single NGOs were allotted works exceeding Rs. 25 lakhs. Similar shortcomings were observed in the second phase of monitoring.

At the State/UT level, the report found that State-level monitoring committees under Chief Secretaries (or similar) were often not holding regular meetings. Mizoram, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, and Daman & Diu had not constituted these committees. For many States/UTs, the Ministry could not provide information on the number or dates of these meetings, or whether MPs were invited or attended. Six States had not invited MPs to such meetings at all. Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu had existing Chief Secretary-level committees, but no meetings were held. Inspection by Nodal Department officers was inconsistent, with Tamil Nadu reporting 2,016 inspections in three years, while Sikkim reported that its officers generally do not inspect MPLADS works.

At the District level, District Authorities are required to inspect at least 10% of works annually and involve MPs. Maharashtra and Meghalaya reported inability to meet the 10% inspection target due to heavy workload and insufficient staff. MPs were generally not involved in inspections in Maharashtra and Meghalaya, often invited only for commencement or inauguration. NABCONS found that 60% of cases lacked structured inspection records. Monthly review meetings with Implementing Agencies by District Authorities were also inconsistent, with only four States (Assam, Goa, Kerala, Uttar Pradesh) providing details. MPs often sent representatives to these meetings due to busy schedules. The Ministry identified limitations in intensive monitoring, noting that the MPLADS Division had 13 staff in position against 21 sanctioned posts, and 7 additional posts for the Account Branch could not be created.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry should amend MPLADS guidelines to redefine the role of MPLADS Parliamentary Committees. The new guideline should state that these committees review performance and problems, consider complaints from Members, and perform other functions assigned by the Speaker/Chairman for effective and efficient scheme functioning. This amendment should be carried out within one month.
  • The Nodal Department in each State should coordinate with District Authorities to ensure timely submission of Utilization Certificates and Audit Certificates, and take action to address delays in their submission to the Ministry.
  • The Ministry should immediately upload data for the 15th Lok Sabha to the MPLADS Work Monitoring Software on priority within a definite timeframe.
  • An integrated software, combining features of the online Monthly Progress Report and Work Monitoring Software, should be immediately developed and launched. This software should be simple, user-friendly, and less time-consuming for District Authorities.
  • The Ministry should assign a definite role to Nodal Departments in States, via guidelines, to monitor scheme implementation through the proposed integrated software.
  • The Ministry should monitor overall scheme implementation in States on a monthly basis using the integrated software and, within a timeframe, address major violations or inordinate delays with the Nodal Departments.
  • The Ministry should hold annual review meetings, spreading discussions region-wise, to allow for holistic discussions on scheme implementation. These meetings should invite Nodal Secretaries, Members of Parliament, and District Magistrates from the concerned States.
  • Inspections by senior officers of the Ministry should be result-oriented, not just a formality. The Ministry should address violations and delays identified during these inspections on a case-by-case basis with the District Authorities and ensure rectifications are carried out.

What it means for citizens

The MPLAD Scheme is intended to create essential community assets like drinking water facilities, schools, public health centers, sanitation infrastructure, and roads based on local needs. The report highlights that ordinary citizens may experience delays in benefiting from these works due to inadequate monitoring and procedural shortcomings.

If the recommendations are followed, citizens could see improvements in the timely completion and proper maintenance of MPLADS works in their constituencies. Better monitoring by the Ministry, State Nodal Departments, and District Authorities, along with clearer guidelines and improved software, could lead to quicker sanctioning and execution of projects. This would ensure that developmental assets, such as a new school building or a repaired road, are available and functional sooner for public use. Increased involvement of MPs in monitoring, and better record-keeping of inspections, could also lead to greater accountability in project implementation, ensuring that works meet quality standards and address genuine local requirements.
