Committee Reviews Government Action on OBC Employment in Central Public Works Department

Report date: 2020-07-25

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

This Seventh Report of the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes (Seventeenth Lok Sabha) addresses the actions taken by the Government in response to observations and recommendations from the Committee's Twentieth Report (Sixteenth Lok Sabha). The Twentieth Report, presented to the Lok Sabha on 13.02.2019 and laid in the Rajya Sabha on the same day, focused on "Measures undertaken to secure representation of OBCs in employment and their welfare in Central Public Works Department (CPWD)’’ pertaining to the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs.

The Committee received the Government's replies to all recommendations in the Twentieth Report on 17.10.2019. This current report, adopted by the Committee at its sitting on 14.07.2020, examines and categorizes these action taken notes. The original report contained five observations/recommendations.

The report specifically discusses government responses to recommendations concerning the organization and data management in recruitment, ensuring proper representation of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in the total workforce, grievance redressal and the role of the Liaison Officer for OBCs, and policies regarding outsourcing and engaging contractual employees.

What the report found

The Committee noted that as of 1st January, 2018, the Central Public Works Department (CPWD) had 25,452 employees, with 2,113 (8.3%) belonging to the OBC category. OBC representation was 12.54% in Group ‘A’ service, 11.93% in Group ‘B’, and 6.3% in Group ‘C’. The Committee found this low representation unreasonable, given that OBC reservation policy was made effective in 1993.

The Committee observed that no recruitment data was available for the period from 1993 to 2010. While the Ministry later furnished some compiled data, the Committee found substantial shortfalls in OBC vacancies, including 29 for Lower Division Clerk in the Northern Region and 37, 224, and 32 for Multi-Tasking Staff (MTS) in the Eastern, Northern, and Southern Regions, respectively. In the Group ‘C’ category, 11,480 out of 16,544 employees were work-charged, for whom no direct recruitment was being made.

Regarding grievance redressal, the Committee noted that while OBC employees could report grievances to a Liaison Officer for OBCs, this was not explicitly mentioned in the officer's charter of duties. The Ministry responded that the Liaison Officer discharges duties as per a Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT) Office Memorandum (O.M.) dated 4th January, 2013, which describes responsibilities including reporting non-compliance. The Committee also noted that no OBC Employees’ Welfare Association had approached the management with grievances. Concerning contractual employees, the Ministry admitted that details of contract labourers and agencies were not centrally maintained, and there was no provision in CPWD manuals for directing contractors to engage specific categories of labourers. The Ministry stated these were "policy issues" without specific guidelines.

What was recommended

The Committee recommended the following:

  • CPWD needs to put in efforts to make up the shortfall of reservation in the OBC category while filling vacancies in a year.
  • A central nodal officer should be appointed in the Office of Director General (D.G.), CPWD, for monitoring the digital compilation of data to create transparency and synergies between regions for smooth implementation of OBC reservation policy.
  • Regular recruitment in Group-C in the work-charged category should be made with due representation provided to the OBC category in the future.
  • The Ministry should inform the Committee about action on its recommendation regarding changes in recruitment rules and the introduction of direct recruitment in the work-charged category of employees.
  • The Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs should hold periodic meetings with the representative bodies/OBC employees proactively and hear/redress the grievances of OBC employees promptly.
  • The Liaison Officer for OBCs needs to discharge his duties as enshrined in the Department of Personnel and Training (DOPT) Office Memorandum (O.M.) No. 43011/153/2010-Estt.(Res.) dated 4th January, 2013.
  • The Ministry should scrupulously follow the instructions of the Government of India on reservation for Scheduled Caste (SC), Scheduled Tribe (ST), and Other Backward Classes (OBC) candidates in all temporary appointments, which are to last for 45 days or more, as stated in DOPT O.M. No.36036/3/2018 - ESTT. (Res.) dated 15.05.2018.
  • There should be some initiative on the part of the Government to explore ways to give representation to backward classes in contractual employment. The Committee also suggested that the payment of minimum wages as per the Minimum Wages Act and provision of other facilities, pertaining to healthcare, PPF (Public Provident Fund), insurance, and social security benefits, should be ensured for such contractual workers by establishing a proper monitoring mechanism in the department.

What it means for citizens

This report highlights the Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes' ongoing efforts to ensure fair representation and welfare for Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment within the Central Public Works Department (CPWD). For citizens belonging to OBC communities, the report underscores the need for proactive steps by the government to fill vacant positions, particularly in Group ‘C’ roles, and to digitize recruitment data for transparency.

It also means that the Committee expects the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs to address the low representation of OBCs in CPWD and to revise recruitment rules to include reservation in "work-charged" employee categories. For OBC employees, the report emphasizes that grievance redressal mechanisms should be strengthened, with Liaison Officers empowered to address complaints, and that periodic meetings with employee representatives should be held. Furthermore, for citizens working as contractual employees with CPWD, the report calls for the implementation of reservation policies for temporary appointments and for ensuring that they receive minimum wages and essential benefits like healthcare, provident fund, insurance, and social security.
