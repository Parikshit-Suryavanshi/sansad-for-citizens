Committee Reviews OBC Welfare Measures and Constitutional Status for NCBC

Report date: 2013-08-30

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 15

What was discussed

The Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes examined a review of various welfare schemes and measures of the Government of India for Other Backward Classes (OBCs). It also followed up on recommendations from the Committee's First Report regarding strengthening and granting constitutional status to the National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC).

The report details the historical context of efforts to identify and support backward classes, beginning with the Kaka Kalelkar Commission, which was set up on January 29, 1953, and submitted its report on March 30, 1955. This was followed by the Mandal Commission, appointed on January 1, 1979, which commenced functioning on March 21, 1979, completed its work on December 12, 1980, and submitted its report on December 31, 1980. The Indra Sawhney judgement by the Supreme Court, delivered on November 16, 1992, upheld 27% reservation for OBCs. The National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) Act was enacted on February 15, 1993, to establish the NCBC. The Committee gathered evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment on April 23, July 26, August 6, and August 23, 2013. Representatives from the Planning Commission and the Ministry of Finance also provided evidence during sittings on August 6 and August 23, 2013. The report was considered and adopted by the Committee on August 27, 2013.

What the report found

The report found that the Mandal Commission estimated the OBC population at 52%, while the National Sample Survey Organization (NSSO) 61st round survey (2004-05) estimated it at 40% for the country. Caste-wise census was discontinued after the 1931 census. The Socio Economic and Caste Census (SECC) was initiated from June 29, 2011, and field data collection was mostly complete in 32 States/Union Territories as of the report date.

Regarding funding, the Planning Commission provided 12.41% of the Department of Social Justice and Empowerment's 11th Plan allocation for OBCs, increasing to 21% in the 12th Plan. Overall allocation to the department increased by 100% from the 11th to the 12th Plan, and for OBCs by 230%. For the Pre-matric Scholarship Scheme, rates fixed in 1998-99 had not been revised, and the amount was considered insufficient. The Budget Estimate for 2012-13 was Rs. 50 crore, with Rs. 46 crore spent. For 2013-14, the allocation was Rs. 150 crore. The Post-matric Scholarship Scheme was revised on July 1, 2011, raising the parental income ceiling from Rs. 44,500 to Rs. 1 lakh per annum and increasing maintenance and other allowances. However, these revised rates were still considered insufficient. The Budget Estimate for 2012-13 was Rs. 625 crore, which was revised to Rs. 722 crore, but only Rs. 666 crore was spent. For 2013-14, the allocation was Rs. 900 crore. The Ministry noted that scholarship schemes for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs) were "open-ended," unlike those for OBCs, which faced fund constraints.

The Scheme of Hostels for OBC Boys and Girls, revised in 2010-11, provides financial assistance for construction. From 1998-99 to 2012-13, 925 hostels were sanctioned, with 669 completed and 256 remaining incomplete. Achievements and expenditure since 2008-09 were not aligned with targets, primarily due to non-submission of Utilization Certificates by states. The Ministry stated that it does not have information on OBC population for allocation, relying instead on the state's total population ratio. Funds earmarked for the North-East for hostels remained unutilized because only four North-Eastern states have an OBC population.

Under the scheme of Assistance to Voluntary Organisations for welfare of OBCs, Rs. 37.82 crore was spent out of Rs. 60 crore allocated from 1998-99 to 2012-13. Grants were not released to Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs) in 21 out of 30 States/Union Territories due to factors such as a lack of interested NGOs, late submission of documents, and outdated financial norms (Rs. 5,000 per trainee fixed in 1998-99).

The National Backward Classes Finance and Development Corporation (NBCFDC) had an authorized share capital of Rs. 700 crore, enhanced on December 11, 1998, from Rs. 200 crore. A proposal to further increase it to Rs. 1500 crore was under consideration. The annual family income limit for eligible members was enhanced from Rs. 40,000 in rural areas and Rs. 55,000 in urban areas to Rs. 81,000 in rural areas and Rs. 1,03,000 in urban areas.

The report noted that there is no provision in the guidelines on the Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme (MPLADS) for using its funds for the development of areas inhabited by Other Backward Classes (OBCs), unlike for Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs).

The Ministry's replies to the Committee's First Report (presented August 27, 2012), which recommended constitutional status for NCBC, indicated that the recommendations were "being examined" (December 4, 2012) and that a "Draft Note for the Cabinet to further empower the NCBC is presently under preparation" (July 2013).

What was recommended

  • Immediate action should be initiated for amending the Indian Constitution and the National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) Act appropriately for setting up NCBC with constitutional status and exercising identical powers as given to the National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC) and National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (NCST) in relation to Scheduled Castes (SCs) and Scheduled Tribes (STs). These powers should be entrusted to NCBC in relation to Other Backward Classes (OBCs) and not to NCSC.
  • Article 338(10) of the Constitution should be deleted and a new Article 338B should be inserted to establish NCBC. The Constitutional amendment should also include existing powers of NCBC under the prevailing NCBC Act, specifically powers to include in or exclude from the Central list of OBCs and the obligation on the Government of India to consult NCBC for list revision.
  • All issues pertaining to the welfare of OBCs, including complaints and grievances, should be dealt with by NCBC. This may be done only by amending the Constitution. The office of the National Coordinator under NCSC should be dissolved.

What it means for citizens

For citizens belonging to Other Backward Classes (OBCs), the report highlights that access to educational support through Pre-matric and Post-matric scholarships is currently limited by insufficient funds and outdated scholarship rates. While income ceilings for these scholarships have been reviewed and some allowances increased, the report notes that the amounts are still not enough, and many eligible students do not receive benefits. The report also indicates that the construction of hostels for OBC boys and girls is slow, impacting their access to secondary and higher education, especially for those from rural areas.

Furthermore, OBC citizens do not have a dedicated body with full constitutional powers to address their grievances, as the National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) does not currently possess the same constitutional status and investigative powers as the commissions for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. The report calls for constitutional amendments to rectify this. Opportunities for vocational training through Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs) are also underutilized due to low NGO participation and outdated funding. Additionally, unlike Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, there is no specific provision in the Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme (MPLADS) for funds to be used in areas inhabited by OBCs.
