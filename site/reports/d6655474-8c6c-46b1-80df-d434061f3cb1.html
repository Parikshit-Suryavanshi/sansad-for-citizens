Report on Implementation of Scheme for Integrated Cold Chain and Value Addition Infrastructure

Report date: 2017-08-30

Committee: Committee on Agriculture, Animal Husbandry and Food Processing

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

The Committee on Agriculture, Animal Husbandry and Food Processing examined the "Implementation of Scheme for Integrated Cold Chain and Value Addition Infrastructure" pertaining to the Ministry of Food Processing Industries. The Committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Food Processing Industries on 2017-06-19. The report was considered and adopted by the Committee on 2017-08-28.

The discussion focused on the scheme, which was launched in 2008, with the objective of providing integrated cold chain, value addition, and preservation infrastructure from the farm gate to the consumer. This aims to reduce post-harvest losses of agriculture produce, link producers to processors and markets, ensure better prices for farmers, and provide year-round availability of food products. The scheme's guidelines were initially notified on 2010-03-18 and later modified on 2013-11-20, 2014-08-08, and further revised and re-christened on 2016-08-29 as the "Scheme for Integrated Cold Chain and Value Addition Infrastructure."

The report highlighted findings from a 2012-14 study by the Central Institute of Post-Harvest Engineering & Technology (CIPHET) which estimated annual post-harvest losses for major crops and commodities in India at Rs. 92,651 crore. A September 2015 study by the National Center for Cold Chain Development (NCCD) through NABARD Consultancy Services Pvt Ltd. (NABCONS) assessed the cold chain infrastructure requirement at 3.2 million tonnes of cold storage.

What the report found

The Ministry allocated Rs. 704.17 crore for the Cold Chain Scheme during the 12th Plan period (2012-13 to 2016-17), releasing Rs. 683.44 crore. For the current fiscal year (2017-18), Rs. 180.00 crore was allocated, with Rs. 8.11 crore spent as of 2017-05-20. As of 2017-05-15, the Ministry is assisting 236 cold chain projects, with 100 having achieved completion and started commercial operations. Of the 236 projects, 102 are completed, and 134 are under various stages of implementation. 103 projects were sanctioned in March/April 2017.

Reasons for delays in project implementation included changes in location by promoters for viability, difficulties in obtaining statutory approvals (such as land use permissions, pollution control board consents, electricity and water connections), remote locations, and financial challenges faced by promoters. The Ministry stated that it conducts regular review meetings to monitor projects and resolve issues. Revised guidelines from 2016-08-29 include a penalty of 1% of the grant installment for each month's delay, up to a maximum of 5% of the installment amount.

An impact evaluation study by NABCONS noted that, on average, each cold chain project created around 555 jobs (201 direct and 354 indirect) and linked 9329 farmers. Value addition averaged 24% for fresh fruits & vegetables, 18% for frozen meat & meat products, and 12% for the fish sector. The average farm gate price across sectors increased by approximately 34%, and average capacity utilization by projects was around 78%. In the North Eastern States, Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttarakhand, 43 integrated cold chain projects are being assisted. Out of these, 9 projects are in the North Eastern region, with 4 completed (one in Assam, one in Manipur, and two in Mizoram).

What was recommended

  • The Ministry should raise the issue with the Ministry of Finance and seek additional allocation for the projects sanctioned.
  • The implementation of the pending projects should be expedited.
  • The subsidy provided by the government should be enhanced for entrepreneurs/developers who set up cold chain infrastructure in rural areas to encourage such setups.
  • A status report should be submitted for projects sanctioned after the 2016-08-29 revision of the operational guidelines, specifically regarding the mandatory farm-level infrastructure.
  • The Ministry should focus on agriculturally important states like Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, and Madhya Pradesh and take steps to encourage entrepreneurs to apply for the establishment of cold chain infrastructure there.
  • A country-wide integrated cold chain infrastructure network should be created at the block level and district level.
  • The guidelines of the Scheme should be suitably revised.
  • A Cold Chain Coordination and Monitoring Committee should be constituted at the district level.
  • Public representatives (MP/MLA) of the respective districts should be included in the Cold Chain Coordination and Monitoring Committee.
  • The Ministry should make provision under the Scheme to create awareness and provide training to farmers on value addition.
  • The Ministry should expedite the implementation of projects in the North Eastern region.
  • The Ministry of Food Processing Industries should take new initiatives to foster a coordinated approach towards the creation and development of food processing infrastructure and ancillary industries in areas known for the production of major fruits, vegetables, and perishable items.
  • The Ministry should take proactive measures for the attainment of objectives.

What it means for citizens

The implementation of the Scheme for Integrated Cold Chain and Value Addition Infrastructure aims to significantly benefit ordinary people. By reducing post-harvest losses, the scheme intends to increase the availability of food products year-round. For farmers, particularly small and marginal farmers, the scheme's objective is to ensure they receive better and more remunerative prices for their produce, which can be achieved through value addition and access to well-equipped supply chains.

The report highlights that the scheme is expected to create substantial employment opportunities, especially in rural areas, with an average of 555 jobs per cold chain project. The establishment of farm-level infrastructure is intended to directly help farmers by reducing transportation costs and enabling them to add value to their produce closer to their farms. Citizens can expect a more stable supply of food items and increased economic activity in rural areas due to reduced wastage, increased processing, and better market linkages for agricultural products.
