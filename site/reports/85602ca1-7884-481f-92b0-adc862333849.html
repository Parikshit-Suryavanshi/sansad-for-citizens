Parliamentary Committee Reviews OBC Representation and Welfare Measures in GAIL

Report date: 2022-12-21

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 17

What was discussed

The Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes (17th Lok Sabha) examined the 'Measures undertaken to secure representation of Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in employment and for their welfare in Gas Authority of India Limited (GAIL)'. This subject pertained to the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas.

The Committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas and GAIL on 31.05.2022. The report, which includes inputs from a study visit to Srinagar, Chandigarh, and Shimla, was considered and adopted by the Committee at a sitting held on 15.12.2022.

What the report found

The report found that there is no Director belonging to the OBC category on the Board of Directors of GAIL. As of 30.04.2022, the total staff strength in GAIL was 4789, with 1143 employees belonging to the OBC category, constituting 23.86% of the total, which is below the mandated 27% representation.

Specifically, OBC representation in Group ‘A’ (Management) was 21.02%, falling short of the 27% mandate. However, representation in Group ‘B’ was 30.42%, Group ‘C’ was 35.73%, and Group ‘D’ was 27.90%, all of which were above the prescribed 27%. Reasons provided for the shortfall in Group A included the non-availability of suitable OBC candidates in highly specialized disciplines, selected OBC candidates not joining, and OBC candidates selected on merit being counted against Unreserved (UR) vacancies.

GAIL provides reservation to OBCs in direct recruitment (27% for open competition, 25.84% otherwise) for all posts since 08.09.1993, offering concessions such as relaxation in the upper age limit (3 years for OBC-NCL, 13 years for OBC-NCL-PwBD), relaxed selection standards, separate interview sessions, and reimbursement of travel fare for interviews.

As of 31.12.2021, there were 24 backlog vacancies for OBCs in the Management Category (Group ‘A’) and 29 in the Non-Management Category (Group ‘B’ and ‘C’). Through various processes, 14 of the Management backlog vacancies were filled, leaving 10. The 29 Non-Management backlog vacancies remained unfilled due to restrictions on physical examinations during the Covid-19 pandemic. GAIL has a Chief Liaison Officer for OBCs (who is from the OBC category) and an online grievance redressal system; no complaints were received from OBC employees in the last two years. The report noted that there is no dedicated OBC employees’ welfare association/union in GAIL. GAIL outsources some non-core functions, and contract provisions require contractors to make efforts to provide employment opportunities to OBCs. GAIL's Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) initiatives show an average of approximately 35% OBC beneficiaries across various projects in FY 2021-22.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should address the matter at the appropriate level to ensure representation of OBCs in the Board of Directors of GAIL, particularly when filling the 4 vacant positions.
  • The Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas should implement all necessary measures, including relaxing recruitment standards and initiating special recruitment drives, to fill the backlog vacancies in Group 'A' for OBCs in a time-bound manner.
  • GAIL must ensure that the overall representation of OBCs does not fall below the mandated 27% reservation at any point.
  • Unfilled backlog vacancies should be filled on a priority basis to provide employment opportunities to deserving OBC candidates as early as possible.
  • GAIL management should coordinate with the OBC Employees’ Welfare Association, once formed, for the redressal of grievances of OBC employees.
  • Due consideration should be given to OBC employees for sponsorship in various training/skill development programmes, in accordance with existing Government of India guidelines.
  • All Liaison Officers at regional levels who are not from the OBC category should be replaced by Liaison Officers from the OBC category only, without delay, to safeguard the interests of OBC employees.
  • Skill Development Centres should be established in metro cities to provide basic skill training to children from socially and educationally backward families who have migrated to these cities.

What it means for citizens

Ordinary citizens belonging to Other Backward Classes could see increased opportunities for leadership roles in GAIL if the recommendation for OBC representation on the Board of Directors is implemented. They may also find improved access to employment within GAIL, particularly in Group 'A' positions, if recruitment standards are relaxed and special drives are initiated to clear backlog vacancies as recommended. The report suggests that OBC employees could experience more effective support for their welfare and grievances if an OBC Employees’ Welfare Association is formed and if Liaison Officers who are not from the OBC category are replaced by those who are. Furthermore, the recommendation to open Skill Development Centres in metro cities as part of GAIL's Corporate Social Responsibility initiatives could provide practical skill training and potential livelihood opportunities for socially and educationally backward youth, including those from OBC communities, who reside in urban areas.
