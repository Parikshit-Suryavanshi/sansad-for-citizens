Government Takes Action on Recommendations for Revival of Railway Capital Fund from 2005-06

Report date: 2008-04-17

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 14

What was discussed

This Eighth Report of the Railway Convention Committee (2004) focuses on the action taken by the Government regarding the recommendations and observations made in the Committee's Fourth Report. The Fourth Report, titled 'Revival of Capital Fund from 2005-06', was presented to the Lok Sabha on 22nd December, 2005, and laid on the Table of the Rajya Sabha on the same day.

The Fourth Report contained two observations or recommendations. The Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) provided action taken replies for both of these observations or recommendations. The Committee considered and adopted this Eighth Report at its sitting held on 04th January, 2008.

What the report found

The report found that both of the two recommendations or observations (Para Nos. 10 and 11) from the Fourth Report have been accepted by the Government. No recommendations were deemed not to be pursued, had their replies unaccepted by the Committee, or had replies still awaited.

The Ministry of Railways confirmed that the Capital Fund has been made operative from 2005-06 (Revised Estimates). For the provisional accounts of 2005-06, an amount of Rs. 4031.68 crore was appropriated to the Capital Fund, with Rs. 2416.09 crore allocated for capital works and Rs. 1615.59 crore for payment of the principal component of lease hire charges to the Indian Railway Finance Corporation (IRFC). For 2006-07 (Budget Estimates), the appropriation was kept at Rs. 5432.94 crore, comprising Rs. 3712.82 crore for capital works and the remainder for lease charges.

The report also noted that details of railway projects being taken up from the Capital Fund, along with their anticipated costs and outlays for 2005-06 (Revised Estimates) and 2006-07 (Budget Estimates), are available in the 'Works, Machinery and Rolling Stock Programme of Railways for 2006-07 Part-II (C) (Detailed Programme)'. During 2005-06, specific doubling, traffic facility, and gauge conversion works were identified for faster implementation using the Capital Fund. From 2006-07, all doubling projects are being funded through the Capital Fund, alongside several traffic facility works and some gauge conversion projects. Doubling projects are expected to be completed within 3 to 4 years. Seven gauge conversion projects were transferred to the Capital Fund, with five targeted for completion during 2006-07: Katihar-Barsoi (39 Kms), Mudhkhed-Adilabad (167 Kms), Ajmer-Chittorgarh-Udaipur (185 Kms), Neemuch-Ratlam (135 Kms), and Samastipur-Khagaria (84 Kms). Two other projects, Jaynagar-Darbhanga-Narkatiaganj (268 Kms) and Kanpur-Farrukkabad-Kasganj (544 Kms), are phased for completion by 2008-09. Traffic facility works are targeted for completion within two years of sanction, and efforts are made to avoid time and cost overruns for these projects, though the strict concept of overrun applies to projects with financial closure and assured funds.

What was recommended

  • The Capital Fund should be made operative from 2005-06 onwards so that surplus funds are reinvested into the Railways system to improve efficiency, taking into account the financial performance of the Railways for 2004-05 and 2005-06 and the Ministry of Finance's observations.
  • The exact amount appropriated in the Capital Fund, the Railway projects/works taken up/to be taken up for execution from this Fund, their target dates of completion, and any time and cost over-run should be placed before the Committee.
  • The Committee urged that it should be ensured that projects taken up under the Capital Fund are properly planned and executed within the originally scheduled completion date and anticipated cost.

What it means for citizens

The revival of the Capital Fund for Railways from 2005-06 means that surplus money generated by the railway system can be reinvested into critical infrastructure projects. This reinvestment aims to improve the efficiency of the railway network through capital works such as doubling of tracks, enhancing traffic facilities, and converting existing rail lines to broader gauges.

Citizens can expect potential benefits from these investments, including more efficient and possibly faster train services due to improved infrastructure, such as the completion of doubling projects within 3 to 4 years and specific gauge conversion projects by 2006-07 or 2008-09. The Committee's urging for proper planning and execution within original timeframes and costs aims to ensure that these projects are completed efficiently, potentially leading to better public services without unnecessary delays or cost increases.
