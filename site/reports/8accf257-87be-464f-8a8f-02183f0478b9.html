Committee Reviews Government Action on Railway Coaches and Wagons

Report date: 1991-01-10

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 09

What was discussed

This Second Report of the Railway Convention Committee (1989) details the action taken by the Government on the recommendations contained in the Committee's Fourteenth Report (1985). The Fourteenth Report, which focused on "Rolling Stock Programme (Availability of coaches and wagons)," was presented to Parliament on 18 August 1989. The Government's replies to all 32 recommendations and observations from that report were received on 11 July 1990.

The current report examines the Government's responses, particularly concerning the availability and maintenance of railway coaches and wagons. Key areas discussed include the capacity for coach production, the "Periodical Overhaul" (POH) and "ineffective percentage" of coaches (referring to coaches out of service for repairs), the use of overaged coaches, funding for maintenance facilities, and the phasing out of overaged wagons.

The Committee considered the Government's replies at its sitting on 3 January 1991 and adopted this Second Report on the same day.

What the report found

The Committee found that for the Seventh Plan period, the Ministry of Railways planned to acquire 6,970 coaches and 950 Electric Multiple Units (EMUs), while the assessed need was 12,914 coaches and 1,390 EMUs. The total number of coaches on Broad Gauge (BG) and Metre Gauge (MG) collectively decreased from 34,165 in 1984-85 to 33,758 in 1987-88.

The Ministry of Railways stated it augmented coach production capacity by establishing a new coach factory at Kapurthala with a production capacity of 1,000 coaches per year, and by increasing the capacity of the Integral Coach Factory (ICF), Perambur, from 800 to 1,000 coaches during 1990-91. This results in an additional production of 1,200 coaches per year. However, the Railway Reforms Committee had concluded that an additional capacity of 1,534 coaches per year was needed for 1985-2000, indicating an unsatisfied backlog of 334 coaches per year.

The report noted that 7.99% of Broad Gauge (BG) coaches and 3.37% of Metre Gauge (MG) coaches were overdue for "Periodical Overhaul" (POH), which refers to scheduled major repairs. The Ministry of Railways stated that "for the present load, adequate POH capacity exists." The "ineffective percentage" of coaches (the percentage of coaches out of service) decreased from 15.10% to 9.22% on BG and from 10.99% to 9.56% on MG between 1982-83 and 1986-87, but no further improvement was observed since then. The Committee noted that these figures were high compared to the 1962-63 figures of 7.68% for BG coaches and 7.78% for MG coaches.

Regarding overaged coaches, the Ministry of Railways stated that "only those coaches which fulfil all the safety and passenger amenity standards are retained in service," and that defined repair schedules are considered adequate. As of 31 March 1988, there was a balance of 19,616 overaged wagons requiring replacement or condemnation. The Ministry of Railways stated that replacing all overaged wagons was not possible due to fund constraints, and condemnation would continue based on their age and condition.

What was recommended

  • The Committee expects the final reply to recommendation Sl. No. 26 (regarding a dialogue with M/s Texmaco on design and technology improvements for wagons), for which only an interim reply was furnished, to be submitted expeditiously.
  • Additional resources must be arranged for augmenting the production capacity of coaches in the country.
  • Both the Ministry of Railways and the Planning Commission should seriously consider the matter and draw both long-term and short-term schemes to augment coach production capacity to the desired level.
  • The Ministry of Railways should make serious efforts to clear all the backlog of coaches overdue for Periodical Overhaul (POH), which refers to scheduled major repairs.
  • The Ministry of Railways should make concerted efforts to further reduce the "ineffective percentage" of coaches (the percentage of coaches out of service).
  • Intermediate examination of passenger train coaches should be done after every six months to fully ensure that the passengers have complete safety and are provided with basic needs.
  • Vulnerable sections where cases of theft, pilferage, and vandalism occur frequently should be identified, and suitable measures, such as deployment of Railway Protection Force (RPF), in these areas/trains should be taken expeditiously.
  • More funds should be allocated during the Eighth Plan for Rolling Stock, both by generating additional resources from the Railways' own revenues and by increasing plan funds allotment, to speedily execute schemes for augmenting coach production, maintenance facilities, terminal, and line capacities.
  • The Committee would like to know the steps taken by the Ministry of Railways to generate additional resources from their own revenues by efficient utilization of existing assets (both human and material) and by cutting down all wasteful expenditure and pilferage in the Railways.
  • The Committee would like to know the number of wagons yet to be condemned on an age-cum-condition basis and whether the present stock would be sufficient to meet future demands, considering the rising trend in originating traffic.

What it means for citizens

The report indicates that Indian citizens may continue to experience overcrowding on trains because the number of coaches and Electric Multiple Units (EMUs) planned for acquisition is less than what is needed. There is also a noted shortfall in the capacity to produce new coaches.

For passengers, particularly those in rural areas, the report highlights that overaged coaches are still in use. While the Ministry claims these coaches meet safety standards, the committee recommends that they undergo an intermediate examination every six months to ensure complete safety and basic comforts, such as proper windows and electrical fittings.

Furthermore, the report acknowledges passenger dissatisfaction due to overcrowding, theft, pilferage, and vandalism. The committee has recommended identifying areas and trains where these issues are frequent and deploying the Railway Protection Force (RPF) to improve security. The recommendations ultimately aim to enhance passenger safety, improve basic facilities on trains, and reduce congestion by increasing the availability and effective maintenance of railway rolling stock.
