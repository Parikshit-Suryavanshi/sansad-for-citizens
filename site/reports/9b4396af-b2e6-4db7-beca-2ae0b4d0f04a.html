Parliamentary Committee Report on Procedures for Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme Funds for Natural Calamities

Report date: 2012-08-09

Committee: Committee on MPLADS

Lok Sabha: 15

What was discussed

The Committee on MPLADS (Members of Parliament Local Area Development Scheme) examined the procedures for providing MPLADS funds for natural calamities. The MPLADS Scheme, launched on December 23, 1993, aims to enable Members of Parliament (MPs) to recommend developmental works and create durable community assets in their constituencies.

Initially, the MPLADS guidelines did not include provisions for using funds for rehabilitation work after natural calamities. Following recommendations from the MPLADS Committees of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha after the Odisha Super Cyclone in 1999, the guidelines were amended. This amendment authorized MPs to recommend works outside their constituencies or states, up to Rs. 10 lakh per year, for rehabilitation measures in calamity-affected areas. Currently, two provisions exist: Lok Sabha MPs from non-affected areas of a state can recommend works up to Rs. 10 lakh per annum in affected areas of that state, and for a "calamity of severe nature" in any part of the country, an MP can recommend works up to Rs. 50 lakh for the affected district. The Government of India decides if a calamity is of "severe nature."

The Committee examined the subject due to the generous contributions by MPs from their MPLADS funds for rehabilitation works in areas devastated by various natural calamities, including the Gujarat earthquake in 2001, Tsunami in 2004, Kosi River floods in Bihar in 2008, Aila cyclone in West Bengal in 2009, and Sikkim earthquake in 2011. The Committee on MPLADS (2010-11) was briefed on the subject on May 10, 2011, and took oral evidence on July 22, 2011. The draft report was considered and adopted by the Committee on MPLADS (2011-12) at its sitting held on July 18, 2012.

What the report found

The report found that there are no specific criteria laid down in the Disaster Management Act, 2005, for deciding whether a calamity is of a "severe nature." The Ministry of Home Affairs adjudges such calamities on a case-by-case basis. Different Ministries are responsible for declaring different types of natural calamities. No specific timeline is prescribed for declaring a calamity as severe, though the Leh cloud burst in 2010 was declared within three days of receiving requests from the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha Secretariats.

The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation issues appeals for MPLADS contributions for calamities several days after a severe calamity declaration, with delays ranging from 19 to 24 days for major calamities like the Kosi floods, Leh cloud burst, and Sikkim earthquake. MPs' consent letters for these funds are sent to the Chairmen of the respective Parliamentary Committees, which are then forwarded to the Ministry for action, adding to delays. The Ministry's procedure for finalizing proposals for rehabilitation works involves consulting the Parliamentary Committees on MPLADS, which also contributes to time loss.

The report also noted significant delays in the utilization of MPLADS funds. As of August 11, 2011, Rs. 9.42 crore (including interest) contributed for the Gujarat Earthquake in 2001 remained unspent with the Government of Gujarat, with proposals for its utilization still awaited despite reminders. For Tsunami Relief Works from 2004, Rs. 5.21 crore was unspent as of December 31, 2011. The Ministry received no information from district authorities regarding the commencement or completion status of works for the Kosi floods in Bihar in 2008. For Cyclone AILA in West Bengal (May 25, 2009), 30 out of 81 sanctioned works in North 24 Parganas district and 56 out of 73 works in South 24 Parganas district were yet to be completed. For the Leh cloudburst (August 6, 2010), only three specific works were reported in progress, and the status of other authorized rehabilitation works was not known to the Ministry. Work proposals for the Sikkim earthquake (September 18, 2011) were still awaited from the State Government. The Ministry does not undertake concurrent monitoring specifically for calamity-affected areas but uses NABARD Consultancy Services (NABCONS) for sample-based physical monitoring of MPLADS works, including those in calamity-affected areas. The report does not specify whether any works related to natural calamities were monitored by NABCONS, their findings, or action taken.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry of Home Affairs should assess and declare sudden natural calamities (e.g., earthquakes, floods, landslides, tsunami) as "severe nature" within three days of their occurrence. For calamities that unfold over time (e.g., drought, pest attack), the concerned Ministry should assess and declare them expeditiously. Necessary amendments should be made in the Disaster Management Act, 2005.
  • The MPLADS guidelines should be amended to increase the fund limit for permissible works: up to Rs. 50 lakh for works outside an MP's constituency but within the same state (currently Rs. 10 lakh), and up to Rs. 1 crore for works in any part of the country affected by a "calamity of severe nature" (currently Rs. 50 lakh).
  • The Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation should issue appeals for MPLADS contributions within one week from the date the Ministry of Home Affairs declares a natural calamity as severe.
  • MPs' consent letters for MPLADS funds for calamity rehabilitation should be addressed directly to the Minister or Secretary of the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, instead of the Chairman of the Parliamentary Committee.
  • The Ministry, as the nodal executing agency, should examine and approve work proposals submitted by State Governments for calamity rehabilitation within two weeks, without referring them to Parliamentary Committees. The Ministry should also submit periodic progress reports of rehabilitation works to the Committee and to contributing MPs.
  • MPLADS guidelines should stipulate that rehabilitation works in calamity-affected areas be identified by the nodal department within one month of pooling funds and completed by the concerned District Authority within eight months.
  • The Ministry should periodically review the physical and financial progress of MPLADS funds for rehabilitation, and State-level monitoring Committees should specifically review these works. State Nodal Departments should submit monthly progress reports on rehabilitation works to the Ministry.
  • The Ministry should undertake quarterly Secretary-level reviews of the physical and financial progress of works in calamity-affected areas, using video conferencing with State Chief Secretaries/Secretaries and District Authorities.
  • Specific reference should be made to NABARD Consultancy Services (NABCONS) to monitor the physical and financial progress of rehabilitation works, and nodal department officers should be assigned responsibility for monitoring these funds.
  • The release of MPLADS funds to MPs' constituencies should not be linked to the submission of utilization and audit certificates for calamity relief works in other states.
  • For the Gujarat earthquake funds, the Ministry and the State Government should resolve land-related problems for 25 Anganwadis and finalize new sites for execution of works within the current financial year (2012-13). If MPLADS funds remain unutilized with State Governments beyond one year from the date of pooling, they should be transferred back to the respective constituencies of the contributing MPs. For Gujarat, a definite timeframe for utilization should be fixed, and if not utilized, funds should be withdrawn and returned to nodal districts.
  • For the Kosi floods in Bihar, the Ministry should send a team to inspect work progress. If works have not started, the Government of Bihar should be asked to transfer the funds immediately back to the Ministry for distribution to the constituencies of contributing MPs.
  • The Ministry should take expeditious steps to complete the remaining works for Cyclone AILA in West Bengal during the current year (2012-13).
  • The Ministry should vigorously pursue with State Authorities to ensure all works for the Leh cloudburst are completed within the current year (2012-13).
  • For the Sikkim earthquake, the Ministry should take up the matter at the Chief Secretary level and make all efforts to approve and execute the works within the current year (2012-13).

What it means for citizens

This report highlights that funds meant for helping people affected by natural disasters, which are contributed by MPs from their local development funds, are often delayed in reaching the beneficiaries. The existing procedures have led to significant amounts of money remaining unspent or to projects being incomplete years after calamities like the Gujarat earthquake and the Tsunami occurred.

For ordinary citizens living in disaster-prone areas, the report's findings mean that relief and rehabilitation efforts may be slow, and the creation of essential community assets meant to help them recover is often delayed. The recommendations aim to make the process of declaring calamities, allocating funds, approving projects, and monitoring their execution much faster and more efficient. If implemented, these changes would mean that aid reaches affected communities more quickly, and crucial rehabilitation works, such as building schools or shelters, are completed in a timely manner, directly benefiting the people who need them most.
