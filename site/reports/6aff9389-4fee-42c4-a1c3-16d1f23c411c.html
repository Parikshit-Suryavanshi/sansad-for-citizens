Committee Examines Measures for OBC Representation and Welfare in NTPC Ltd.

Report date: 2018-08-09

Committee: Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes

Lok Sabha: 16

What was discussed

The Committee on Welfare of Other Backward Classes examined 'Measures undertaken to secure representation of OBCs (Other Backward Classes) in employment and for their welfare in NTPC Ltd.' pertaining to the Ministry of Power. NTPC Ltd., incorporated in 1975, is India's largest power generator and a Maharatna Company of the Government of India. The Government of India holds a 62.27% stake in the company as of August 2017.

The Committee took evidence from representatives of the Ministry of Power and NTPC Ltd. on 2018-04-05. The report was considered and adopted by the Committee at a sitting held on 2018-08-07. The examination focused on the total staff strength, number of OBC employees at various levels, implementation of reservation policy, backlog vacancies, special recruitment drives, welfare measures, corporate social responsibility (CSR) activities, and outsourcing practices.

What the report found

As of 2017-12-31, NTPC Ltd. had a total of 21,828 employees, with 3,914 (17.9%) belonging to OBCs. In Group 'A' posts, the representation of OBCs was 18.1%, while the direct recruitment reservation for OBCs in these posts (through open competition) is 27%. The Ministry of Power stated that the reservation for OBCs came into existence on 2009-08-09, and the total manpower includes recruitments made before and after this date, which contributed to the overall lower percentage.

The Committee observed 12 backlog vacancies for OBCs in Group 'A' posts and 3 in Group 'D' posts in 2017. Reasons cited for non-filling of Group 'A' posts included non-materialization of offer of appointments and non-availability of suitable candidates (Medical Specialist). For Group 'C' and 'D' posts, reasons included non-availability of adequately qualified candidates, minimal representation of reserved categories among landoustees/local populace, and pending court cases for Group 'D'.

NTPC Ltd. maintains Roster Registers as per Department of Personnel & Training (DoPT) norms and follows DoPT instructions regarding the inclusion of OBC members in Selection Committees/Boards. Liaison Officers for OBCs have been appointed at Corporate Centre, Regional Headquarters, and Units/Projects, with 19 out of 43 such officers belonging to the OBC category. Grievances of employees, including OBCs, are handled through a common online system called "Madad," and no complaints were received from OBC employees during the last two years. There is no separate association for OBC employees in NTPC.

NTPC Ltd. undertakes CSR activities, spending 2% of the average net profit of the previous three years. Amounts spent were Rs. 205.18 crore in 2014-15, Rs. 491.80 crore in 2015-16, and Rs. 277.81 crore in 2016-17. No separate funds are allocated for the welfare of OBCs under CSR. Skill development programs are initiated under CSR, which benefit various sections of society, including OBCs. NTPC also outsources non-core jobs, ensuring that no workforce is discriminated against based on caste, creed, religion, gender, or beliefs. The report states that, except for some doctors and consultants, NTPC does not engage employees on a contract basis. Minimum wages and social security benefits like Provident Fund (PF) and Employees' State Insurance (ESI) are ensured for contract workers.

What was recommended

  • The Ministry of Power should take initiative to ensure representation of OBCs on the Board of Directors in all Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), including NTPC Ltd., for social inclusion.
  • The possibility of providing pre-recruitment training to OBC candidates should be explored.
  • Advertisements for vacancies should be widely publicized to ensure candidates from socially and educationally backward sections are aware of opportunities.
  • The Ministry should make efforts to settle pending court cases related to Group 'D' vacancies.
  • A special Recruitment Drive should be launched to fill all backlog vacancies in Group 'A' and Group 'D' under the OBC category on a priority basis.
  • Liaison Officers for OBCs at both Corporate Centre and Regional Offices should be nominated from the OBC category, as far as possible.
  • The Ministry should facilitate the formation of a separate OBC Employees’ Welfare Association in NTPC and extend requisite support.
  • NTPC management should hold periodical meetings with representatives of such an Association to address grievances promptly.
  • NTPC should focus its Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) activities on local areas, prioritizing the benefit of socially and educationally backward sections of society, in line with Section 135(5) of the Companies Act, 2013.
  • More people from the backward community should be included in skill development programs under CSR.
  • NTPC Ltd., as the Principal Employer, should find a way to give representation to the backward section people when engaging any workforce on an outsourced basis and issue necessary guidelines.
  • Payment of minimum wages and benefits of social security schemes (health, education, insurance, provident fund) should be ensured for employees engaged on an outsourced basis.

What it means for citizens

This report highlights concerns regarding the representation and welfare of OBC citizens in employment at NTPC Ltd. For OBC citizens seeking employment, the report points to a shortfall in their representation, particularly in Group 'A' posts, and identifies backlog vacancies that the Committee recommends should be filled through a special recruitment drive. It also suggests exploring pre-recruitment training and wider advertisement of job opportunities, which could provide better access to information and preparation for OBC candidates.

For OBC employees of NTPC, the report recommends appointing Liaison Officers from their own community and facilitating the formation of an OBC Employees’ Welfare Association to ensure their grievances and problems are effectively addressed. In terms of community welfare, the report emphasizes that NTPC’s Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) activities, including skill development programs, should prioritize local areas and specifically benefit socially and educationally backward sections, which includes OBCs. This could lead to more targeted support for education, health, and skill development in communities around NTPC power stations. For citizens working on an outsourced basis for NTPC, the report recommends measures to ensure their representation, payment of minimum wages, and access to social security benefits, aiming to protect their interests and improve working conditions.
