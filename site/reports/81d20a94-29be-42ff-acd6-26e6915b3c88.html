Railway Convention Committee Recommends Interim Dividend Rate and Fund Allocations for 1998-99

Report date: 1999-01-27

Committee: Railway Convention Committee

Lok Sabha: 12

What was discussed

The Railway Convention Committee examined the rate of dividend that the Railway Undertaking should pay to the General Revenues, which are the central government's funds, and other related financial matters for the financial year 1998-99. The Committee also considered arrangements for various railway funds, including the Depreciation Reserve Fund (used for replacement and renewal of assets), Pension Fund (used for pension payments), Development Fund (used for passenger and user amenities, labour welfare, unremunerative operating improvements, and safety works), and Capital Fund (used to finance capital works), as well as the interest rates on these funds.

The previous Railway Convention Committee (1996), in its Third Report, had recommended an interim dividend rate of 7% for the year 1997-98. This report was presented to the Lok Sabha on 1997-12-02 and laid in the Rajya Sabha on 1998-03-25. The Ninth Lok Sabha was dissolved on 1997-12-04, leading to the formation of the current Railway Convention Committee (1998) on 1998-07-22.

On 1998-11-18, the Ministry of Railways submitted an interim memorandum, including the Ministry of Finance's proposal for the Railways to pay a dividend at a rate of 7.5% on Capital-at-charge (Capital contributed by General Revenues) for the year 1998-99. The Ministry of Railways, however, presented arguments against increasing this rate. The Committee considered these proposals and adopted its report on 1999-01-27.

What the report found

The report found that the Railway Convention Committee (1996) had previously recommended a 7% dividend rate for 1997-98, which included contributions for grants to States in lieu of passenger fare tax and for financing safety works during 1996-97. The Ministry of Finance proposed increasing the dividend rate to 7.5% for 1998-99. They argued that the average government borrowing rate (12.20% for 1998-99 Budget Estimates) was significantly higher than the effective dividend rate paid by Railways (4.21% after excluding subsidy). They also noted that General Revenues incurred an additional expenditure of Rs. 357 crore because Railways paid only Rs. 23 crore for grants to States in lieu of passenger fare tax, while the Tenth Finance Commission recommended Rs. 380 crore.

In response, the Ministry of Railways (Railway Board) expressed concerns about the Railways' financial position, noting a shortfall of Rs. 618 crore in earnings up to September 1998. They highlighted declining capital from the General Exchequer and increasing reliance on market borrowing, leading to Rs. 2392 crore in lease charges. The Ministry of Railways also stressed that unresolved issues regarding compensation for social obligations, such as the public service obligation (amounting to Rs. 1800 crore in 1996-97), burdened their resources. The Committee noted that the Inter-Ministerial Working Group Report on Social Service Obligation of Indian Railways (May 1997), agreed upon by the Ministries of Railways and Finance and the Planning Commission, had not been implemented.

Regarding railway funds, the balance in the Depreciation Reserve Fund (DRF) at the end of 1997-98 was expected to be Rs. 1516 crore, with a proposed appropriation of Rs. 2473 crore for 1998-99. The Pension Fund balance at the end of 1997-98 was expected to be Rs. 765 crore, with a proposed appropriation of Rs. 2218 crore for 1998-99. For the Development Fund, Rs. 350 crore was appropriated in 1997-98, with Rs. 475 crore projected for 1998-99. The Capital Fund had Rs. 1120 crore credited in 1997-98 (Revised Estimates), with an appropriation of Rs. 1181 crore proposed for 1998-99, which, along with Rs. 74 crore in interest, was estimated to permit a withdrawal of Rs. 1255 crore for Plan needs.

What was recommended

  • As an interim measure for the year 1998-99, dividend to General Revenues may be paid at 7% on the entire Capital invested on Railways from the General Revenues, irrespective of the year of investment. This rate includes the amount that was payable by Railways to General Revenues for payment to States as grant in lieu of passenger fare tax and contribution for assisting States for financing safety works during the financial year 1997-98.
  • All other existing concessions on residential buildings, new lines, and subsidies from General Revenues may be allowed to continue on the existing basis for the year 1998-99.
  • The contribution to the Depreciation Reserve Fund (DRF) for the Budget Estimates of 1998-99 may be kept at Rs. 2473 crore, subject to minor adjustments as warranted by the Annual Plan for 1998-99 and the capacity of the system to generate internal resources.
  • The contribution to the Pension Fund should be kept at Rs. 2218 crore for the year 1998-99, subject to minor adjustment keeping in view the likely withdrawals anticipated for the year and the financial position of the Railways.
  • The Ministry of Railways should, in their action taken notes, apprise the Committee about the actual amount that will be credited to the Development Fund at the end of the current financial year.
  • The Ministry of Railways should apprise the Committee about the actual amount that would be credited to the Capital Fund at the end of the current financial year.
  • The balances in the various Railway Reserve Funds (other than Development Fund) may carry the same rate of interest as the rate of dividend. The rate of interest on the balance in Development Fund may be the same as the rate of interest on the loan from General Revenues for Development Fund Works for the purpose of Budget Estimates for 1998-99.
  • The Committee desires to be apprised about the precise action taken by the Government on the findings of the Inter-Ministerial Working Group Report on Social Service Obligation of Indian Railways (May 1997), and finds no reason for delay in its implementation.

What it means for citizens

This report outlines how Indian Railways manages its finances, specifically how it contributes to the central government's funds and allocates money for its own operations and future needs. For the financial year 1998-99, the report recommends that Indian Railways pay an interim dividend of 7% of the capital (money) invested in it by the central government. This payment includes amounts that go to states as grants in place of a passenger fare tax and to help states fund railway safety projects.

The report also details the recommended amounts for various railway funds. Money is set aside in the Depreciation Reserve Fund for replacing and renewing railway assets, in the Pension Fund for railway employees' pensions, in the Development Fund for passenger amenities, worker welfare, operating improvements, and safety works, and in the Capital Fund for capital projects. The Committee also emphasized the need for the government to take action on compensating Railways for social obligations, which are services provided for public benefit that may not be profitable.
